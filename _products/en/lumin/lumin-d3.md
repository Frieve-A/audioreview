## Overview

The Lumin D3 is a network streamer, DAC and digital preamplifier built around an ES9028PRO and a fully balanced analog stage with an X1-derived output buffer [1]. It provides 6Vrms XLR and 3Vrms RCA outputs, Leedh digital volume, Gigabit Ethernet, USB storage playback, and BNC S/PDIF output. Native and resampled playback extends to PCM 384kHz and DSD256. Supported services and protocols include UPnP OpenHome, Roon Ready, Qobuz Connect, TIDAL Connect, Spotify Connect, AirPlay 2, Audirvana, JPLAY, QPlay and gapless playback [1].

## Scientific Validity

$$ \Large \text{0.8} $$

Hi-Fi News measured 5.03Vrms maximum from the balanced output with a 10-ohm source impedance [2]. With 48kHz/24-bit native and upsampled signals, THD at 1kHz was 0.00005% at 0dBFS and 0.0008% at -30dBFS; THD+N at 20kHz was 0.00035% and 0.0018%, respectively. A-weighted S/N Ratio was 116.5dB with resampling off and 118.5dB with it on, while 20Hz-20kHz response was +0.0/-1.2dB. The report's distortion graph plots 1kHz native/upsampled traces in black/orange and 20kHz traces in cyan/blue from 0 to -120dBFS. Its jitter graph plots native 48kHz in red, 192kHz upsampling in black and DSD64 conversion in green: correlated sidebands fall from 65psec to 10psec with PCM upsampling, while DSD64 conversion measures 165psec with added high-frequency noise; native 48/96/192kHz jitter is 10/9/10psec. Distortion, noise, linearity and jitter are strong, while the larger high-frequency response deviation keeps the overall result below the highest tier.

## Technology Level

$$ \Large \text{0.5} $$

The D3 integrates an ES9028PRO converter, Leedh Processing digital volume control, an X1-derived analog buffer, a newer processor, and Lumin's network hardware, control software and streaming platform [1]. The product-level integration and analog implementation reflect genuine in-house engineering and accumulated network-audio experience. Its converter is commercially available and Leedh Processing is licensed rather than exclusive to Lumin, while the underlying approaches are mature and readily reproducible by established competitors. The result is competent, full-system engineering without a durable technical barrier, supporting an average technology assessment.

## Cost-Performance

$$ \Large \text{1.0} $$

The Lumin D3 is currently in stock at 3,000 USD [3]. The 859 USD Eversolo DMP-A6 Gen 2 is substantially cheaper and lists many overlapping playback and connectivity functions [4][5], but no independent measurements of that exact model were found. Measurements of the preceding DMP-A6 cannot be transferred to the Gen 2, so it cannot qualify as an equivalent-or-better measured comparator.

The 1,980 USD Eversolo DMP-A8 is independently measured [6][7][8], but it also fails the strict measured-performance comparison: its balanced output impedance is 101 ohms and maximum output is 4.36 Vrms, versus 10 ohms and 5.03 Vrms for the D3 [2][7][8]. The available evidence also does not establish every D3 service and playback function for the DMP-A8. No cheaper finished product or buildable combination was therefore confirmed to meet all of the D3's relevant functions while equaling or exceeding every comparable independently measured performance axis. The resulting cost-performance assessment is 1.0.

## Reliability & Support

$$ \Large \text{0.6} $$

Published warranty coverage varies by region. Lumin states a two-year worldwide warranty for the original purchaser with dated proof from an authorized dealer, while the official Japanese distributor lists one year for the D3 [9][11]. The manufacturer documents hundreds of trained dealers across 46 countries, regional distributors and a direct company contact route [9]. Firmware support is active: releases from 2024 through 2026 include v21.2 in February 2026 with D3-specific sample-rate changes, v22.0 in April and v23.0 on July 9 [10]. The networked player has normal electronic complexity, and no model-specific RMA, MTBF, recall or statistically useful failure data are published. The regional one-year term is balanced by the broad support infrastructure and sustained firmware record, supporting a moderately above-average assessment without inferring hardware reliability from brand reputation.

## Rationality of Design Philosophy

$$ \Large \text{0.6} $$

The D3 directs its hardware and software toward user-facing functions: network and USB-storage playback, broad service integration, digital volume control, and balanced and unbalanced analog connectivity [1]. Compared with the D2 generation, it adds a newer conversion platform, higher output capability, Leedh volume processing and higher-rate resampling, representing practical progression. A PC or smartphone plus suitable network software and a DAC can reproduce the core role, but the D3 provides it as a single managed appliance with a dedicated control ecosystem. Lumin's subjective marketing language receives no credit, and the high price does not correspond to a unique technical function, but the functional integration and model progression support a moderately positive assessment.

## Advice

The Lumin D3 delivers excellent measured distortion and noise performance validated by independent lab testing, making it a sound choice for buyers prioritizing verified electrical performance in a network-attached DAC/preamp with broad streaming-service and Roon Ready support. Its DAC chip and volume-control technology are not exclusive, but the cheaper alternatives examined could not be confirmed to match every relevant function and independently measured performance axis. Buyers should therefore compare service support and control workflow carefully, while treating lower-cost claims based only on manufacturer specifications or measurements of a related model as unproven.
