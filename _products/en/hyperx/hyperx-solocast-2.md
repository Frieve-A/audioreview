## Overview

HyperX SoloCast 2 is a cardioid USB desktop microphone. It combines a 14mm electret capsule, tap-to-mute control, internal suspension, foam pop treatment, a tilting weighted stand, and 3/8-inch and 5/8-inch mounting threads. It supports recording at up to 24-bit/96kHz and offers high-pass, low-pass, and voice-enhancement filters through HyperX NGENUITY [1][2][4][6]. The representative current US price is 44.99 USD [9].

## Scientific Validity

$$ \Large \text{0.5} $$

The manufacturer specifies a 20Hz–20kHz range without a tolerance, a cardioid pattern, and a maximum USB format of 24-bit/96kHz [2]. The US page gives sensitivity as −7.8±3 dB (1 V/Pa at 1kHz), while the Japanese page uses −7.8±3 dBFS with the same parenthetical reference [1][2]. Those are not interchangeable units, and the documents do not reconcile them. The A-weighted RMS noise is also expressed relative to digital full scale (≤−79.5 dBFS on the US page and <−79.5 dBFS on the Japanese page), so it cannot be converted into a standard microphone S/N ratio or equivalent input-noise level from the published information.

Maximum SPL, standard S/N, equivalent noise, distortion, a tolerance-bounded frequency-response graph, and calibrated polar attenuation are not published. The available third-party use test supplies recordings and software observations rather than calibrated acoustic measurements [6]. Scientific performance therefore cannot be rated as either good or problematic from the available data.

## Technology Level

$$ \Large \text{0.5} $$

HyperX describes SoloCast 2 as a design developed from the ground up, combining its internal suspension, tilting base, and NGENUITY control [1][2]. This supports product-level in-house design, without establishing ownership of every component. The capsule, USB conversion, capacitive mute, passive vibration treatment, and high-pass/low-pass processing are established technologies. Their combination is appropriate for a compact recording device, but no model-specific patent or advanced computing implementation is documented. The available evidence supports competent integration rather than a technical breakthrough; it does not establish how easily a competitor could reproduce this particular implementation.

## Cost-Performance

$$ \Large \text{1.0} $$

SoloCast 2 costs 44.99 USD at Target [9]. The comparison includes cardioid USB recording at up to 24-bit/96kHz, tap mute, tabletop positioning, vibration and pop treatment, standard arm mounting, and adjustable high-pass, low-pass, and voice filters. HyperX explicitly says the settings are saved to the microphone for use after changing devices; a Windows PC is required to configure them [1][2][4]. These are manufacturer-stated functions, not independently verified acoustic results.

UGREEN CM581 is listed at 39.99 USD before any applicable delivery charge [10]. Its manufacturer materials specify cardioid capture, 24-bit/96kHz, a 20Hz–20kHz range, mute, a stand, pop and vibration treatment, and PS4/PS5 support [7][8]. Free Equalizer APO can add high-pass, low-pass, and EQ processing on a Windows capture device [11][12], but that processing remains on the PC. It does not supply the saved filtering when the microphone moves to another host. CM581's ANS bypass and standard-thread compatibility are also unconfirmed. A longer cable or mounting accessory would not close the processing gap.

PreSonus Revelator provides onboard processing and stored presets, but its published compatibility does not establish PS5 support. A separate processor such as Revelator io44 accepts analog microphone inputs, not a direct USB microphone connection; it therefore cannot simply add portable filtering to CM581 [13][14]. Neither route establishes a cheaper complete substitute. Comparable calibrated response, noise, and distortion measurements are unavailable, and CM581's claimed 70 dB S/N cannot be equated to SoloCast 2's digital noise figure [1][7]. On the available catalog evidence, SoloCast 2 itself is the cheapest complete option identified, giving a provisional CP of 1.0. This is not a finding of superior sound quality.

## Reliability & Support

$$ \Large \text{0.6} $$

HyperX provides a two-year warranty for standalone microphones. Its warranty can be serviced internationally where HyperX or an authorized service provider supports the same model, subject to regional availability and terms [5]. The current NGENUITY page shows active software maintenance, but it does not establish a regular SoloCast 2 firmware schedule [4]. The integrated suspension and limited physical controls do not by themselves prove exceptional durability. No model-level failure-rate, RMA, repair-price, spare-parts-duration, or long-term reliability data were identified. The global manufacturer support structure is a positive, while the remaining evidence is average or unknown.

## Rationality of Design Philosophy

$$ \Large \text{0.7} $$

The visible product features direct spending toward capture, mute control, positioning, and ordinary mounting. Internal suspension and foam address desk vibration and plosives through physically plausible mechanisms, while software-configured high-pass, low-pass, and voice-shaping filters offer useful control without requiring a separate processor [1][2]. These are rational uses of mechanical treatment and digital processing. Integrating them can simplify a desktop setup, although it does not guarantee that every room or placement can omit a boom arm or additional acoustic treatment. The design gives a dedicated microphone a clear practical role in desktop voice recording.

## Advice

SoloCast 2 suits buyers who want a compact desktop microphone, standard arm threads, and configurable filtering in one package. Its quick-start guide lists PC, Chromebook, and PS5, while NGENUITY configuration requires Windows [3][4]. HyperX advertises saved settings across connected devices [1][2]. A December 2025 beta-software test reported persistence failures, but it does not establish the behavior of the current release; buyers relying on this feature should check operation in their setup [4][6]. CM581 plus Equalizer APO has a lower listed price before delivery costs for Windows recording if portable filtering is unnecessary, but it is not a complete substitute. For quiet or loud-source recording where noise or overload limits matter, the missing calibrated measurements remain a limitation.
