## Overview

The LEKATO SF2401 is a 2-in/2-out USB audio interface priced at 83 USD, targeting home studio recording, content creation, and podcasting [1]. LEKATO markets the device with dual XLR/TRS combo inputs, 48V phantom power, HI-Z mode, switchable direct monitoring, and USB-C bus power with plug-and-play support on macOS, iOS, and Android.

## Scientific Validity

$$ \Large \text{0.5} $$

The hardware OEM manufacturer Simplefly Technology publishes performance specifications for the SF2401 unit [3]: THD+N of 0.0012% (mic input) and 0.002% (instrument/line input), dynamic range of 104-111 dBA across channels, and frequency response of 20 Hz-20 kHz (±0.1 dB). These figures would indicate competitive performance if accurate. However, LEKATO publishes no official audio performance specifications, and no independent third-party measurements exist to verify these OEM-claimed values. Given the absence of both brand-level specifications and independent verification, reliable assessment is not possible, and Scientific Validity is set to 0.5.

## Technology Level

$$ \Large \text{0.2} $$

The same SF2401 model is listed by Shenzhen Simplefly Technology Co., Ltd. for OEM/ODM supply and sold under the LEKATO brand without identified proprietary in-house engineering [1][3]. All core features - 24-bit/192kHz conversion, USB Audio Class, 48V phantom power, HI-Z input switching, and direct monitoring - are mature, widely available implementations with no technical differentiation. The "AIR mode" is described as an input-stage frequency-response adjustment for high-frequency boost, a simple function with no substantive technical contribution [1]. No patents, proprietary circuit designs, or novel engineering were identified. The supplier advertises OEM/ODM orders, so the hardware does not present a meaningful entry barrier [3]. Given OEM sourcing serving lineup expansion rather than competitive differentiation, no durable competitive advantage, and an AIR mode that offers no substantive technical value, the technology level rates 0.2.

## Cost-Performance

$$ \Large \text{0.7} $$

The SF2401 is priced at 83 USD [1]. No LEKATO-published audio performance specifications or independent third-party measurements exist for the SF2401; cost-performance comparison is therefore based on functional equivalence only and is treated as provisional.

The Behringer U-Phoria UMC202HD [4][5] at 58 USD provides equivalent core user-facing functions: 2x combo XLR/1/4" TRS inputs (both mic-capable), 48V phantom power, HI-Z instrument support, balanced 1/4" TRS stereo monitor outputs, 1/4" headphone output, direct monitoring, 24-bit/192kHz capability, and USB bus power. iOS/Android is not officially marketed for the UMC202HD but is accessible via standard USB adapters (class-compliant device).

CP = 58 USD ÷ 83 USD = 0.699 → 0.7 (provisional: functional and price comparison only because LEKATO-specific independent measurements are unavailable)

## Reliability & Support

$$ \Large \text{0.6} $$

The SF2401 carries a 12-month warranty [2]. The product's construction - a metal-case enclosure, solid-state electronics, USB bus power, and no moving parts beyond knobs and switches - is inherently resistant to degradation and failure. LEKATO publishes a web-based warranty process with repair or replacement for qualifying defects and requires photos or videos for claims after the 30-day return window [2]. No hardware failures, recalls, or product-specific service bulletins have been documented for the SF2401. Windows driver files are distributed from the product page without a versioned changelog, which is suboptimal documentation practice [1]. The short warranty and weak driver documentation prevent a higher support score, while the simple solid-state construction and documented warranty process keep the score above neutral.

## Rationality of Design Philosophy

$$ \Large \text{0.5} $$

LEKATO's approach with the SF2401 is OEM resale: Simplefly Technology lists the hardware under the same model number, and LEKATO applies its own branding [1][3]. Cost allocation is directed toward functional hardware - XLR inputs, phantom power, AD/DA conversion - with no allocation to premium enclosures, exotic materials, or non-functional aesthetics. The product is solid-state digital throughout, the appropriate technical choice for this application. On the opposing side, LEKATO performs no identified original engineering, contributes no novel solutions to audio interface design, and relies on borrowed model identity rather than any genuine technical identity. These two factors - cost allocation aligned with functional output, and an entirely conservative approach without any engineering contribution - result in a neutral score.

## Advice

The LEKATO SF2401 delivers the functional essentials of a 2-in/2-out USB audio interface - dual XLR combo inputs, 48V phantom power, HI-Z switching, balanced 1/4" monitor outputs, and USB-C connectivity - at 83 USD. No independently verified LEKATO audio performance data exists; buyers requiring confirmed performance should prioritize products with documented third-party measurement data. The 12-month warranty is limited. The shared volume knob controlling both headphone and monitor outputs simultaneously is a practical limitation for setups requiring independent level control of each. The Behringer U-Phoria UMC202HD is available at 58 USD with equivalent core functionality.
