## Overview

The F.Audio T3D is sold by a Bangladesh retailer as a compact touchscreen digital audio player [1]. The listing describes dual devices marked "ES9318C," PCM up to 32-bit/768kHz and native DSD256 decoding, 3.5mm single-ended plus 2.5mm and 4.4mm balanced outputs, and bidirectional Bluetooth with aptX HD/AAC/SBC/aptX codecs listed collectively. A 1500mAh battery is rated for approximately 12 hours when playing a 16-bit/44.1kHz file. The seller also describes microSD playback and USB-C PC decoding for Windows, Android, and macOS, using the ambiguous phrase "mimics the XMOS interface." All four product-gallery images were inspected: they confirm the three output formats, microSD/USB-C edges, touchscreen, and physical controls, but contain no measurement graph [1]. The live price is 5,500 BDT with stock shown; converted at the 2026-07-16 Bangladesh Bank weighted-average USD rate, it is 44.58 USD [1][2].

## Scientific Validity

$$ \Large \text{0.5} $$

An exact-model search found no credible third-party bench measurements of output power, frequency response, THD/THD+N, S/N, dynamic range, crosstalk, or jitter. The retailer-published decoding formats and feature claims do not establish analog-output fidelity, so the audio performance remains unverified rather than demonstrated to be either poor or transparent.

## Technology Level

$$ \Large \text{0.3} $$

The retailer description combines a touchscreen controller, dual DAC devices identified as "ES9318C," local-storage playback, USB-C PC decoding, and bidirectional Bluetooth. No technical paper, patent, circuit documentation, or manufacturer statement establishes proprietary design ownership. Searches of ESS Technology's official site for both ES9318C and ES9318 returned no product documentation [3], but absence from the public site is not proof that the installed device is counterfeit; the seller's exact component identification remains unverified.

This is an appropriate digital/circuit/software combination for a current low-cost player, but the touchscreen dual-DAC DAP format and its codec set are mature, with no documented barrier to replication. The published "PLL clock synchronization" claim has no accompanying jitter result [1]. The integration is functional, but there is no documented proprietary or difficult-to-copy achievement.

## Cost-Performance

$$ \Large \text{1.0} $$

The target's live price is 5,500 BDT, or 44.58 USD at the Bangladesh Bank's 2026-07-16 weighted-average USD/BDT rate [1][2]. Function-only searches started with low-cost touchscreen local players and required microSD playback, USB decoding, 2.5mm and 4.4mm balanced compatibility, bidirectional Bluetooth, and at least the advertised 12-hour battery life. No complete product below the target price was found.

The closest mainstream checkpoint found was the HiBy R3 II 2025 at its regular 179 USD price [5]. It exceeds the T3D claim on battery life (up to 19 hours single-ended and 16 hours balanced), provides touchscreen microSD playback, USB DAC and digital-transport modes, bidirectional Bluetooth, 4.4mm output, and DSD256. Its manufacturer table also gives THD of 0.0007%/0.0005%, dynamic range of 120/122dB, and S/N of 119/120dB, albeit without test conditions [5]. PCM tops out at 384kHz rather than the T3D seller's 768kHz, but both rates are beyond audibility and that ceiling is not a meaningful disadvantage. The R3 II lacks native 2.5mm output and does not document aptX HD; its protocol table lists aptX as send-only. It is therefore not a complete equal-or-better replacement, and its price is already about four times the target before any adapter or Bluetooth accessory.

HIFI WALKER H20 Pro was another higher-priced checkpoint, at a regular 39,800円 (245.45 USD at the 2026-07-16 Bank of Japan central USD/JPY rate) [2][4]. It covers most of the claimed functions and publishes THD below 0.005% and S/N of at least 118dB, but its approximately 10-hour battery rating is below the T3D seller's 12-hour claim and it also lacks 2.5mm output [4]. Lower-priced players located in the search missed at least the 4.4mm output or another required function. No cheaper equivalent-or-better configuration was confirmed, so CP = 1.0.

## Rationality of Design Philosophy

$$ \Large \text{0.7} $$

F.Audio publishes no design-philosophy statement, but the product's direction is largely rational at the stated low price. A touchscreen, microSD playback, USB DAC mode, bidirectional Bluetooth, three headphone-output formats, and a rechargeable battery are integrated into one portable device. These functions directly serve offline listening, phone-independent playback, and wired/wireless source use; they give a dedicated player a clear practical role even though the same basic listening task can also be assembled from a smartphone and dongle.

Using mature, widely available functions is a sensible cost-control choice rather than a flaw by itself. The stated low price and dense functional integration make the product direction clearly cost-focused. The weaknesses are evidentiary: the "PLL clock synchronization" claim lacks jitter data, and no objective design targets are published [1]. The result is functionally well integrated, but conservative rather than innovative.

## Advice

The T3D is attractive mainly as a low-cost, compact combination of local playback, PC decoding, three headphone-output formats, and bidirectional Bluetooth. Its audio performance remains unverified by credible bench measurements, and the exact "ES9318C" component identification is not confirmed by public ESS documentation. Buyers should verify the seller's USB decoding behavior, Bluetooth direction/codec combinations, return terms, and warranty for the exact unit, because no manual, manufacturer support, or firmware route was identified. Users who value documented output performance and long-term support more than the unusually low entry price should prefer a better-supported player even if it costs more.
