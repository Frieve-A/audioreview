## Overview

The iriver E100 is a 2008 flash-memory portable multimedia player. Its official support material covers MP3, WMA, and OGG audio, photo, video, and text viewing, an FM tuner with recording, built-in/external microphone and stereo line-input recording, stereo speakers, custom EQ/SRS processing, and microSD expansion [1][2]. The later compatibility list includes tested SanDisk 8GB cards, superseding the manual's 4GB limit; it does not guarantee every card [12]. The 2.4-inch player is rated for 18mW per channel into 16 ohms, and the Japanese manual quotes about 18 hours of audio playback under its stated 128kbps MP3, volume 20, normal-EQ, screen-off conditions [2]. Line-input recording can split files at silence, but FM timer recording is not supported [2][13]. It is discontinued, although limited unused 8GB stock remains [14].

## Scientific Validity

$$ \Large \text{0.5} $$

iXBT tested the E100 with RightMark Audio Analyzer 6.0.5 at 16-bit/44kHz through an external line-out-to-line-in loopback [3]. The response was within +0.34/-0.34dB from 40Hz to 15kHz, but widened to -2.50/+0.66dB from 20Hz to 20kHz; the graph locates the sharp roll-off near 20kHz. It measured a -88.2dBA noise level, 88.4dBA dynamic range, 0.0062% THD, -64.2dBA THD+noise, and 0.064% IMD+noise. The -84.3dB summary crosstalk figure conceals a weaker right-channel result of -67dB at 10kHz [3].

An independent 3DNews test of a 2GB unit, using an E-MU 0202 USB interface, RMAA 6.2.2, and 16-bit/44kHz with EQ/effects off, found 91dBA dynamic range, 0.003% THD, -64.8dBA THD+noise, 0.068% IMD+noise, and -88.1dB crosstalk. Its full-band response was -2.76/+0.29dB [16]. Together these show good midband flatness and low harmonic distortion, but more limited noise, dynamic range, and intermodulation performance. iXBT leaves the receiving interface, load, absolute level, and unit capacity unspecified; neither report establishes performance under a stated headphone load or specifically verifies the currently offered 8GB stock. The manual's 90dB S/N and 18mW+18mW into 16 ohms remain manufacturer specifications. Speaker and recording-input fidelity are not independently characterized [2][3][16].

## Technology Level

$$ \Large \text{0.4} $$

Local flash playback, microSD expansion, licensed SRS DSP, codec decoding, a small display, FM reception, recording, and built-in speakers form a useful package of mature technologies [1][2]. No model-specific patent, unusual circuit implementation, or documented in-house engineering contribution was found that would establish an advanced implementation today. Its integration remains useful, but the available engineering documentation does not establish the effort a new entrant would need to reproduce the complete design.

## Cost-Performance

$$ \Large \text{1.0} $$

The price basis is the 8GB version: an active US retail listing offers unused old stock for 389.99 USD, before tax [14]. This is a limited secondary-market supply, not a manufacturer-current model; optional coupons are excluded. The Japanese import offer adds 78.50 USD shipping, before any import charges, so regional purchase costs differ [14][15].

The lower-cost single-player candidate is a MECHEN listing at 66.99 USD plus 5.99 USD shipping, with video, photos, text, speaker, FM/voice/line recording, and removable storage [4]. Its exact model, external-microphone support, measured output quality, and continuous runtime are not sufficiently documented to establish equivalence.

A more capable functional alternative combines a 49.88 USD carrier-locked Moto g play 2026 for local media and stereo speakers, a Tecsun M-601 at 39.99 USD for actual FM reception/recording, a Qudelix-5K at 109 USD plus mandatory 20 USD shipping for USB headphone playback/EQ, and a TASCAM DR-05XP at 109 USD for external microphone/line recording with silence-triggered new files [5][6][7][8][17][18][22]. The hardware subtotal is 327.87 USD, with microSD cards for the two recorders, two AA cells, and any additional delivery/import charges still required. Phone service is not included; this is an offline-media configuration. External-source cables and microphones are separate accessories for both this configuration and the E100.

The Qudelix measurements include 111–112dB dynamic range and -94.1/-98.4dB unloaded 1kHz crosstalk, but its loaded crosstalk is materially different, and the 2020 ASR measurements precede a documented production change [19][20][21]. They cannot establish every current-unit performance axis under conditions matching the E100. Neither the component battery claims nor adding a power bank establishes the assembled system's continuous playback time. Thus this configuration is a concrete partial substitute, not a demonstrated equal-or-better replacement. With no cheaper complete equivalent established after these combinations, the E100 itself is treated as the world's cheapest qualifying option and receives 1.0. This does not imply that the scarce-stock asking price is attractive for ordinary music playback.

## Reliability & Support

$$ \Large \text{0.2} $$

The Japanese manufacturer's standard warranty rule provides one year for the product and 90 days for accessories, excludes battery wear, and is valid only in Japan [9]. It does not establish coverage for imported old stock today. The official E100 support page, manual, FAQs, and V1.16 firmware information remain online [1][2][11], but download availability is not ongoing hardware support. A notice dated March 15, 2013 lists E100 repair and replacement of the main board, exterior, battery, and accessories as already ended because parts could no longer be secured [10]. Current repairs therefore depend on options outside that manufacturer service. The display, controls, connectors, and sealed rechargeable battery also prevent solid-state storage alone from establishing exceptional durability. No statistical failure-rate evidence was found.

## Rationality of Design Philosophy

$$ \Large \text{0.5} $$

The E100 combines local music, video, photos, text, FM, voice and external-source recording, stereo speakers, and removable storage in one pocket device. Codec decoding, custom EQ/SRS processing, UMS/MTP transfer, and multiple recording paths give it a practical purpose [1][2]. These established implementations do not demonstrate advanced integration or a cutting-edge development approach by current standards. The available material also does not establish a measurement-led development program or unusual cost optimization, while providing no evidence of irrational spending on inaudible components. The design direction is practical but unexceptional.

## Advice

The E100 has independent loopback measurements, but they do not fully characterize loaded headphone output, speakers, or recording inputs. 3DNews measured 15 hours 24 minutes of mixed 128–320kbps MP3 playback, under conditions different from the manufacturer's 18-hour claim [2][16]. Neither figure predicts the endurance of an aged battery, even in unused stock. Manufacturer repair and parts support have ended. Check battery endurance, display and buttons, headphone output, FM and external recording, microSD access, and USB transfer before purchase. Lower-cost players and phone-based sets cover many uses; the E100's narrow combination of recording functions matters more than its scarcity for deciding whether its asking price makes sense.
