## Overview

The SAMSON SR950 is a passive, closed-back circumaural headphone intended for studio monitoring. Samson documents 50mm dynamic drivers, 32Ω impedance, 96dB sensitivity, a self-adjusting headband, velour ear cushions, and a fixed 2.5m cable terminated in a 3.5mm plug with a 6.35mm adapter[1][2]. The product remains listed by Samson and US retailers[1][3].

## Scientific Validity

$$ \Large \text{0.5} $$

The official manual specifies a 10Hz–25kHz frequency range and includes a frequency-response graph, but it does not disclose the measurement fixture, reference target, smoothing, level, or other test conditions[2]. The plotted response visibly departs from a straight line, but the graph cannot be converted into a defensible Harman-target deviation without those conditions. The manufacturer does not publish THD, S/N ratio, or passive-isolation results, and no independent SR950 measurement set covering the required headphone metrics was located. Scientific Validity therefore cannot be evaluated because the available evidence is insufficient.

## Technology Level

$$ \Large \text{0.2} $$

The documented design combines a conventional 50mm neodymium dynamic driver with a passive closed-back circumaural enclosure, self-adjusting headband, velour cushions, and a fixed analog cable[1][2]. These are mature and readily reproducible headphone technologies. The official materials document no distinctive transducer, acoustic-control, digital, or DSP technology that would establish a meaningful technical advance over established passive studio headphones. The technology level is consequently low, while recognizing that the product is a complete and functional implementation of a conventional architecture.

## Cost-Performance

$$ \Large \text{0.8} $$

This site evaluates headphones solely by functions and measured performance, without assigning value to driver type or configuration. The SR950's regular US price is 69.99 USD; a temporary lower offer is excluded from the standard-price comparison[3]. The Audio-Technica ATH-M20x provides the same user-facing role as a wired analog over-ear monitoring headphone, without requiring a battery, app, wireless connection, or DSP, and is currently in stock at 59.00 USD[4][6]. Sonarworks' single-pair measurement graphs show bass roll-off below about 60Hz and mid/treble deviations in the perceived acoustic frequency response; its 80dB harmonic-distortion graph is mostly below 0.5% above about 100Hz except for a narrow rise near 5kHz, while distortion is higher in the low bass[5]. The SR950 has no comparable independent data, and its manufacturer graph lacks the conditions needed for numerical target-error comparison. The comparison is therefore provisional; on the available evidence, no measured SR950 performance advantage offsets the ATH-M20x's lower price, so the ATH-M20x is used as the provisional equivalent-or-better benchmark.

CP = 59.00 USD ÷ 69.99 USD = 0.843 → 0.8

## Reliability & Support

$$ \Large \text{0.6} $$

The passive design has no battery, firmware, wireless radio, or digital electronics, limiting the number of failure-prone subsystems. Samson provides direct support links, manuals, product registration, and service-center information through its official site[1]. Its official warranty page states that Samson Audio products purchased in the United States carry a two-year warranty with proof of purchase; coverage outside the United States is handled by the local distributor[7]. The manual specifies a fixed 2.5m cable rather than a detachable user-replaceable cable[2]. No statistical RMA rate, MTBF data, or documented expedited repair program was located. The simple core construction supports an above-baseline assessment, while the fixed cable and limited published support metrics constrain it.

## Rationality of Design Philosophy

$$ \Large \text{0.5} $$

The passive architecture, circumaural fit, long cable, and included 6.35mm adapter form a coherent, low-cost design for studio tracking and monitoring[1][2]. The fixed cable is consistent with cost containment but reduces serviceability. Costs appear concentrated on the basic audio function, with no ornamental excess or pseudoscientific mechanism documented. Conversely, the concept is conservative and introduces no innovative mechanism for improving function or measured performance. These offsetting considerations support a neutral assessment of the design philosophy.

## Advice

The SR950 is a plausible low-cost choice for buyers who need a passive closed-back headphone with a long fixed cable and velour cushions. Its tonal accuracy, distortion, and isolation should not be treated as independently verified: the supplied response graph lacks test conditions, and the other required measurements are unavailable. Buyers prioritizing measurement transparency should also consider the less expensive Audio-Technica ATH-M20x, for which independent frequency-response and distortion graphs are available[5][6]. The SR950's fixed cable should also be considered if easy field replacement is important.
