## Overview

The XENNS Mangird Top Pro is a 499 USD wired in-ear monitor combining two dynamic drivers with eight balanced armature drivers (Knowles and Sonion units) across a 4-way passive crossover, released in 2025. It ships with a detachable 196-core copper/silver hybrid cable featuring interchangeable 3.5mm single-ended and 4.4mm balanced plugs [1].

## Scientific Validity

$$ \Large \text{0.5} $$

Bloom Audio's IEC-711 clone coupler graph provides usable raw frequency-response evidence [2]. Visual readings show approximately 66dB at 20–30Hz, 61dB at 100Hz, 59dB across 200–400Hz, a trough near 57dB at 800Hz, a rise to 65dB at 2.5–3kHz, 63dB at 4kHz, a fall to 57dB at 6kHz, and a local peak near 62dB at 8.5kHz. Relative to 1kHz, the low bass and 2.5–3kHz region are each elevated by roughly 8–9dB. The broad bass shelf and pinna-region rise are consistent with a target-shaped IEM response, while the roughly 5dB fall and recovery from 6kHz to 8.5kHz show treble irregularity. The plot has no target trace or separate left/right traces, and the source gives no insertion depth, drive level, smoothing, calibration, or sample count; it also warns that the coupler is unreliable above 10kHz [2]. Target-error deviation and channel matching therefore cannot be calculated. The manufacturer specifies 20Hz–33kHz, 16-ohm impedance, and 103dB±1dB sensitivity, but gives no response tolerance, sensitivity test condition, THD, or isolation result [1]. The favorable broad response shape, visible treble unevenness, and unmeasured distortion and isolation together support a mid-level Scientific Validity result rather than a high one.

## Technology Level

$$ \Large \text{0.3} $$

The Top Pro combines two dynamic drivers using standard NdFeB magnets with eight balanced armature units (Knowles and Sonion) through a 4-way passive crossover—established, industry-wide approaches. The cited product page describes revised magnetic circuits and hand-picked drivers but identifies no proprietary patented technology [1]. Integrating ten acoustic drivers through a four-way crossover demonstrates implementation know-how. However, the disclosed core technologies are mature and widely reproducible, with no demonstrated cutting-edge element or durable competitive advantage, and the fully analog, passive design has no digital or software integration. This combination of implementation skill and limited originality supports a below-average result.

## Cost-Performance

$$ \Large \text{0.1} $$

This site evaluates based solely on functionality and measured performance values, without considering driver types or configurations.

Current market price: 499 USD [1].

CP = 41.98 USD / 499 USD = 0.084 (rounded 0.1)

Comparator: Truthear GATE, 21.99 USD at its official store [4], bundled with the 19.99 USD Tripowin Zoe 0.78mm 2-pin to 4.4mm cable [6], for a total price of 41.98 USD. GATE's included 3.5mm cable and the added 4.4mm cable together match the review target's two analog termination options. Both products are passive wired IEMs with detachable 0.78mm 2-pin cables and included eartips.

The review target's raw graph is broadly target-shaped but has visible treble unevenness and no target-error or distortion result [2]. Audio Science Review measured GATE within approximately -2dB to +1.5dB of its target over most of 20Hz–10kHz, excluding the less-reliable 6–10kHz coupler region, while THD at 94dB SPL remained generally below 0.1% [5]. GATE therefore provides equal-or-better documented frequency-response performance and confirmed low distortion on the available axes; the comparison remains provisional because equivalent target-error and distortion values are unavailable for the Top Pro.

## Reliability & Support

$$ \Large \text{0.3} $$

Warranty coverage is limited: 1 year on the IEM shells and 3 months on the included cable [1], both below typical terms for this category. The cited warranty route is administered by Linsoul as retailer/distributor, and no separate XENNS Mangird-run repair or service infrastructure is documented [1]. No published statistical failure-rate data, such as an RMA ratio or MTBF, was identified for this model. One third-party report describes the stock cable's chin slider as loose and prone to sliding during use [3], an accessory-level issue rather than a defect in the IEM shells or drivers themselves.

## Rationality of Design Philosophy

$$ \Large \text{0.2} $$

The manufacturer's design language for the Top Pro emphasizes subjective tonal descriptions—musicality, decay-time tuning across frequency bands, and an "organic" signature [1]. The ten-driver configuration carries no controlled evidence of an audible benefit over simpler configurations, while the "196-core" copper/silver hybrid cable, "hand-picked drivers," and "magnetic circuit revisions" are presented as sound-quality improvements without independent verification. The subjective-first direction, unverified audibility claims, and investment in component complexity without demonstrated measured benefit make the design philosophy poorly rationalized.

## Advice

Buyers should treat the Top Pro's ten-driver configuration and cable marketing as unverified by controlled evidence. Its third-party graph documents the raw frequency-response shape, but the missing target trace, channel-separated traces, and distortion measurement prevent a conclusive fidelity assessment. The 41.98 USD Truthear GATE and Tripowin Zoe cable bundle provides both 3.5mm and 4.4mm connectivity and independently documented measurement performance at a small fraction of the price. Buyers who still choose the Top Pro should note its below-typical warranty (1 year shells, 3 months cable) and the reported stock cable chin-slider issue.
