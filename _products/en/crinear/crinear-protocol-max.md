## Overview

crinear protocol max is a portable USB DAC-amplifier from CrinEar, Crinacle's own brand. It combines USB-C input, 4.4 mm balanced and 3.5 mm single-ended headphone outputs, Eco/Boost operation, and device-stored 10-band PEQ configured through a browser workflow. The official price is 89.99 USD, and the product is positioned as a compact source device rather than an earphone or headphone [1][2].

## Scientific Validity

$$ \Large \text{0.7} $$

Manufacturer specifications state THD+N of 0.000126% balanced and 0.000158% single-ended, converted from -118 dB and -116 dB at 1 kHz 0 dBFS. S/N Ratio is 132 dB balanced and 127 dB single-ended, while Crosstalk is -116 dB balanced and -65 dB single-ended at 1 kHz -6 dBFS [1][2]. These published values indicate excellent distortion and noise performance, with excellent balanced Crosstalk and good single-ended Crosstalk. No independent third-party measurements are available for verification, so the evaluation is moderated despite the strong manufacturer figures.

## Technology Level

$$ \Large \text{0.5} $$

The technical package is competent but mostly conventional for a current portable USB DAC-amplifier. The strongest user-facing element is persistent 10-band PEQ stored on the device, which makes the EQ profile usable across compatible playback devices after setup [2]. The rest of the implementation uses established DAC and amplifier components, common balanced and single-ended output connectivity, and standard Eco/Boost operating modes. No CrinEar-specific patent, disclosed in-house circuit origin, or durable technical barrier was documented, so the technology level is average rather than advanced.

## Reliability & Support

$$ \Large \text{0.5} $$

Support is usable but not exceptional. CrinEar states that warranties are handled through official partners, and its partner list covers multiple regions [3]. Product-specific retailer evidence documents one-year coverage for the AMP & DAC while excluding included accessories [4]. No published failure-rate data, long parts-support commitment, recall notice, or regular firmware update history was documented in the checked sources, so reliability and support remain average rather than strong.

## Rationality of Design Philosophy

$$ \Large \text{0.8} $$

The design direction is rational: the product emphasizes measurable THD+N, S/N Ratio, and Crosstalk values, practical dual headphone outputs, persistent PEQ, and compact USB-C operation [1][2]. These choices point toward directly usable functions and measurable performance rather than visual luxury or unsupported sonic claims. The score is limited because PEQ-capable dongle DAC-amplifiers were already present by the release period, and the published performance claims still lack independent verification.

## Advice

crinear protocol max is most relevant if you specifically need a compact USB DAC-amplifier with both 4.4 mm and 3.5 mm outputs plus persistent 10-band PEQ at 89.99 USD. The manufacturer THD+N, S/N Ratio, and Crosstalk figures are strong, but independent measurement data is not yet available; buyers who rely on third-party bench data should treat that as the main limitation. If persistent PEQ is not required, other portable DAC-amplifiers can be considered, but they were not confirmed as lower-priced equivalents to this complete function-and-specification set.
