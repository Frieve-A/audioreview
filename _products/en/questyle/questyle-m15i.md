## Overview

The Questyle M15i is a portable USB-C DAC/headphone amplifier announced in 2024 as the second-generation successor to the original M15. It adds Apple MFi certification, an ESS ES9281AC DAC chip supporting PCM up to 32-bit/768kHz and native DSD512, and two proprietary Current Mode Amplification (CMA) SiP modules driving 3.5mm single-ended and 4.4mm balanced outputs [1][2]. Questyle's current-mode amplifier technology was patented in 2006 and commercialized in 2011, and the company continues to position it as its core technical differentiator. Current market price is 189 USD [1].

## Scientific Validity

$$ \Large \text{0.8} $$

Manufacturer specifications state THD+N of 0.0003% (headline) [2] and 0.00057% (4.4mm balanced output) [1], frequency response of 20Hz-20kHz within ±0.1dB [1], and a noise floor stated as -130dB [1]. These figures indicate excellent distortion control, frequency response, and noise performance. No independent third-party measurement of the M15i itself has been published. Independent testing of the predecessor M15's shared amplifier stage found real-world THD+N of approximately 0.0008%, still indicating excellent distortion control despite exceeding that model's own manufacturer claim [3]. Because the M15i's own performance data rests on manufacturer specifications only, this evaluation is treated conservatively, and no independent verification of these claims currently exists.

## Technology Level

$$ \Large \text{0.6} $$

The M15i's amplification is built around two independent proprietary Current Mode Amplification (CMA) SiP modules protected by a patent (US 9,614,483 B2) [3], a genuinely in-house, non-OEM circuit reflecting substantial accumulated engineering know-how not replicated by other portable DAC/amp manufacturers — crediting patent ownership, in-house design, and technical expertise. This is offset by two factors: independent measurement of the identical CMA output stage used in the predecessor M15 found it fell short of its own manufacturer-rated output power into a 300-ohm load [3], indicating underperformance relative to its stated specification, and a competing product built around a standard all-in-one chip solution was independently measured to deliver strong output into the same load impedance [5], showing that matching this real-world performance does not require replicating the proprietary circuit. The DAC chip (ESS ES9281AC) and power management IC are standard, non-differentiating components common across the product category, contributing no additional credit. On balance, the circuit's proprietary engineering pedigree is weighed against its shortfall relative to its own rated specification and the absence of a demonstrated practical advantage over standard alternatives.

## Cost-Performance

$$ \Large \text{0.3} $$

The Questyle M15i currently sells for 189 USD [1]. The cheapest identified product with equivalent-or-better user-facing functions and measured performance is the Fosi Audio DS2 at 59.99 USD [4], which provides USB-C input, 3.5mm single-ended and 4.4mm balanced outputs, and broad OS compatibility.

Fosi Audio DS2 demonstrates equivalent-or-better measured performance: THD+N of approximately 0.0001% (-120dB, independently measured) [5] versus the M15i's own manufacturer-claimed 0.00057% (4.4mm balanced) [1], and a manufacturer-stated SNR of 130dB or higher [4] versus the M15i's manufacturer-claimed -130dB noise floor [1]. Format ceilings differ (384kHz/DSD256 versus 768kHz/DSD512), but no commercially distributed content exceeds either limit, so this creates no practical compatibility gap. This comparison is provisional because the M15i's own specifications are manufacturer-published rather than independently measured.

## Reliability & Support

$$ \Large \text{0.3} $$

The M15i carries a 1-year limited warranty against manufacturing defects, plus a separate 7-day money-back guarantee [1], at the shorter end of typical coverage. Paid post-warranty repair service is explicitly limited to mainland China customers, with customers in other regions directed only to make further inquiries [1], a more restricted support scope than standard global warranty and parts-support coverage. The CNC-machined aluminum chassis shows no reported flex or structural fragility [1][2]. No statistical failure-rate data or documented hardware defects were found for this product.

## Rationality of Design Philosophy

$$ \Large \text{0.5} $$

The M15i pairs a standard, widely-used DAC chip with a proprietary in-house current-mode amplifier circuit, a cost allocation directed toward function and measured performance rather than materials or brand — a rational, function-directed choice. The generational update over the predecessor adds Apple MFi certification, higher PCM/DSD format ceilings, and a manual gain switch [1][2], representing real functional advancement, though the core amplifier output stage, the primary proprietary differentiator, remains unchanged from the predecessor. Independent measurement of that shared output stage found it falls short of its own rated 300-ohm output specification [3], and a competing product using a standard all-in-one chip solution was independently measured to drive the same load more effectively [5], indicating no demonstrated practical advantage from the proprietary approach over standard alternatives. No independent verification of the M15i's own headline distortion and noise-floor claims exists, and marketing language retains some subjective framing alongside the numeric specifications. These positive and negative factors offset one another, yielding a neutral overall assessment.

## Advice

Buyers who value Apple MFi certification, high format ceilings (768kHz PCM/DSD512), and Questyle's in-house current-mode amplifier circuit for its accumulated engineering pedigree may find the M15i's feature set appealing. However, no independent third-party measurement of the M15i itself is currently available, and prior independent testing of the shared amplifier stage found output power into higher-impedance loads below rated specification. Buyers prioritizing verified measured performance per dollar spent should note that independently measured lower-priced alternatives exist in this category.
