## Overview

The Questyle M15C is a portable, driver-free USB-C DAC/headphone amplifier positioned as a lower-cost addition to Questyle's current M-series lineup, sitting below the M12i and M15i and replacing the discontinued original M15. It combines a Cirrus Logic CS43198 DAC chip with Questyle's proprietary Current Mode Amplification (CMA) circuit, implemented as four independent SiP amplifier modules, supporting PCM up to 32-bit/384kHz and native DSD256 through simultaneous 3.5mm single-ended and 4.4mm balanced outputs [1][2]. The CNC-anodized aluminum housing includes a transparent panel exposing the internal circuit board. Current market price is 129 USD [1][2][3].

## Scientific Validity

$$ \Large \text{0.7} $$

No independent third-party lab measurements of the M15C were found; the following figures are manufacturer-published specifications. THD+N is stated at 0.00028%, indicating excellent distortion control, and frequency response is stated at ±0.1dB across 20Hz-20kHz, also indicating excellent performance. S/N ratio, dynamic range, crosstalk, and IMD are not published by the manufacturer, and no independent source provides this data; their absence does not reduce the score. Because both available figures originate exclusively from manufacturer claims with no independent confirmation, a conservative downward adjustment is applied to the evaluation.

## Technology Level

$$ \Large \text{0.4} $$

The M15C's amplification stage uses Questyle's proprietary, patented Current Mode Amplification circuit (implemented as four SiP modules), a genuinely in-house design reflecting accumulated engineering know-how, crediting patent ownership and design origin. This is offset by several factors: the DAC chip (Cirrus Logic CS43198) and USB controller (SA9312L) are standard, off-the-shelf third-party components common across the product category; the CMA circuit itself has been in continuous, unchanged use since 2012 rather than representing a recent technical advancement; the M15C is explicitly a cost/format-reduced compromise variant within Questyle's own concurrent lineup, capped at 384kHz/DSD256 versus the M12i/M15i siblings' 768kHz/DSD512; and no independent measurement confirms any performance advantage attributable to the CMA circuit over conventional voltage-mode designs, with several standard-architecture competitors already matching or exceeding its published THD+N figure without requiring the proprietary technology. On balance, the mixed in-house/third-party design origin, lineup-compromise positioning, and lack of demonstrated competitive edge outweigh the patent and know-how credit.

## Cost-Performance

$$ \Large \text{1.0} $$

The Questyle M15C currently sells for 129 USD [1][2][3]. Its user-facing functions are USB-C driver-free input, simultaneous 3.5mm single-ended and 4.4mm balanced outputs, PCM up to 32-bit/384kHz, and native DSD up to DSD256. Manufacturer-stated measured performance is THD+N of 0.00028% and frequency response of ±0.1dB (20Hz-20kHz).

An exhaustive progressive-price search of portable USB-C dongle DAC/headphone amplifiers, using only function and specification criteria from 60 USD to 199 USD, found no product priced below 129 USD with equivalent-or-better measured performance alongside equivalent-or-better user-facing functions. Candidates in this range either published a worse THD+N or frequency response figure, or left frequency response unpublished entirely and so could not be confirmed equivalent. One candidate, the Audioengine HXL, matched or exceeded the M15C on both metrics — THD+N of 0.0002% versus the M15C's 0.00028%, and frequency response of ±0.1dB over a wider 20Hz-40kHz band versus the M15C's ±0.1dB over 20Hz-20kHz — but its 169 USD price is higher than the M15C's 129 USD, so it cannot serve as a comparator [4].

No cheaper equivalent-or-better product exists, so the Questyle M15C is the cheapest available option among products with its specific combination of functions and measured performance: CP = 1.0.

## Reliability & Support

$$ \Large \text{0.5} $$

The M15C carries a 12-month manufacturer warranty, at the shorter end of typical coverage, and post-warranty repair service is explicitly limited to mainland China, with customers elsewhere directed to remote/online support channels only [5][6]. Offsetting this, the product is a simple, bus-powered USB-C dongle with no battery and few moving parts beyond a manual gain switch, giving it inherently robust, low-complexity construction [7]. No statistical failure-rate or MTBF data exists for this model. The only documented issues are a single review unit's cosmetic fit/finish variance and isolated unofficial forum reports of connection popping on certain Android devices, both unit-level or anecdotal rather than confirmed systemic defects [7]. The shorter warranty and restricted repair scope offset the robust, low-complexity construction, yielding an average score.

## Rationality of Design Philosophy

$$ \Large \text{0.6} $$

Questyle's stated design approach for the M15C centers on quantitative specifications (THD+N, frequency response, output power) rather than vacuum tubes, R2R DACs, or analog nostalgia, though marketing materials also include some unverifiable subjective language, making the approach balanced rather than purely measurement-driven. Cost allocation is reasonably function-oriented, pairing an in-house proprietary amplifier stage with an adequately capable, non-exotic DAC chip and a modest aluminum housing, without evidence that a majority of cost goes toward non-functional materials or brand premium. As a compact, driver-free smartphone accessory, the product itself embodies an alternative to bulkier dedicated equipment, avoiding the low evaluation reserved for products lacking justification for dedicated hardware. The real-world benefit of the proprietary CMA circuit remains unconfirmed by independent measurement, and the product's reduced format ceiling relative to its own lineup siblings shows no functional progression, though this format difference carries no audible relevance on its own. Overall, the design direction is neutral-to-rational, with function-oriented cost allocation as the clearest positive factor.

## Advice

Buyers seeking a driver-free USB-C dongle DAC/amp with dual 3.5mm/4.4mm outputs and strong manufacturer-claimed distortion figures at the lowest confirmed price for this combination of functions and performance will find the M15C's cost-performance position difficult to beat. However, no independent third-party measurement of the M15C exists, so its performance claims remain unverified, and its Cirrus Logic-based DAC chip caps format support below higher-priced siblings in its own lineup. Buyers requiring repair service outside mainland China should note that only remote support is currently offered.
