## Overview

The Denon AH-D5200 is a passive wired headphone with 50 mm FreeEdge drivers and zebrawood housings. It includes a detachable 3 m cable with 3.5 mm plug and 6.3 mm adapter, but no Bluetooth or active noise cancellation. Denon's US store lists it in stock at 799 USD [1][4].

## Scientific Validity

$$ \Large \text{0.5} $$

The manufacturer specifies 24 ohms, 103 dB/mW sensitivity and 5 Hz–40 kHz response without a tolerance [4]. Headphone Test Lab's Harman 2017-corrected response gives an error standard deviation of approximately 3.0 dB, calculated from the graph's 30 third-octave values from 20 Hz to 16 kHz. This describes the smoothed left/right average, not an unsmoothed or universally comparable result; reduced deep bass and upper-midrange output remain evident [10]. The corresponding Hi-Fi News test reports sensitivity of 117.2 dB/V and THD below 0.1% at both 100 Hz and 1 kHz at 90 dB SPL, but also identifies a resonance around 1.3 kHz [11].

DIY-Audio-Heaven's different, corrected flatbed fixture shows a dominant second harmonic of about 0.3% at 1 kHz, 0.5% at 200 Hz and 0.6–1% in much of the deep bass at 90 dB SPL. Higher harmonics are much lower; the stated 0.2% measurement floor concerns the second harmonic [3]. These results cannot be treated as identical-condition replications of the Hi-Fi News test. RAA's different HDM-X fixture also shows an upper-midrange recession [2]. Overall fidelity is moderate: distortion is generally controlled, but tonal deviations remain. No quantitative passive-isolation measurement was found, so attenuation cannot be graded numerically.

## Technology Level

$$ \Large \text{0.5} $$

FreeEdge suspends the diaphragm on a separate rolled surround, with compliant mounting intended to reduce unwanted vibration. Denon also describes its own artificial leather and shaped pads [1][4]. These demonstrate product-specific mechanical development and acoustic/materials expertise. The implementation nevertheless uses mature driver, suspension and enclosure techniques and remains entirely analog and mechanical. The documentation does not establish an adopted in-house patent or a technical barrier that would take competitors years to reproduce. Manufacturing in Japan alone does not establish ownership of the driver technology.

## Cost-Performance

$$ \Large \text{1.0} $$

This site evaluates based solely on functionality and measured performance values, without considering driver types or configurations. The comparison covers passive analog playback, replaceable cables, adequate sensitivity, frequency response, distortion and isolation. The AH-D5200 costs 799 USD [1]. FiiO FT1 at 164.99 USD and AKG K371 at 199 USD are in-stock alternatives with analog connectivity, detachable cables and independent measurements [6][7][8][9].

On the same measurement site's flatbed fixture at the same nominal 90 dB SPL test level, FT1's deep-bass harmonics reach approximately 1–2%, exceeding the AH-D5200's corresponding results. K371 has a prominent rise near 4–5 kHz, reaching approximately 2% in the plotted second harmonic [3][6][8]. A connector adapter cannot remove these differences. External EQ can change tonal balance, but an EQ/amplifier bundle has not been shown to match all the measured performance at comparable output levels. Neither candidate is adopted as an equivalent-or-better substitute. Based on the functions and measurements confirmed in this comparison, the AH-D5200 is the lowest-priced qualifying option, yielding CP = 1.0. This remains provisional: quantitative isolation is unavailable, and the Harman-error figure cannot be compared indiscriminately across fixtures. It does not establish that every cheaper headphone performs worse.

## Reliability & Support

$$ \Large \text{0.6} $$

The manufacturer specifies a one-year warranty in Japan; coverage should be checked for the country of purchase [4]. The simple passive construction, detachable cable, replaceable pads and metal adjustment parts support maintenance [3][4]. Denon also organizes authorized service facilities across multiple regions, including headphone repair services [12]. These practical strengths offset the short published warranty. No model-specific statistical failure rate or unusually long guaranteed parts-supply period was available.

## Rationality of Design Philosophy

$$ \Large \text{0.5} $$

Denon shares construction and parts with the AH-D7200 and changes the diaphragm, magnet, headband and cable to reduce cost [4]. That is a concrete manufacturing-economy approach. The suspension, housing damping and pad shaping also serve identifiable mechanical functions. However, claims that 4N OFC preserves signal purity do not demonstrate an audible advantage over an electrically suitable ordinary cable [4]. Published information does not disclose a cost breakdown, so the share spent on decorative materials cannot be determined.

## Advice

The AH-D5200 offers straightforward analog operation and serviceable wear parts, but its upper-midrange recession deserves attention when accurate tonal balance matters. Its CP result reflects trade-offs in the measured alternatives rather than a claim of universally superior sound. Compare fit and tonal response with cheaper models before purchasing, and verify the local price and applicable warranty terms [1][4][5].
