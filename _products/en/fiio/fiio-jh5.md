## Overview

The FiiO JH5, also marketed under the affiliated Jade Audio sub-brand, is a wired in-ear monitor released in early 2024. It combines a 10mm carbon-based dynamic driver with four balanced armature drivers across three isolated acoustic chambers. The official material states that its acoustic design draws on the FH7 and FH9, and the package includes a detachable 0.78mm 2-pin, 392-strand silver-plated copper cable. [1]

## Scientific Validity

$$ \Large \text{0.4} $$

Two independent coupler datasets show closely matched left and right channels and reproduce the same broad response pattern. [2][3] With the Super* Review clone-IEC-711 graph normalized to 60dB at 500Hz, JH5 is roughly 2dB-4dB below the Harman IE 2019 v2 target from 20Hz-80Hz, remains broadly close from 200Hz-1kHz, rises about 1dB above it near 2.7kHz-3kHz, and develops large peaks and dips from about 4kHz-8kHz, including a sharp drop near 5kHz. [2] The spectrum dataset, measured with Sony tips, similarly shows 20Hz-80Hz about 2dB-3dB low, 100Hz-300Hz about 2dB-3dB high, close tracking through much of 500Hz-3kHz, and an irregular 5kHz-8kHz region. [3] Results above 10kHz are not treated as reliable because of coupler limitations. FiiO specifies 20Hz-40kHz, 13 ohms at 1kHz, and 111dB SPL/mW, but supplies no response tolerance; the cited sources provide no independent distortion or isolation measurement. [1] The consistent bass and upper-treble deviations, together with the incomplete measurement set, support a Scientific Validity score of 0.4.

## Technology Level

$$ \Large \text{0.4} $$

The JH5 combines a carbon-composite dynamic driver, four balanced-armature drivers, three acoustic chambers, a passive crossover, nozzle tuning, and a silver-plated cable. [1] FiiO's documented transfer of acoustic-design concepts from the FH7 and FH9 indicates accumulated product-line know-how. These remain established passive-IEM techniques, however, and the cited official material does not document a patented mechanism, technical paper, or implementation that would be difficult for competitors to reproduce. Its technical content lies in competent mechanical and acoustic integration rather than recent electronic processing or software, supporting a Technology Level score of 0.4.

CP = 30.52 USD ÷ 90.10 USD = 0.3387

This site evaluates based solely on functionality and measured performance values, without considering driver types or configurations. The comparator is Moondrop CHU II at 30.52 USD, converted from its current in-stock price using the same official rate. [5][8][9] Both products are passive wired IEMs with a 3.5mm analog plug and detachable 0.78mm 2-pin cable; their published impedance and sensitivity are sufficient for ordinary portable sources. [1][8] On the same Super* Review clone-IEC-711 rig, CHU II is about 1dB-1.5dB higher from 20Hz-60Hz and therefore closer to the target, is approximately equal from 100Hz-1kHz, rises earlier through 1kHz-3kHz, and is generally smoother than JH5 across 4kHz-8kHz. [6] A separate GRAS 45CA test measured CHU II at the 94dB SPL reference level with THD near or below 0.1% through almost all of the band; even at 104dB SPL, bass distortion peaked near 0.5% and the 2.5kHz feature was about 0.2%. Its impedance measured 18.76 ohms at 1kHz and remained nearly flat, and 61mV produced 94dB SPL. [7] JH5 lacks an independent distortion measurement, so that axis remains provisional, but the comparator is equal-or-better in the directly comparable response data and has independently verified low distortion without losing an essential function. The calculated ratio rounds to a Cost-Performance score of 0.3.

## Reliability & Support

$$ \Large \text{0.4} $$

FiiO's published terms provide a one-month replacement period and one year of free maintenance for the main unit; accessories and packaging are excluded, and local-agent terms take priority. [10] The detachable cable is replaceable, but the cited sources do not establish unusually high durability for the five-driver earpieces and do not provide failure-rate, RMA, or MTBF statistics for this model. The one-year limit and absence of extended support provisions support a Reliability & Support score of 0.4.

## Rationality of Design Philosophy

$$ \Large \text{0.5} $$

The JH5 uses a passive crossover, chamber isolation, and nozzle tuning to control acoustic output through mechanisms directly related to frequency response. [1] This is a rational basis for an IEM design, although it remains a conventional passive implementation without adaptive correction or another distinctive functional advance. The official material identifies the included 392-strand silver-plated cable as a specification but does not claim a measured or audible fidelity benefit from it. [1] The design is functionally justified but technically conservative, supporting a Rationality score of 0.5.

## Advice

The FiiO JH5 is a passive hybrid IEM with a detachable cable, but the cited measurements show low bass below the Harman target and substantial 4kHz-8kHz irregularity. They do not establish a fidelity advantage over the substantially cheaper Moondrop CHU II, whose same-rig response is equal-or-better and whose distortion is independently low. Buyers should verify connector and fit requirements and account for JH5's one-year service period rather than treating driver count or cable strand count as evidence of better sound.
