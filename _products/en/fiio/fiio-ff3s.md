## Overview

FiiO FF3S is an open-type wired earbud released in 2023, succeeding the FiiO FF3. It carries a 14.2mm beryllium-plated dynamic driver, an internal dual-cavity acoustic tube aimed at extending bass response, and a detachable 0.78mm 2-pin cable with swappable 3.5mm/4.4mm plugs, priced at 89.99 USD [1].

## Scientific Validity

$$ \Large \text{0.5} $$

Manufacturer specifications state a frequency response of 20Hz-20kHz (rated) and a separately reported "effective" range of 42.5Hz-17kHz using FiiO's own undisclosed test method, but neither figure carries a stated ±dB tolerance, and FiiO publishes no THD or S/N ratio specification for this earbud [1]. No independent third-party measurement is available for verification. Without a toleranced deviation figure or any THD/S/N data, performance cannot be classified as excellent, acceptable, or problematic on any measurable axis, placing this product at the midpoint between problematic and transparent performance.

## Technology Level

$$ \Large \text{0.3} $$

The FF3S centers on a 14.2mm beryllium-plated dynamic driver and a Daikoku copper-clad-aluminum voice coil, both mature, industry-common components used across the earphone market for years [1]. Its detachable 0.78mm 2-pin cable with swappable 3.5mm/4.4mm plugs is new to FiiO's own earbud lineup but is a market-standard solution already widespread by 2023 [2]. The internal dual-cavity acoustic tube is in-house-developed, but relies on an established acoustic-tuning technique with no associated patent and no independent measurement confirming its claimed bass-extension effect, giving it limited durability as a competitive advantage. As a fully passive earbud, there is no digital or electronic integration of any kind. In-house design work provides a partial offset, but outdated core driver technology, a low barrier to replication of the acoustic-tube design, and the complete absence of digital integration result in a below-average technology level.

## Cost-Performance

$$ \Large \text{0.5} $$

This site evaluates based solely on functionality and measured performance values, without considering driver types or configurations.

The FiiO FF3S currently sells for 89.99 USD [1]. The cheapest identified product with equivalent-or-better user-facing functions and measured performance is the Truthear HOLA, priced at 18.99 USD [3]. Both products offer a detachable 0.78mm 2-pin cable, but the HOLA ships with a single-ended 3.5mm cable only; adding a 24.99 USD aftermarket 0.78mm 2-pin 4.4mm balanced cable [4] closes this gap, bringing its normalized total to 43.98 USD. Truthear documents THD of 0.1% or below at 1kHz/94dB SPL and a frequency response of 20Hz-20kHz (-3dB) measured with an IEC60318-4 coupler [3], while the FF3S has no THD figure of any kind and only an untoleranced nominal frequency response range [1]; on both measured axes, the HOLA's documented performance is equal-or-better.

## Reliability & Support

$$ \Large \text{0.5} $$

FiiO backs the FF3S earbud unit with a 1-month replacement guarantee plus a 1-year free repair warranty; accessories such as the cable, tips, and case are excluded from coverage [5]. This warranty period is shorter than the 2-3 year terms common among audio manufacturers. The product itself is a simple, passive single dynamic-driver design with no active electronics, which is inherently resistant to failure, and its detachable 2-pin cable allows cable replacement without full-unit service. No statistical failure-rate data, recalls, or documented hardware issues were found for this product. The combination of a below-average warranty term and an inherently robust, simple construction results in an average overall reliability and support level.

## Rationality of Design Philosophy

$$ \Large \text{0.4} $$

The FF3S's update direction is ergonomics- and accessory-driven rather than measurement-driven: FiiO carried over the same 14.2mm beryllium-plated driver used in the FF3 unchanged [2], directing engineering effort toward shell weight reduction, materials, and a newly added detachable cable system with swappable 3.5mm/4.4mm plugs [1]. Its launch price is below the outgoing FF3's, and the added cable modularity is a genuine functional gain, reflecting active cost and value optimization [2]. However, the product retains a decades-old open, non-isolating earbud form factor without addressing its inherent lack of isolation, at a point in time when sealed in-ear monitors and ANC-equipped wireless earbuds were already dominant, better-measured alternatives, a conservative choice with no stated scientific justification. The claimed bass extension to 42.5Hz from the internal acoustic tube remains unverified by any independent measurement, and the 152-strand silver-plated monocrystalline copper cable represents a materials investment with no established audible benefit. These factors combine for a slightly below-average design-philosophy rationality score.

## Advice

Buyers should treat the FF3S primarily as an accessory-focused update to the FF3, valued for its lighter shell and swappable-plug detachable cable rather than for a measured sound-quality advance, since its core driver is unchanged and no independent THD, S/N ratio, or toleranced frequency-response data currently exists. Readers prioritizing verified measured performance or passive isolation should also compare sealed in-ear monitors with published third-party measurements, such as the Truthear HOLA [3], before purchasing.
