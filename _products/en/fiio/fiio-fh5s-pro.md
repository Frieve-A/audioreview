## Overview

FiiO FH5s Pro is a hybrid in-ear monitor combining two beryllium-plated dynamic drivers and a Knowles dual balanced-armature driver, launched in August 2021 as a cable-upgraded variant of the FH5s (May 2021). It is differentiated from the base FH5s solely by its bundled "LC-RC" 152-core Litz silver-plated copper cable with swappable 2.5/3.5/4.4mm plugs, versus the base model's 120-core cable [1]. Current market price is 319.99 USD.

## Scientific Validity

$$ \Large \text{0.6} $$

Third-party measurements from Soundnews.net show THD of 0.267%, indicating average-to-good distortion performance [2]. Frequency response deviation from the Harman IE target curve is 1.98dB, indicating good target compliance [2]. Sub-bass THD rises to approximately 2% below 60Hz, a characteristic broadly consistent with dynamic-driver IEMs at low frequencies and not treated as the primary distortion indicator. S/N Ratio, SINAD, IMD, and Crosstalk are not applicable, as this is a fully passive wired IEM with no onboard electronics. FiiO publishes a frequency range of 10Hz-40kHz but no ±dB tolerance figure or THD/S-N specification for this model [1]. Overall measured performance is moderate-to-good.

## Technology Level

$$ \Large \text{0.1} $$

FH5s Pro's driver technology — beryllium-plated dynamic drivers, a Knowles TWFK-30017 dual balanced-armature receiver, physical tri-switch tuning, and a CNC-machined aluminum-magnesium shell [1] — is mature, industry-standard technology that is easily replicated by competitors and provides no lasting competitive advantage. The device includes no digital, DSP, or AI integration; tuning is achieved purely through analog/mechanical switches. Critically, the "Pro" variant reviewed here differs from the base FH5s only in its bundled cable [1], a change with no established audible or measurable performance benefit, representing stagnation rather than technical progression between the two models. In-house design of the driver layout and shell [1] is a positive factor, but is outweighed by the outdated component base, ease of replication, absence of digital integration, and lack of advancement over the base FH5s.

## Cost-Performance

$$ \Large \text{0.1} $$

This site evaluates based solely on functionality and measured performance values, without considering driver types or configurations.

FiiO FH5s Pro is currently priced at 319.99 USD. The cheapest identified product with equivalent-or-better user-facing functionality and equal-or-better measured performance is the Tanchjim One (base 3.5mm wired variant), priced at 24.99 USD [3][4]. Both are wired, passive in-ear monitors offering detachable-cable analog listening; the Tanchjim One lacks swappable plug terminations and physical tuning switches, but these are minor convenience/tone-shaping features not essential to the core listening function and are replicable through playback-side software EQ.

Tanchjim One demonstrates equivalent-or-better measured performance:
- THD: <0.062% vs 0.267% (comparison better) [3]
- FR deviation: 1.91dB vs 1.98dB from Harman IE target (comparison better) [3]

## Reliability & Support

$$ \Large \text{0.5} $$

FiiO provides a 1-year manufacturer warranty on the FH5s Pro's mainframe; accessories, including the bundled cable, are explicitly excluded from coverage [5]. This is offset by a documented international support infrastructure, including an official support portal, community forum, and a product authenticity-verification tool, though region-specific service-level consistency is not confirmed [5]. No statistical failure-rate data or product-specific known issues were found. General MMCX-connector wear characteristics are industry-wide and not documented as a defect specific to this model, so they were not treated as a negative factor. Repair costs, parts availability, and long-term track record are undocumented. Firmware update support is not applicable, as this is a fully passive IEM with no digital components.

## Rationality of Design Philosophy

$$ \Large \text{0.3} $$

FH5s Pro's design centers on user-adjustable, subjective tone-shaping via three physical switches rather than a documented measurement-target design goal [1]. Its sole differentiation from the base FH5s — the "Pro" cable — represents a cable construction change with no established audible or measurable benefit, directing cost toward a non-functional element. This same lack of acoustic change between the FH5s and FH5s Pro also represents stagnation rather than measurable model progression. As a purpose-built transducer, the product's existence as dedicated audio equipment is inherently justified. These factors combine to a below-average score.

## Advice

Prospective buyers should recognize that the FH5s Pro's 319.99 USD price is driven almost entirely by an upgraded cable with no established audible or measurable benefit over the base FH5s, which shares identical drivers and shell. Measured THD (0.267%) and Harman-target frequency response deviation (1.98dB) [2] represent average-to-good performance, but the far cheaper Tanchjim One [3][4] matches or exceeds these figures. Buyers prioritizing swappable-plug convenience or physical tuning switches may still find value in the Pro cable, but should not expect it to change sound quality.
