## Overview

The FiiO FF1 is the entry-level model in FiiO's FF open-wearing earbud lineup, released as a lower-cost sibling to the stainless-steel FF3. It uses FiiO's own 14.2mm PU diaphragm with beryllium plating, a driver lineage traced back to the company's EM5 in-ear monitor. Current US market price is approximately 19.90 USD, and the package bundles a detachable 0.78mm 2-pin cable, an in-line microphone/remote, interchangeable foam tuning covers, and an active USB-C to 3.5mm DAC/amplifier dongle. [1]

## Scientific Validity

$$ \Large \text{0.5} $$

FiiO publishes distortion (THD) for the FF1 at under 0.2% (150Hz-20kHz @ 94dB SPL), indicating good distortion control for an earphone. [1] No independent third-party measurement source publishes any data for the FF1, so this THD figure is manufacturer-only and unverified. Frequency response is stated only as 20Hz-20kHz with no deviation figure, so it cannot be numerically scored and is treated as unscoreable rather than as a negative indicator. As a passive earbud with no onboard electronics, further audio-electronics metrics do not apply. Because the sole available metric is manufacturer-only and lacks independent verification, a conservative adjustment applies, resulting in a moderate overall assessment.

## Technology Level

$$ \Large \text{0.4} $$

The FF1 uses a 14.2mm PU diaphragm with beryllium plating, an internal acoustic resistance pipe, interchangeable tuning covers, and a bundled active USB-C to 3.5mm DAC/amplifier dongle that is recognized by host devices as a USB audio device rather than acting as a passive adapter. [1][5] No evidence of third-party OEM/ODM sourcing was found for the driver, earning credit for in-house design ownership. However, beryllium-plated diaphragms and internal acoustic damping pipes have been standard industry practice for over a decade, and bundled USB-C dongle DACs are now a mainstream accessory rather than a differentiator. No patents or unique know-how beyond routine engineering were identified, and the technology can be replicated by competitors without meaningful delay. Technology integration is limited to a standard digital accessory rather than advanced software or cloud integration. In-house design credit is offset by mature, easily replicated technology, landing below average.

## Reliability & Support

$$ \Large \text{0.6} $$

FiiO backs the FF1 mainframe with a 1-year warranty; the cable, tips, and adapter are explicitly excluded from coverage. [2] This is below the typical multi-year benchmark, so a negative adjustment applies. Support is manufacturer-direct through globally accessible self-service channels — FAQ, firmware/driver portal, community forum, and an authenticity-verification tool — plus an authorized distributor network, earning credit for global manufacturer support. [3] The product is mechanically simple, using a single passive dynamic driver with a basic in-line microphone/remote circuit and no other internal electronics, which is inherently resistant to the failure modes of more complex devices and earns a simple/robust design credit. No statistical failure-rate data or documented product-specific defects were found, so this factor remains neutral. The cable uses a non-universal connector shape that limits aftermarket replacement options, though this was not treated as evidence of inherent fragility. [4]

## Rationality of Design Philosophy

$$ \Large \text{0.5} $$

The FF1's open, non-sealing earbud form factor is retained primarily for wearing comfort — side-sleeping, gaming, and calls — rather than for measured performance, even though sealed in-ear designs, wireless connectivity, and DSP-equipped alternatives were already commercially mature at the product's release. [1][4] Combined with driver, acoustic-pipe, dongle, and tuning-cover technology that are all reused or industry-standard approaches, this reflects a conservative development attitude, earning a negative adjustment. On the positive side, the bulk of cost is directed toward function — a proven driver platform, a detachable cable, and a genuinely active USB-C DAC/amplifier dongle for device compatibility — rather than toward aesthetics or brand premium, both rated low in cost allocation, earning a function-focused-cost credit. These adjustments offset each other, landing at a neutral score.

## Advice

The FF1 suits buyers who want FiiO's beryllium-plated driver platform and a bundled USB-C DAC dongle in an open, non-sealing earbud at a low price, and who value the comfort of an unsealed fit for side-sleeping or calls over isolation or bass extension. Because no independent measurement source has verified its manufacturer-stated THD, and its 1-year warranty excludes the cable and adapter, buyers who require lab-verified performance or longer coverage should weigh those factors before purchasing.
