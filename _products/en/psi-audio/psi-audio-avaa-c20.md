## Overview

PSI Audio AVAA C20 is a compact active bass absorber released in 2016 and still sold alongside the newer digital AVAA C214. Its microphone, resistive membrane, driver, amplifier, and analog feedback/feedforward control lower acoustic impedance at a room boundary rather than inserting processing into the playback signal. The manufacturer specifies automatic operation across 15Hz to 150Hz without measurement or calibration; one or more units can be placed near pressure maxima such as walls and corners [1][6].

## Scientific Validity

$$ \Large \text{0.7} $$

The function-relevant evidence is the change in spatial frequency response and low-frequency decay time, not electronic-signal metrics such as THD or crosstalk. Barry Rudolph published before/after Room EQ Wizard frequency-response images from one room using two C20 units. Direct inspection of those graphs shows that switching the units on substantially reduces a deep, narrow cancellation around 70–80Hz, while much of the remaining plotted response stays similar [2]. The images do not provide a readable numeric dB comparison or enough test metadata for reproduction. Tape Op reports measurements with two C20 units in opposite corners, a Cross-Spectrum-calibrated Dayton Audio EMM-6 microphone, and a room already fitted with 21 passive bass traps plus a Helmholtz resonator; its text reports smoother waterfall decay and reduced resonances down to 28Hz [3]. The current page no longer exposes the linked waterfall plots, so that result cannot be independently read numerically. These two single-room reports support a real, audible low-frequency benefit, but neither isolates one unit nor provides controlled absorption coefficient, decay-time reduction in seconds, repeatability, or room-to-room variance. The evidence is therefore good but incomplete.

## Technology Level

$$ \Large \text{0.7} $$

The C20 implements an in-house active-absorption mechanism protected by European patent EP3225038B1. It combines a pressure microphone, porous resistive layer, moving transducer, and feedback/feedforward control to modify boundary impedance rather than generate room-wide anti-noise [1][7]. Four years of development with two Swiss universities, a decade of continued production, and the absence of a directly equivalent broadband automatic competitor indicate substantial specialized know-how and a durable implementation barrier. The design is nevertheless a 2016 all-analog platform; PSI Audio's newer C214 adds digital control, and the C20 has no software, network, or advanced computing integration. No adoption of this exact patented approach by another manufacturer was found. Its proprietary mechanism and accumulated expertise are strong, but its age and analog-only integration limit the current technology score.

## Cost-Performance

$$ \Large \text{1.0} $$

The current U.S. price is 3,115.50 USD for one unit [4]. A function-only search for finished active room-mode absorbers found Bag End E-Trap as the most relevant alternative design. Its official datasheet specifies 20Hz to 80Hz operation, simultaneous tuning of only two separated resonances, and manual setup with an FFT analyzer and measurement microphone; its own before/after plots show the result after tuning to two selected frequencies [5]. It therefore lacks the C20's automatic, calibration-free treatment of all modes across 15Hz to 150Hz and is not functionally equivalent. No lower-priced finished product with the C20's complete user-facing function set and documented in-room benefit was identified.

## Reliability & Support

$$ \Large \text{0.6} $$

PSI Audio warrants the electronics and transducers for five years and the wooden cabinet for two years. Warranty and servicing are initiated through the reseller, while manufacturer repair is available when the unit is returned to Relec SA; non-warranty work is quoted, and transport costs and risks are assigned to the customer [6]. The manual also documents overload/overheat indication and the need to reduce sensitivity if the unit becomes unstable in a very small or highly reverberant room. These are operating limits rather than evidence of a recurring hardware defect. The powered microphone, amplifier, and moving transducer make construction more complex than a passive absorber, and no RMA rate, failure-rate, parts-supply term, or quantified long-term durability data is published. The long electronics warranty is a clear positive, while the mainly dealer-mediated service model and missing field data keep support below the highest level.

## Rationality of Design Philosophy

$$ \Large \text{0.9} $$

The C20 addresses an audible room-acoustics problem through a testable physical mechanism. Its microphone, resistive membrane, driver, amplifier, and control loop are all directly involved in absorption, and the compact autonomous integration replaces a much larger volume of passive low-frequency treatment without altering the source signal. Independent in-room measurements show that the mechanism changes both modal response and decay [2][3]. An analog controller is rational here because it operates as a local feedback system, not because of nostalgia or a claim that analog sound is inherently superior. The dedicated product is also justified where broadband low-frequency treatment must fit into limited floor area. The C20's all-analog control has no software or network interface, while PSI Audio's newer C214 adds digital control [1]. That limits current functional integration, but the C20's development direction remains scientific, function-focused, and innovative.

## Advice

AVAA C20 is appropriate when measurements have confirmed persistent room modes below 150Hz and large passive traps are impractical. The accessible independent data show meaningful improvement with two units in specific rooms, but they do not establish a universal dB or decay-time reduction for one C20; room geometry, placement, and quantity remain important. Measure the room before and after installation even though the device itself requires no tuning. Buyers should budget 3,115.50 USD per unit and account for dealer-led service, customer-paid transport, and the different five-year electronics and two-year cabinet warranty periods.
