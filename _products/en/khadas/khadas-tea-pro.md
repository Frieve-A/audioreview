## Overview

KHADAS Tea Pro is a card-sized portable DAC/headphone amplifier for wired USB-C and Bluetooth 5.4 use. It supports PCM up to 768kHz/32-bit and native DSD512 over USB, as well as LDAC, aptX Adaptive, aptX HD, AAC, and SBC over Bluetooth. Its 7.85mm-thick, 96g chassis incorporates 19 magnets for attachment to compatible phones and provides 3.5mm single-ended and 4.4mm balanced outputs, a 0.95-inch AMOLED display, seven EQ presets, and a built-in microphone. The manufacturer rates battery life at no less than 11 hours over Bluetooth AAC and no less than 8 hours over USB-C at 44.1kHz [1][2].

## Scientific Validity

$$ \Large \text{0.8} $$

The manufacturer's technical specification lists, at 1kHz with no load, THD+N of 0.000126%, S/N Ratio of 122dB, dynamic range of 121dB, and noise below 3.5uVrms for the 4.4mm output. The corresponding 3.5mm figures are 0.000158%, 120dB, 119dB, and below 2.5uVrms. The same document gives maximum output at several loads: the balanced output reaches 167mW at 150 ohms and 165mW at 33 ohms, while the single-ended output reaches 42mW and 120mW at those loads, with all maximum-output ratings specified at THD+N below -90dB [2]. These data cover distortion, noise, dynamic range, and load-dependent output power, but omit frequency response, IMD, crosstalk, and output impedance. No credible independent laboratory measurement of the Tea Pro was found as of the review date, so the strong manufacturer results remain unverified.

## Technology Level

$$ \Large \text{0.7} $$

Tea Pro combines a current Qualcomm QCC5181 Bluetooth platform, XMOS XU316 USB controller, and ESS ES9039Q2M DAC with separate digital and analog board sections, dedicated power and volume-control circuitry, and 32 precision resistors in a compact battery-powered chassis [1][2]. The integrated 19-magnet mounting system, dual headphone outputs, display, microphone, and wired/wireless operation demonstrate meaningful product-level integration and refinement of KHADAS's earlier Tea concept. The main semiconductors and circuit techniques are commercially available, however, and no patent or other durable barrier to copying the magnetic format is disclosed. Its sophistication therefore lies in compact integration and implementation rather than exclusive core technology.

## Cost-Performance

$$ \Large \text{1.0} $$

Tea Pro is currently 199 USD through both the manufacturer's store and Amazon.com [3][5]. Because Tea Pro lacks independent measurements, the performance comparison is provisional and uses the manufacturers' specifications [2][6]. HiBy W4 is a relevant lower-priced candidate at 99 USD and offers the same broad wired/Bluetooth category, 3.5mm and 4.4mm outputs, a display, and modern codecs [6]. It is not a full functional equivalent: its stated battery life is 6.3 hours through 3.5mm and 5 hours through 4.4mm, versus Tea Pro's ratings of no less than 11 hours over Bluetooth AAC and no less than 8 hours over USB-C at 44.1kHz, and HiBy presents magnetic attachment as a separate W4+ backplate rather than an integrated feature of the base unit [1][2][6]. Because these are material portable-use functions, W4 is excluded from the equivalent-product set. No lower-priced current product with the complete Tea Pro function set was identified.

## Reliability & Support

$$ \Large \text{0.4} $$

KHADAS's warranty table gives Tea a 12-month warranty, and the returns policy states that paid support may be offered after warranty expiry [4]. Remedies depend on whether the purchase was made through the official store or a distributor, and the company does not publish Tea Pro-specific repair prices, parts-availability periods, RMA or failure-rate data, or a global repair-center network. The integrated battery, display, connectors, and magnetic/leather construction create several wear or replacement points, but no recurring Tea Pro-specific failure pattern was found. With a short warranty and no long-term field record or detailed repairability commitment, support is adequate but not strong.

## Rationality of Design Philosophy

$$ \Large \text{0.8} $$

The design allocates resources to useful portable functions: magnetic phone mounting, a battery that supports both wired and wireless use, dual outputs, a display, hardware volume control, seven EQ presets, and a microphone. Standard high-volume controller and converter chips, compact board integration, and shared wired/wireless hardware are reasonable ways to control cost and size. KHADAS also publishes unusually detailed distortion, noise, dynamic-range, and output-power figures with test conditions [1][2]. The official presentation nevertheless turns component specifications into unsupported audible claims, including that the product rivals a professional studio, that its clock eliminates note smearing, and that precision resistors smooth high frequencies and increase clarity [1]. Those claims go beyond the published evidence, but they do not negate the otherwise functional, data-backed allocation of hardware.

## Advice

Tea Pro is a strong fit for listeners who value a phone-mounted form factor, both wired and Bluetooth operation, 3.5mm and 4.4mm outputs, a display, and longer portable battery life in one device. Its manufacturer measurements indicate low distortion and noise, but buyers who require independently verified performance should wait for a credible laboratory test. HiBy W4 costs substantially less if the shorter stated battery life and separate magnetic backplate are acceptable. The Tea Pro's 12-month warranty and limited public repair information should also be considered for long-term ownership.
