## Overview

The Yamaha RX-V4A is a 5.2-channel AV receiver introduced in 2020, positioned as an entry-level model in Yamaha's home theater receiver lineup beneath the RX-V6A and AVENTAGE series. Yamaha rates it at 80W per channel (8 ohms, 20Hz-20kHz, 0.06% THD, two channels driven). It combines HDMI connectivity supporting 8K/60Hz and 4K/120Hz passthrough, VRR and ALLM with CINEMA DSP 3D surround processing and YPAO automatic room calibration. Wireless features include Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, AirPlay 2 and MusicCast multi-room streaming [1][2].

## Scientific Validity

$$ \Large \text{0.6} $$

Manufacturer specifications state an S/N Ratio of 110dB (IHF-A weighting, Pure Direct mode), Crosstalk of 70dB at 1kHz and 50dB at 10kHz, and a Frequency Response of 10Hz-100kHz (+0/-3dB, Pure Direct mode) [1][2]. The S/N Ratio and 1kHz Crosstalk figures are strong, while the wider frequency-response tolerance and weaker 10kHz separation prevent a higher assessment. These are manufacturer specifications rather than independent bench results, so the model's distortion, noise and multichannel behavior under load remain unverified.

## Technology Level

$$ \Large \text{0.5} $$

The RX-V4A integrates Yamaha's High Slew Rate Amplifier circuit, CINEMA DSP 3D surround processing, YPAO automatic room calibration and Compressed Music Enhancer in a single in-house receiver platform [1]. This demonstrates established system-integration and DSP know-how, but the underlying functions are mature and competing receivers offer comparable amplification, surround decoding and room-correction capabilities. The implementation is therefore competent rather than technically distinctive by current standards.

## Cost-Performance

$$ \Large \text{1.0} $$

The Yamaha RX-V4A is currently listed new from 403.20 USD [3]. The 399.95 USD Yamaha RX300A was the cheapest lower-priced candidate located by a function-only search, but its official comparison table lists no Wi-Fi, Ethernet, network streaming or DTS:X support [4]. The RX500A adds those functions but costs 599.95 USD [4]. No cheaper current receiver or finished-product bundle was confirmed to match the RX-V4A's network playback, AirPlay 2, MusicCast, surround-format, HDMI and room-correction functions together with its manufacturer-specified audio performance. The RX-V4A is therefore the cheapest confirmed equivalent option and its CP score is 1.0.

## Reliability & Support

$$ \Large \text{0.5} $$

Yamaha's U.S. warranty covers receivers for two years, parts and labor, for the original owner when purchased from an authorized U.S. or Puerto Rico dealer; coverage is non-transferable and service is handled through authorized centers [5]. Firmware support continued into March 2026, but Yamaha's downloads page lists version 1.78 while its linked updater is titled version 1.80 and says units at 1.80 or later are current, leaving the official version information inconsistent [6][7]. More importantly, affected RX-V4A units could not pass some Xbox Series X and NVIDIA RTX 30-series 4K/120Hz signals without a hardware update; Yamaha offered a free HDMI-board exchange for 24 months and documented an eARC workaround [8]. Yamaha also withdrew previously announced QMS support because the installed HDMI IC could not support the revised HDMI 2.1a specification [9]. Continued firmware and authorized repair support are positives, but the hardware remedy and withdrawn feature keep the overall assessment at an average level. No model-specific failure-rate or MTBF data are available.

## Rationality of Design Philosophy

$$ \Large \text{0.7} $$

The RX-V4A follows a cost-effective, feature-driven design philosophy. It combines multichannel amplification, HDMI switching, network playback, DSP surround decoding and automatic room correction in one chassis, directing its complexity toward functions that users can employ directly [1][2]. Compared with the RX-V385 generation, the design adds a second subwoofer output, higher-bandwidth video and gaming features, and MusicCast network streaming. This functional progression and practical integration support the above-average assessment; the design does not depend on claims about exotic materials or scientifically implausible audible effects.

## Advice

The Yamaha RX-V4A offers a well-rounded 5.2-channel home-theater package at a current new-listing price of 403.20 USD. No cheaper option was confirmed to equal all of its network, video, room-correction and manufacturer-specified audio performance, so it receives the maximum cost-performance score. Its published S/N Ratio, Crosstalk and Frequency Response figures indicate a strong baseline, though independent bench verification is still desirable. CINEMA DSP, YPAO and the High Slew Rate Amplifier are mature technologies rather than lasting differentiators. Buyers considering an early-production or used unit should establish whether Yamaha's HDMI-board remedy was completed; they should also not expect the QMS support that Yamaha originally announced [8][9].
