## Overview

The Yamaha A-S3200 is the flagship model of Yamaha's A-S series integrated amplifiers, released in May 2020 as the successor to the A-S3000 [1]. It is built around Yamaha's proprietary Floating and Balanced Power Amplifier circuit, a design carried across multiple generations of Yamaha's A-S series [1]. The amplifier is rated at 100W/channel into 8Ω and 150W/channel into 4Ω, and provides two balanced XLR inputs, four unbalanced line inputs, and an MM/MC phono stage. The current US MSRP is 8,999.95 USD [1].

## Scientific Validity

$$ \Large \text{0.7} $$

Stereophile's line-stage graphs are essentially flat from 20Hz–20kHz, with about ±0.15dB variation into its simulated loudspeaker load [2]. THD+N is roughly 0.005–0.006% through much of the 8Ω power sweep before clipping near 110W, and roughly 0.007–0.011% into 4Ω before clipping near 168W. At 15W/8Ω and 1kHz it measures 0.009%, with third harmonic dominant; the 1kHz intermodulation difference product is about -96dB [2]. THD+N rises with frequency to approximately 0.015–0.02% into 8Ω and 0.03% into 4Ω near 20kHz. Line crosstalk exceeds 100dB below 2kHz and remains 80dB at the top of the audioband. Stereophile reports line S/N of 70dB unweighted, 78.5dB over the audioband, and 82dB A-weighted, all referenced to 2.83V output at maximum volume [2].

SoundStage tested both channels with an Audio Precision APx555 B after 30 minutes at 10W/8Ω; most tests used the balanced input, 1kHz, 10W/8Ω, and a 10Hz–90kHz measurement bandwidth [3]. It measured 102W/channel into 8Ω, 169W/channel into 4Ω, 10kHz crosstalk of -93/-110dB, damping factors of 289/291 at 1kHz, and full-power A-weighted S/N of 111.9/112.8dB. Its 20Hz–20kHz S/N results were 110.3/107.3dB, THD stayed below 0.0085/0.0082%, and A-weighted THD+N remained below 0.0099/0.0096% under the table's stated conditions [3]. The higher SoundStage S/N figures use a full-power reference and therefore are not directly interchangeable with Stereophile's 2.83V-reference results. The phono stage is less exact: RIAA response rises by roughly 0.4–0.55dB at the frequency extremes, and Stereophile measured MM S/N at 88dB A-weighted while MC mode was about 9–10dB noisier [2][3]. Line performance is unlikely to add audible distortion or response error in ordinary use, but the lower-level S/N results, MC noise, phono-response error, and high-frequency distortion prevent a higher assessment.

## Technology Level

$$ \Large \text{0.4} $$

The A-S3200's core technical distinction is Yamaha's in-house Floating and Balanced Power Amplifier topology, refined across multiple A-S generations and therefore evidence of design ownership and accumulated implementation knowledge [1]. Neither Yamaha's product documentation nor the reviewed technical sources identify a product-linked patent. The topology is mature rather than a recent advance, and comparable present-day amplifiers attain similar measured output and fidelity, so it does not establish a durable technical barrier. The product is also an analog circuit/mechanical design without digital or software integration. PC-Triple C internal wiring and the Mechanical Ground Concept add construction detail, but no controlled result establishes them as difficult-to-copy technical achievements [1]. The combination of in-house expertise and mature, readily matched analog implementation supports a slightly below-average technology assessment.

## Cost-Performance

$$ \Large \text{1.0} $$

The A-S3200's representative current US price is 8,999.95 USD [6]. The closest current one-box candidate, the Luxman L-507Z at 10,495 USD [4][7], matches or exceeds its two balanced and four unbalanced line inputs, MM/MC phono, pre-out/main-in, speaker A/B switching, headphone outputs, remote control, meters, and tone/balance controls. SoundStage measured the L-507Z with the same APx555 B platform: its 135W/8Ω and 222W/4Ω output and 355/314 damping factors are higher, but its 20Hz–20kHz full-rated-power S/N of 102.5/102.6dB, 10kHz crosstalk of -65.4/-75.0dB, and worst-channel THD of 0.0165% are inferior to the A-S3200's 110.3/107.3dB, -93/-110dB, and 0.0085/0.0082% results [3][5]. The L-507Z therefore does not meet equal-or-better measured performance and is also more expensive in the US. No lower-priced product was confirmed to match all of the A-S3200's user functions and measured performance, so it remains the cheapest confirmed equivalent option and CP = 1.0.

## Reliability & Support

$$ \Large \text{0.7} $$

In the US, Yamaha currently lists the A-S3200 for a registration-based additional 3-year warranty beyond its standard coverage [8]. Yamaha maintains manufacturer support across major regions, but the additional term is regional and buyers should confirm the coverage attached to their place of purchase. No statistical failure-rate or MTBF data, product-specific systemic fault, or unusually long parts-support commitment was identified. Its integrated power-amplifier, phono, switching, and analog-control architecture is not inherently simple, and field data do not show it to be unusually fragile or robust.

## Rationality of Design Philosophy

$$ \Large \text{0.1} $$

The A-S3200 combines preamplification, power amplification, MM/MC phono gain, two balanced inputs, unbalanced inputs, a headphone output, speaker A/B switching, remote control, and tone/balance controls in one unit [1]. These functions serve identifiable analog-system needs, but their integration is conventional rather than advanced.

Yamaha's design narrative mixes objective circuit descriptions with subjective themes such as "Emotion," "Openness," and "Groove," and describes selected capacitors in terms of "musical performance" [1]. PC-Triple C wiring, elaborate mechanical grounding, heavy structural parts, premium exterior materials, and illuminated meters receive substantial design emphasis without controlled evidence that they improve audible fidelity. The analog-only architecture omits DSP and digital/network functions without establishing a compensating user or measurement advantage. Its useful integrated controls keep the product functional, but the unsupported audibility language, material-intensive cost allocation, and conservative architecture make the underlying design direction poorly rationalized.

## Advice

The A-S3200's very low line-stage distortion, flat response, useful power, and broad analog controls are confirmed by two independent bench-test sources. It suits buyers who specifically need two balanced inputs, integrated MM/MC phono, speaker A/B switching, and physical tone/balance controls in one chassis. Its topology is long-established and it offers no digital connectivity or DSP, while several material and sound-quality claims lack controlled support; buyers should separate those claims from the confirmed bench performance.
