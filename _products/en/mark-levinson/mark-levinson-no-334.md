## Overview

The Mark Levinson No.334 is a discontinued stereo power amplifier developed by Madrigal Audio Laboratories and reviewed in 1999 [1]. It uses a fully balanced dual-mono design and is rated at 125, 250, and 500 W per channel into 8, 4, and 2 ohms [2]. User-facing connections include RCA and XLR audio inputs, remote turn-on triggers, Mark Levinson Communication Links, and two bidirectional PHAST-compatible automation ports [1]. The chassis weighs 50.9 kg. With no current new-product channel identified, the price basis is a live used listing at 2,499 USD, posted April 13 and updated May 18, 2026 [8].

## Scientific Validity

$$ \Large \text{0.6} $$

Stereophile's independent measurements found an output impedance of 0.014-0.022 ohm, essentially flat response through the audio band and to approximately 100 kHz, and channel separation described as irrelevant in practice [3]. At 1% THD+N with both channels driven, continuous output was approximately 139 W per channel into 8 ohms and 240-243 W per channel into 4 ohms; one channel produced 430 W into 2 ohms, while burst output reached 492 W into 2 ohms [5]. These results demonstrate strong load-driving capability.

The distortion results are not uniformly low across conditions. The small-signal THD+N-versus-frequency graph is approximately 0.015% through much of the low and mid band, then rises with frequency and load to roughly 0.02-0.1% around 10 kHz [4]. A separate 50 Hz spectrum at 167.5 W into 4 ohms places each visible harmonic below 0.01%, but that result does not establish THD+N below 0.01% across all frequencies and loads. The 19+20 kHz IMD results were 0.025% at 67.7 W into 8 ohms and 0.014% at 125.5 W into 4 ohms [4].

Noise is the clearest weakness. Referenced to 1 W into 8 ohms, S/N ratio measured 79.2 dB unweighted over 22 Hz-22 kHz, 81.6 dB A-weighted, and 58.1 dB over 10 Hz-500 kHz [3]. Stereophile attributed much of the wider-band result to energy above 80 kHz, so it should not be treated as an audible-band figure. Even so, the strong response, low output impedance, separation, and power delivery are offset by marginal audio-band noise and frequency/load-dependent distortion, leaving a capable output stage but non-leading overall fidelity.

## Technology Level

$$ \Large \text{0.4} $$

Madrigal designed the No.334 in-house using circuitry derived from the No.33/No.33H monoblocks, demonstrating substantial analog-amplifier expertise [1]. Its remote triggers, Communication Links, and PHAST ports also provided useful control-system integration for its period. Against current technology, however, the balanced dual-mono Class-AB architecture, adaptive biasing, and soft clipping are mature and readily reproduced. The cited material identifies no product-specific patent or technical barrier that remains difficult to match. A cyanate-ester circuit board said to cost ten times as much as FR4 and plastic-cased output transistors promoted as less microphonic are technically distinctive construction choices, but no controlled evidence establishes a corresponding audible advantage [1]. The design shows genuine accumulated know-how, yet its remaining differentiation is limited and cost-intensive.

## Cost-Performance

$$ \Large \text{1.0} $$

The current price basis is the live 2,499 USD used listing [8]. The closest cheaper modern candidate found is a stereo pair of Buckeye Hypex NCx500 monoblocks at 895 USD each, or 1,790 USD before any input-adapter cost [6]. Independent testing of the same NCx500/SMPS1200 platform measured 0.000220% THD+N and 113.153 dB SINAD at 5 W into 4 ohms, 116.120 dB S/N ratio at 5 W, 723.7 W into 2 ohms at 1% THD+N, and little load dependency [7]. It is clearly superior in noise, distortion, and output capability.

The Buckeye pair is nevertheless not functionally equivalent. It provides XLR input, automatic signal sensing, and 12 V triggering, but not the No.334's Mark Levinson Communication Link or two bidirectional PHAST-compatible ports [1][6]. Its measured response is also approximately -0.267 dB at 20 kHz [7], numerically below the No.334's flatter audio-band response. RCA-to-XLR adaptation can address source connection but cannot reproduce the proprietary automation interfaces. No cheaper finished product or verified accessory bundle was identified that preserves every user-facing function and provides equivalent-or-better documented performance on all axes. The No.334 is therefore the cheapest identified complete option for this exact function set, so the cost-performance score is 1.0.

## Reliability & Support

$$ \Large \text{0.4} $$

The No.334 originally carried a 5-year warranty when registered, but coverage for all production units has long expired [1]. The reviewed sources do not confirm current factory repair availability for this discontinued model. They also provide no model-specific failure-rate, RMA, or lifespan statistics, so neither above-average reliability nor a product-specific failure mode can be established. The live listing states that its unit meets factory specifications, but one seller's statement cannot establish fleet reliability [8]. Given the unit's age and 50.9 kg construction, buyers should verify service history and obtain an inspection or repair estimate; automatic capacitor replacement should not be assumed without unit-level diagnosis.

## Rationality of Design Philosophy

$$ \Large \text{0.1} $$

The No.334 delivers real functional value through high output, low output impedance, broad load capability, and extensive automation interfaces. Its design narrative nevertheless assigns audible significance to a cyanate-ester circuit board and less-microphonic plastic transistor packages without controlled audibility evidence [1]. The amplifier also consumes 260 W at idle and 110 W in standby and weighs 50.9 kg [2]. Those costs are difficult to justify when the measured result still includes marginal S/N ratio and rising high-frequency distortion.

The product's useful performance and control integration prevent it from being dismissed as nonfunctional, but its development direction invested heavily in material quantity and unverified audibility claims rather than measurement efficiency. Modern amplification can exceed its noise, distortion, and power results with substantially less hardware and energy, even though the No.334's proprietary legacy automation functions complicate complete replacement. The philosophy is therefore scientifically and economically weak despite several objectively strong output-stage results.

## Advice

The No.334 remains capable when its balanced/unbalanced inputs, very low output impedance, high current delivery, and legacy Mark Levinson/PHAST automation are specifically required. Its measured noise and high-frequency distortion are not competitive with current high-performance amplification, and a live used price of 2,499 USD must be considered together with an individual unit's service history. Buyers should verify all channels, control ports, and protection behavior and obtain a service estimate rather than presuming either failure or restoration needs from age alone.

Users who do not need the proprietary automation ports should compare modern NCx500-based amplification, which offers substantially better independently measured noise, distortion, and power performance [6][7]. That alternative is not used as the formal cost-performance comparator because it does not reproduce the complete function set or every measured axis.
