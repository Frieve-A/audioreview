## Overview

The JBL 4369 is a passive 2-way floorstanding loudspeaker and the flagship of JBL's Studio Monitor series. It combines a 15-inch Differential Drive woofer, a D2830B compression driver and a Sonoglass HDI waveguide [1][2]. External amplification is required. Each cabinet is 630mm wide and weighs 63.5kg; the published overall dimensions differ between the specification sheet and the white-paper drawing, so installation clearances require confirmation [2][3].

## Scientific Validity

$$ \Large \text{0.5} $$

Manufacturer specifications are 28Hz–25kHz at -6dB, 93dB sensitivity at 2.83V/1m, 6Ω nominal impedance and an 800Hz crossover. The 350W RMS figure is maximum recommended amplifier power, not measured acoustic output [1][3].

JBL's September 2026 white paper supplies acoustic measurements. With neutral trims, the axial and listening-window responses show several-decibel variation, while horizontal coverage is approximately 100° through much of the mid/high band and narrows at the highest frequencies. The normalized 80–112dB curves largely overlap above roughly 40Hz, with more compression at lower frequencies. This supports substantial output headroom, not a claim of flat response or zero distortion throughout the band [2].

At 8.949V input, described as approximately 103dB, the plotted second and third harmonics together correspond to roughly 0.5% around 1kHz; the second harmonic alone reaches approximately 2% around 50–60Hz. These are approximate readings after removing the graph's 20dB harmonic-display offset, not an all-harmonic THD measurement. The paper does not fully specify the sweep distance and duration, and independent 4369 measurements were not located. Output stability is promising, but the response variation and distortion prevent a conclusion of uniformly high acoustic accuracy [2].

## Technology Level

$$ \Large \text{0.6} $$

Patented Differential Drive and HDI implementations, developed in-house with the D2830B driver, demonstrate substantial driver and waveguide expertise [2][3]. Dual voice coils, opposing suspension elements, FEA-optimized magnetic parts and the driver–waveguide interface are concrete electromechanical engineering. These refine established principles. The passive crossover uses MultiCap components, and separate front-panel HF/UHF trims adjust overlapping upper-frequency bands by -1 to +1dB in 0.5dB steps [1][2]. This is differentiated acoustic engineering, although its integration remains limited to mechanical and analog elements.

## Cost-Performance

$$ \Large \text{1.0} $$

The price basis is 22,382.29 USD per pair, converted from a current tax-inclusive Japanese retail listing using the 21 September 2026 ECB rates [4][10]. The retailer lists free domestic shipping, but ships only within Japan. A US retail or landed import price was not confirmed; this is an overseas reference price, with delivery timing and any installation service requiring confirmation.

Equivalence requires stereo reproduction, suitable amplifier connections including the separate input terminals, independent high-frequency adjustment, and comparable measured response, coverage, distortion and output headroom. A frequency-range specification alone does not establish this. Measured alternatives examined include the Neumann KH 420 and Arendal 1528 Monitor 8. The KH 420 has a smoother measured response and low midrange distortion, but its independent test reaches only 108dB at 10% THD averaged over 50–100Hz. The Arendal's published compression test stops at 102dB, and its distortion plots at 96dB; neither establishes parity with all of the 4369 results [7][9].

Adding EQ can provide the tonal controls, while subwoofers and a crossover can relieve bass demands. However, a KH 420 with one RSL Speedwoofer 12S per channel is insufficient: the subwoofer's measured full-space output is around 100–102dB over 40–100Hz at maximum input, with roughly 10% distortion through much of that region [8]. Additional subwoofers, signal routing and input adapters would require a more extensive system, and do not establish the remaining full-band equivalence merely by being added.

No cheaper complete system meeting the functions and measured performance was established, including these combinations. The 4369 is therefore the least expensive confirmed equivalent option in this comparison, giving a provisional CP of 1.0. This conclusion is limited by the available comparative measurements and the overseas price basis.

## Reliability & Support

$$ \Large \text{0.7} $$

The Japanese retailer's 4369 listing specifies a five-year manufacturer warranty [5]. The passive design avoids an onboard amplifier, power supply and battery, reducing the number of subsystems that can fail; the cast driver frame and internally braced enclosure support a favorable structural assessment [2][3]. Japanese warranty coverage requires the applicable domestic warranty documentation; direct imports can have different repair conditions [6]. These are warranty and construction advantages, not a measured failure-rate claim. Model-specific worldwide repair coverage, parts-retention commitments, repair charges and long-term failure statistics remain unconfirmed.

## Rationality of Design Philosophy

$$ \Large \text{0.6} $$

Controlling driver motion, acoustic radiation and cabinet resonances addresses real loudspeaker behavior. The magnetic-system optimization, waveguide and internal bracing therefore direct engineering effort toward useful acoustic performance. Separate frequency trims support basic tonal adjustment, while passive operation permits independent choice of amplification and external processing [1][2]. These trims are not full room correction. MultiCap's lower electrical losses are a plausible goal, but component-selection claims about superior sound require more than premium materials. Likewise, mechanical isolation can reduce vibration transmission; the claim that isolation feet reduce floor reflections conflates this with airborne sound reflection, which the feet do not control [1][3].

## Advice

Check floor loading, delivery arrangements and installation dimensions before buying: the specification sheet lists 1120.2mm height, whereas the white-paper drawing shows 1156.6mm including feet [2][3]. Choose an amplifier suitable for the impedance curve, which falls to roughly 2.5–3Ω in the bass despite the 6Ω nominal rating [2]. Confirm the actual seller's warranty and delivered price. The wide horizontal coverage and output capability are useful for demanding rooms, but placement and room acoustics still determine the response at the listening seats.
