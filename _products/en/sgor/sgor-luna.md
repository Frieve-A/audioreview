## Overview

The SGOR LUNA is a 13.5mm planar magnetic in-ear monitor that became publicly available in 2024. It is distributed globally through Linsoul Audio at 38 USD in its base configuration (3.5mm, no microphone), with USB-C and microphone variants available at 38–44 USD. SGOR has an official product page, while retail sales, warranty terms, and online support for the referenced listing are administered by Linsoul Audio [1][2].

## Scientific Validity

$$ \Large \text{0.5} $$

The manufacturer specifies a frequency response range of 7 Hz – 40 kHz without a ±dB deviation figure, which does not permit evaluation of target curve deviation [1]. No accessible third-party measurements of frequency response, THD, S/N ratio, or passive isolation were found from any credible independent source, and scientific validity cannot be assessed with the currently available information.

## Technology Level

$$ \Large \text{0.2} $$

The SGOR LUNA employs a dual-sided 7+7 N52 neodymium magnet array, a microfabrication-etched planar diaphragm, and a passive acoustic filter cavity marketed as "Spatial Harmonic Resonance." Each of these elements was already established practice in passive planar IEMs before the product's 2024 availability [2]. No public patent documentation was found; the "in-house developed" driver claim is unverifiable from public sources [2]. The product is a fully passive analog device with no digital integration, software, or DSP components. Competitive catch-up requires no significant time or investment given the commoditized underlying technology, and no differentiating IP or novel engineering has been identified.

## Cost-Performance

$$ \Large \text{0.4} $$

This site evaluates based solely on functionality and measured performance values, without considering driver types or configurations.

The SGOR LUNA (3.5mm, no mic) is priced at 38 USD [1]. The cheapest product identified with equivalent or better user-facing functions and documented measured performance is the CCA CRA (without mic) at 14 USD [3].

The CCA CRA provides equivalent essential functions: passive IEM with 3.5mm output, detachable cable, no microphone, no ANC, and no DSP. On measured performance:

- **Frequency Response:** CCA CRA has been measured by headphones.com using an IEC-711 clone microphone, with the published graph documenting its frequency-response trace under that rig [4]. The SGOR LUNA has no accessible frequency response data; no metric establishes the SGOR LUNA superior on this axis (provisionally equivalent).
- **THD, S/N Ratio, Passive Isolation:** Published data is absent for both products — no metric disqualifies the CCA CRA.

This comparison is provisional and should be revised if independent measurements of the SGOR LUNA become available.

CP = 14 USD / 38 USD = 0.368, rounded to 0.4.

## Reliability & Support

$$ \Large \text{0.5} $$

The SGOR LUNA is a fully passive IEM with a single planar driver and no active components, limiting active-electronics failure modes. The user-replaceable cable via standard 0.78mm 2-pin connector further reduces maintenance exposure. Against that, the warranty is 1 year for the IEM and 3 months for the cable, administered through authorized distributor Linsoul [1]. Linsoul provides online support for the listing, and SGOR also has an official product page, but no statistical reliability or failure-rate data is available for this product [1][2].

## Rationality of Design Philosophy

$$ \Large \text{0.3} $$

The SGOR LUNA's core design approach — planar transduction with passive resonator-based frequency response shaping — is physically rational and avoids pseudoscientific claims in the transducer design [1][2]. However, by 2024, passive planar IEMs using comparable magnet-array and etched-diaphragm concepts were already available; SGOR's public materials do not identify novel engineering, tuning methodology, manufacturing innovation, or IP. Additionally, the manufacturer promotes audible benefits from the star-twisted silver-plated oxygen-free copper cable construction, a claim without scientific basis at the signal levels involved in IEM use [1]. These points support a low rationality score.

## Advice

Prospective buyers should be aware that no accessible third-party measurements exist for the SGOR LUNA as of this review date, making it impossible to independently verify frequency response accuracy, distortion, or noise performance. The CCA CRA at 14 USD provides identical passive IEM functionality with a documented frequency-response trace on an IEC-711 clone microphone [4], at 37% of the SGOR LUNA's price [3]. Without performance evidence establishing the SGOR LUNA's superiority, buyers seeking this functionality are better served by the CCA CRA. Should future third-party measurements demonstrate a clear performance advantage for the SGOR LUNA, the cost-performance picture may change.
