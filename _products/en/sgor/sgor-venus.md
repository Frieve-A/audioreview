## Overview

The SGOR Venus is a low-priced wired in-ear monitor released in November 2022, priced at 19.99 USD. SGOR distributes exclusively through Linsoul Audio and associated global retailers and has no independently accessible official brand website or published technical documentation. The Venus features a single dynamic driver, a semi-open acoustic cavity, and a detachable 0.78mm 2-pin cable with a 3.5mm plug [1].

No independent third-party measurements are available for this product, and Scientific Validity cannot be evaluated due to insufficient data.

## Technology Level

$$ \Large \text{0.1} $$

The Venus employs exclusively commodity technologies. The single dynamic-driver architecture is a mature commodity approach and offers no distinguishing implementation by 2026 standards. The semi-open acoustic cavity and 0.78mm 2-pin detachable cable are identically standard across the industry. No patents or proprietary technology of any kind have been identified. The claim of ergonomic shell geometry derived from "3D auricle sampling" is an unverified marketing assertion with no published methodology or dataset [1]. The product is an entirely passive analog device with no digital, software, or computational integration. There is no technical implementation that competitors could not replicate immediately, and none that other manufacturers would have reason to adopt.

## Cost-Performance

$$ \Large \text{0.6} $$

The cost-performance comparison below is based on user-facing functions and documented performance, not on driver type or configuration.

The SGOR Venus is priced at 19.99 USD (Linsoul Audio, without microphone) [1]. The Moondrop Quarks (No MIC), at 12.99 USD (Linsoul Audio) [3], provides equivalent user-facing functions: wired in-ear audio playback via 3.5mm single-ended connection with no DSP, no wireless, and no app control. The Quarks has published third-party frequency-response and THD measurement graphs from SoundStage Network using a GRAS Model 43AG ear/cheek simulator and Audiomatica Clio 12 QC [2], plus a manufacturer THD specification of less than 1% at 1kHz [3]; the SGOR Venus has no published frequency response deviation figure and specifies only the qualitative claim "lower THD" with no numeric value. This comparison is provisional, as the SGOR Venus has no independent measurements or manufacturer audio quality specifications available to establish a direct numeric baseline.

CP = 12.99 USD / 19.99 USD = 0.6498, rounded to 0.6.

## Reliability & Support

$$ \Large \text{0.5} $$

The Venus is a passive analog device with a single dynamic driver in a resin shell and no active electronic components [1] — a construction that is inherently robust and not subject to electronic failure. The warranty period is one year for the IEM unit and three months for the cable, which is below the standard two-year period and reflects particularly limited cable coverage [1]. All after-sales support is routed through Linsoul Audio as SGOR's sole authorized global distributor; no manufacturer-direct support infrastructure or website exists. No statistical failure rate or longevity data is available for this brand.

## Rationality of Design Philosophy

$$ \Large \text{0.4} $$

The Venus presents a conventional low-priced IEM without pseudoscientific claims. The single dynamic driver and semi-open cavity are functional engineering choices for a passive wired IEM, and the design does not add active electronics or expensive accessories unrelated to the basic playback function [1]. However, the manufacturer publishes no measurements to substantiate any of its performance claims, while the product page emphasizes listening-experience and bass/vocal marketing claims rather than measured accuracy [1]. The ergonomic "3D auricle sampling" claim has no published supporting data or methodology [1]. The product lineup progression from Venus to Adonis to Luna reflects driver technology experimentation with no measurable performance trajectory available for assessment. The overall design approach is entirely conventional, applying commoditized and readily available patterns with no novel engineering direction.

## Advice

Buyers considering the SGOR Venus at 19.99 USD should be aware that no independent acoustic measurements are publicly available for this product, making objective pre-purchase performance assessment impossible. The Moondrop Quarks is available at 12.99 USD [3] with published third-party frequency response measurements from SoundStage Network [2], providing a documentably equivalent function set at a lower price. The Venus carries no measurable technical advantage over alternatives with established measurement records, and buyers who prioritize verifiable acoustic performance are better served by products with published independent data.
