## Overview

The ONIX Waltz XM10 Ltd is an Android 13 digital audio player launched in January 2025. ONIX is a British audio brand founded in 1979 whose portable products are manufactured by Shanling (Zhuhai, China) under an official licensing arrangement. The XM10 Ltd upgrades the standard XM10 with AKM's AK4499EX + AK4191 DAC system, a proprietary "Brighton" I/V conversion circuit derived from the ONIX Mystic XP1 desktop reference player, 877mW balanced output power, and an open Android 13 platform on a 6-inch display housed in an 11.5mm ultra-slim stainless steel chassis [1]. It retains physical compatibility with the ONIX Miracle Stack modular system (XP10 DAC/amp + XPS10 power supply). Current US market price is 899 USD [2].

## Scientific Validity

$$ \Large \text{0.8} $$

Manufacturer-specified audio performance indicates excellent results across all applicable metrics [1]. THD+N is 0.0009% via the 3.5mm SE output and 0.0007% via the 4.4mm balanced output, demonstrating excellent distortion performance on both outputs. SNR reaches 122 dB (SE) and 124 dB (balanced), with dynamic range matching those figures identically. Channel separation is 72 dB (SE) and 100 dB (balanced); the balanced output achieves excellent stereo channel isolation while the SE output is acceptable. Frequency response is specified at 20Hz–40kHz (−0.5 dB), meeting the full audible band with excellent deviation control. No independent third-party measurements are available to verify these figures, so a conservative evaluation applies.

## Technology Level

$$ \Large \text{0.4} $$

The XM10 Ltd presents a mixed technology profile. The AKM AK4499EX + AK4191 two-chip DAC combination is a strong commercial DAC solution, but it is an off-the-shelf implementation rather than documented ONIX-exclusive technology [1]. The Qualcomm Snapdragon 665 is an older mobile processor than current Snapdragon 680-based DAP platforms such as the FiiO M21, so the computing platform does not represent current portable-player hardware [3]. The proprietary "Brighton" I/V conversion circuit uses commercially available audio components, with no documented patents or independent measurements found to distinguish its output from standard implementations. The XMOS XU316 USB interface and AD8397 headphone amplifier are established industry-standard components [1]. The genuine technology advance lies in software and connectivity integration: open Android 13 with full Google Play access, dual-band Wi-Fi, DLNA, AirPlay 2, LDAC bidirectional Bluetooth 5.0, OTA firmware updates, and Miracle Stack expandability represent a modern computer/cloud-integrated architecture that merits recognition. The older SoC and the replicability of all core components off the shelf constrain the overall technology standing.

## Cost-Performance

$$ \Large \text{0.4} $$

The ONIX XM10 Ltd is priced at 899 USD [2]. The FiiO M21 at 370 USD [4] is the cheapest identified current Android 13 DAP with broadly equivalent user-facing playback functions and equal-or-better manufacturer-specified core audio metrics when its Super High Gain mode is included. It provides open Android 13, dual-band Wi-Fi with DLNA and AirPlay, two-way LDAC Bluetooth, USB DAC mode, USB audio output, 3.5mm and 4.4mm headphone/line outputs, microSD expansion, and OTA firmware updating [3][4]. Its 950mW 4.4mm Super High Gain mode requires external USB-C power; battery-only 4.4mm high-gain output is 720mW, so maximum-output parity depends on accepting that powered operating condition [3][4].

## Reliability & Support

$$ \Large \text{0.4} $$

The XM10 Ltd carries a 1-year manufacturer warranty covering manufacturing defects on the DAP unit; accessories are explicitly excluded [2]. Support is distributor-based, and Linsoul Audio lists online support availability Monday–Friday, 09:00–18:00 UTC+8 [2]. Firmware updating is supported through the Android platform, but no public long-term update schedule or out-of-warranty repair pathway is documented in the cited sources. No widespread hardware failures or service bulletins are documented as of the review date, though the product's limited production history makes long-term reliability assessment inconclusive. As a complex Android device with an integrated lithium battery, long-term hardware longevity is subject to battery degradation over the product's lifespan.

## Rationality of Design Philosophy

$$ \Large \text{0.7} $$

The XM10 Ltd follows a rational, scientifically grounded design direction. The architecture is fully solid-state and digital throughout, without vacuum tubes or R2R ladder DAC implementation, and no pseudoscientific audio claims are made. The product provides measurable advancement over its predecessor: the DAC system was upgraded from a single AK4493SEQ to the AK4499EX + AK4191 combination, a proprietary I/V conversion circuit and FPGA were added, balanced output power nearly doubled to 877mW, and the platform moved to Android 13 [1]. The open Android 13 environment with full streaming app access, DLNA, AirPlay 2, and LDAC represents advanced functional integration that provides tangible user advantages over closed-OS alternatives [1]. The dedicated DAP form factor has practical justification: high sustained output power, an independent battery, large local storage (128GB plus microSD expansion), and modular expandability via the Miracle Stack offer real operational benefits that a smartphone plus dongle configuration does not consistently replicate [1]. Core costs are directed toward measurable performance components. Two limiting factors: the "British sound character" and "natural, rich and refined soundstage" marketing language constitutes subjective, unverifiable claims [1]; and a portion of product cost reflects brand heritage and form factor aesthetics rather than exclusively function-optimized investment.

## Advice

The ONIX XM10 Ltd is a technically capable Android 13 DAP with manufacturer-specified audio performance pointing to excellent results, 877mW balanced output, full streaming app ecosystem access, and ONIX Miracle Stack expandability [1]. These are functional strengths. The critical counterpoint is the FiiO M21 at 370 USD, which provides Android 13, the same core DAP/USB DAC/Bluetooth receiver use cases, and stronger manufacturer-specified THD+N, SNR, dynamic range, channel separation, and audible-band frequency-response metrics at much lower cost [3][4]. Its maximum 950mW 4.4mm output requires external USB-C power, while battery-only high-gain 4.4mm output is lower than the ONIX maximum, so users who need maximum output while fully portable should treat that as a practical condition rather than an automatic advantage [3][4]. No independent third-party measurements exist to verify either product's specifications, so no objectively confirmed performance advantage justifies the 529 USD premium. The XM10 Ltd's premium is attributable to its ultra-slim 11.5mm stainless steel chassis, larger 6-inch display, 128GB internal storage, DSD1024 support, and ONIX ecosystem positioning [1]. The 1-year warranty is a material concern for a complex Android DAP [2]. Users for whom the Miracle Stack modular system, the ultra-slim form factor, or battery-powered 877mW output in an 11.5mm chassis are priorities may find the premium warranted; users whose primary concern is measured audio performance per dollar are better served by the FiiO M21.
