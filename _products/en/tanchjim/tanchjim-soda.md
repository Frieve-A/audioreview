## Overview

The TANCHJIM SODA is a universal wired in-ear monitor released in early 2026, priced at 309.99 USD [1]. It is TANCHJIM's first multi-driver IEM, combining a proprietary 10mm dynamic driver, four in-house balanced armature units, and two passive radiators in a tribrid configuration. Founded in 2015, TANCHJIM has produced measurement-referenced single-driver IEMs including the Oxygen and Force; the SODA marks a significant architectural departure, deploying several entirely new proprietary subsystems simultaneously.

## Scientific Validity

$$ \Large \text{0.6} $$

A comparative frequency response plot from an independent review [2] confirms the SODA exhibits Harman-leaning tuning characterized by a modest bass lift of approximately 3dB around 200Hz, a dip in the 3–7kHz region, and a forward upper midrange presentation. This maps to an estimated standard deviation of approximately 2.0–2.5dB from the Harman target — an acceptable result. The SODA's FR curve has not been numerically extracted from a direct calibrated measurement; the characterization is based on comparative graph data [2] and is treated as confirmed but approximate.

The manufacturer specifies THD of 0.5% at 1kHz, 94dB SPL, and 0.69%/0.36% odd- and even-order harmonic distortion at 100dB SPL [1]. These figures are measured at high output levels and reflect the point at which distortion becomes measurable rather than performance at typical listening levels; no independent third-party THD measurement is available. S/N ratio is not published by the manufacturer or any third party. Passive isolation is claimed at greater than 39dB based on TANCHJIM's own anechoic chamber measurement [1]; this figure has not been independently verified. Overall measured performance is moderate, reflecting confirmed acceptable frequency response alignment with no confirmed typical-level distortion data.

## Technology Level

$$ \Large \text{0.9} $$

The SODA demonstrates high technology investment across multiple dimensions. All core subsystems were developed by TANCHJIM's in-house R&D team without OEM sourcing [1], including the DMT5 fifth-generation dynamic driver — a dual-magnetic, dual-cavity design with a DLC dome composite diaphragm representing five successive generations of proprietary magnetic circuit and diaphragm engineering. The PURE series balanced armature drivers, deployed here for the first time, are a clear technical differentiator because TANCHJIM presents them as in-house developed rather than sourced components [1]. The Silk System passive units are fabricated via DLP 3D printing for precision acoustic microchamber geometry, enabling structural tolerances unachievable through conventional molding. The pressure-relief acoustic duct is explicitly patented [1], and DLP manufacturing carries additional implicit IP. Both DLP-printed acoustic structures and in-house BA technology are approaches other manufacturers would seek to replicate. The accumulated engineering depth — five driver generations, first-deployment BA R&D, and novel passive radiator acoustic structures — represents substantial concentrated know-how.

The main limiting factor is that the SODA operates as a fully passive analog device with no DSP, no companion app, and no digital signal processing in the audio path. Even with that limitation, the depth of in-house driver and acoustic-structure development supports the 0.9 technology score.

## Cost-Performance

$$ \Large \text{0.1} $$

This site evaluates cost-performance based solely on functionality and measured performance values, without considering driver types or configurations.

The SODA's current market price is 309.99 USD [1]. User-facing functions provided: wired universal IEM, 3.5mm single-ended output (included), 4.4mm balanced output (included via swappable plug), passive acoustic design without DSP or wireless capability.

The Truthear HOLA [4] at 18.99 USD provides wired universal IEM connectivity with 3.5mm SE output but does not include a 4.4mm balanced cable. Adding the lowest-priced compatible 0.78mm 2-pin 4.4mm balanced cable available on Amazon (10 USD) brings the normalized total to 28.99 USD, achieving functional equivalence with the SODA.

Performance comparison (provisional — SODA FR values estimated from comparative graph data; no direct numeric third-party measurement available):

- FR deviation from Harman target: Truthear HOLA at 2.31dB STD (crinacle/hangout.audio, third-party, IEC60318-4 rig [3]) vs SODA at approximately 2.0–2.5dB STD estimated — HOLA value falls within the SODA estimated range; provisionally equivalent
- THD at 1kHz, 94dB SPL: Truthear HOLA at ≤0.1% (manufacturer spec [4]) vs SODA at 0.5% (manufacturer spec [1]) — HOLA better; both are high-SPL manufacturer specifications not representative of typical-level distortion

Passive isolation comparison is doubly provisional: the SODA's claimed >39dB is an unverified manufacturer lab measurement [1], and no independent isolation data is available for the HOLA from recognized measurement sources. This metric cannot serve as a firm disqualification criterion under these conditions.

## Reliability & Support

$$ \Large \text{0.5} $$

The SODA carries a 1-year warranty on the IEM body and a 3-month warranty on the cable [1]. The IEM warranty period is limited, while global support is provided through an established authorized distributor network — Linsoul Audio, HiFiGo, ShenZhenAudio, and regional retailers — alongside direct manufacturer after-sales access [1]. No documented failure patterns, driver defects, or product-specific reliability complaints have been identified as of the review date; evidence for a construction-specific penalty is absent. Support is handled through distributor replacement rather than component-level repair, consistent with the IEM category. The detachable 0.78mm 2-pin cable system permits independent cable replacement, leaving reliability and support at a middle rating.

## Rationality of Design Philosophy

$$ \Large \text{0.7} $$

TANCHJIM explicitly states that "quantitative evaluation based on high-precision measurement is the foundation for creating high-performance products" [1], and third-party FR data confirms the SODA targets a Harman-adjacent tuning [2] — a scientifically grounded approach. The SODA represents genuine model progression: it is TANCHJIM's first multi-driver IEM, deploying entirely new driver subsystems not present in any prior product. The design introduces novel approaches — first-deployment in-house BA drivers, passive radiators for ultra-high-frequency treatment, and a patented ergonomic pressure-relief system — reflecting an innovative posture. Cost allocation is primarily performance-directed: proprietary BA development, DLP acoustic structures, custom crossover engineering, and ergonomic venting represent function-facing investment [1].

The Silk System is marketed as producing "smoother, more natural treble" in the 16–23kHz range [1] without independent measurement verification; individual audibility above 16kHz is strongly age-dependent, making this an unsubstantiated audible-effect claim. Additionally, the two passive radiator units targeting 16–23kHz represent driver quantity investment whose audible advantage over simpler configurations has not been demonstrated. These caveats keep the rationality score below the technology score.

## Advice

The SODA is built on legitimate in-house engineering: first-deployment proprietary BA drivers, DLP-fabricated acoustic structures, and a patented ergonomic pressure-relief system are credible technical achievements. Listeners who specifically require 4.4mm balanced output included in the box, Harman-adjacent tuning, and the patented ergonomic venting for extended-wear comfort have a technically sound foundation to evaluate.

The cost-performance outcome is severe nonetheless. Wired IEM listeners whose primary requirements are frequency response accuracy and low distortion can obtain provisionally equivalent measured performance at 28.99 USD. The SODA commands approximately 10.7x the price of the cheapest identified functionally equivalent alternative, and independent third-party measurements confirming the claimed passive isolation advantage or typical-listening-level distortion performance remain unavailable as of this review.

Buyers considering the SODA are advised to await independent third-party measurement data — particularly for passive isolation and typical-level THD — before committing. The current information available does not provide sufficient basis to verify the performance premium the SODA's price implies.
