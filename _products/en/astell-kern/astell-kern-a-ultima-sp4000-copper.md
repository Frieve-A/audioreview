## Overview

The A&ultima SP4000 Copper is a limited-edition portable digital audio player released in February 2026. Its 99.98% copper body has a multilayer protective finish and weighs 642g, versus 615g for the standard SP4000. The internal 99.9% copper shield is already part of the standard platform, not a Copper-only addition. Both versions share four AK4191EQ processors paired with four AK4499EX DACs, full Android with streaming-app support, 8GB RAM, 256GB storage with microSD expansion, and a 6-inch 2160×1080 touchscreen. Playback supports PCM up to 768kHz/32-bit and native DSD512 [1][2].

## Scientific Validity

$$ \Large \text{0.8} $$

Manufacturer specifications indicate very low noise and distortion: balanced/unbalanced THD+N of 0.0003%/0.0005%, S/N ratio of 131dB/129dB, and crosstalk of -145dB/-136dB, all at 1kHz. Frequency response is ±0.006dB over 20Hz–20kHz; IMD is 0.0004% with 800Hz/10kHz tones at a 4:1 ratio. These specifications use Normal Mode with automute off; load, measurement bandwidth and weighting are not fully stated. Output voltage is 8.2Vrms balanced or 4.1Vrms unbalanced, explicitly without a load, and output impedance is 1.7Ω/0.7Ω. The voltage figures therefore do not establish headphone power under load [1].

Hi-Fi News measured a standard 615g SP4000, not the Copper edition. Its balanced line-output tests found 7.82Vrms maximum, 120.5dB A-weighted S/N, and 0.00018% distortion at 1kHz/0dBFS; 20kHz distortion plus noise was 0.0025%. Its separate headphone test reached clipping at 380mW into 32Ω, with 0dBFS input and volume 148, and 161mW into 8Ω. The boxout does not identify the output socket, so these results must not be treated as a complete balanced/unbalanced power characterization [3]. They are useful evidence about the shared platform, but neither verify a Copper sample nor establish a copper-material benefit.

Filter choice matters: that standard unit's Slow and Short Delay Slow settings fell by 8.3dB at 20kHz with 48kHz files. Sharp and Short Delay Sharp gave the flattest responses, including only 0.1dB attenuation at 45kHz/90kHz with 96kHz/192kHz files [3]. Copper-specific dynamic-range, low-level-linearity and output-jitter results have not been established. The Copper-specific assessment remains provisional; its published specifications are strong, but direct independent measurements are still needed.

## Technology Level

$$ \Large \text{0.8} $$

The technical achievement lies in system integration rather than the enclosure material. Astell&Kern combines its 1:1 processor/DAC arrangement, parallel op-amp High Driving Mode, ESA signal alignment, low-noise power regulation and Any Layer HDI circuit-board construction with its own Android audio path. ADP is designed to bypass Android sample-rate conversion for apps. Networking, Roon Ready, USB DAC operation, selectable DAC filters, crossfeed and ReplayGain make this a substantial hardware/software implementation [1]. These features demonstrate in-house design and accumulated circuit-layout and software expertise. The commercial AKM and Qualcomm components are contemporary building blocks, however; the documentation does not establish adoption of patented technology or a multi-year technological lead. Copper adds no new processing capability. Japanese support specifies that EQ applies to headphone-jack playback of PCM at 8–192kHz and 8/16/24 bits, not Bluetooth output [13].

## Cost-Performance

$$ \Large \text{1.0} $$

As of September 22, 2026, US new-stock prices are 4,499 USD for the Copper edition at MusicTeck and 4,290 USD for the standard SP4000 Black at Audio46, without coupons [4][5]. The standard version is the least expensive complete match established among the alternatives examined. It preserves Android apps, display and controls, storage expansion, EQ/crossfeed, ReplayGain, DAC filters and DAR, Roon Ready/Qobuz Connect, Wi-Fi file/network playback, Bluetooth transmission/reception, USB DAC/digital output, optical output, and both headphone outputs. The published output voltage, impedance, frequency response, THD+N, IMD, S/N and crosstalk specifications are identical [1][11]. This is a provisional, manufacturer-specification comparison, not a claim that both chassis versions have been independently measured alike.

Lower-cost candidates do not automatically provide the same complete package. FiiO JM21's local decoding stops at PCM 384kHz/DSD256 [6]. iBasso DX180 specifies 6Vrms balanced and 3Vrms unbalanced, below SP4000's unloaded voltage ratings, and provides coaxial rather than optical digital output. Its 690mW-per-channel balanced rating uses 32Ω and THD+N below 0.1%; its -121dB THD+N specification instead uses A-weighting and a 600Ω load. Those are different tests, not evidence of identical performance [7]. Adding storage or a digital converter does not raise the analogue output ceiling.

FiiO M27 covers the larger display, 256GB storage, optical output and high-output requirements, but its specified balanced headphone channel separation of at least 114dB does not establish the SP4000's 145dB figure under matching conditions [8]. A Samsung Galaxy A16 5G plus FiiO Q15 and microSD storage was also considered [9][10]. Q15 supplies 3.5mm/4.4mm outputs and PCM 768kHz/DSD512 USB decoding, but lacks optical output and specifies balanced headphone separation of at least 113dB. Storage or digital-output accessories do not resolve that analogue-performance uncertainty. These specifications use different loads and levels, so they are not proof of audible inferiority, but they do not support full equivalence either.

Astell&Kern's less expensive SP4000T also covers Android apps, optical output and PCM 768kHz/DSD512, but its published balanced/unbalanced THD+N is 0.0004%/0.0006% at 1kHz, higher than the Copper edition's figures. Its specification sheet therefore does not establish an equal-performance substitute [14].

CP = 4,290 USD ÷ 4,499 USD = 0.9535, rounded to 1.0.

The rounded score does not erase the standard model's 209 USD saving. It reflects the small premium over the verified full substitute, not a general claim that this is the cheapest way to obtain excellent headphone sound. The manufacturer has announced worldwide price adjustments from October 1, 2026; this comparison uses the prices available before that change [15].

## Reliability & Support

$$ \Large \text{0.6} $$

The Copper edition has a one-year limited warranty [4]. Regional manufacturer support covers Korea, the US, China, Hong Kong, Europe and Japan, including an authorized US repair center [12]. SP4000 firmware V1.020 (September 22, 2025) added Qobuz Connect, while the latest listed release, V1.041 (August 31, 2026), adds Control Lock and improves high-temperature charging stability and wake-on-touch operation [11]. These releases demonstrate continuing SP4000 support. The protective copper finish addresses oxidation, but does not by itself establish long-term durability [2]. Model-specific failure statistics, guaranteed parts-support duration and repair-cost data were not found; there is no basis to infer either exceptional reliability or unusual failure risk.

## Rationality of Design Philosophy

$$ \Large \text{0.3} $$

Integrated app playback, network access, physical controls and headphone amplification serve a practical portable-listening purpose. The Android audio path and adjustable DSP provide useful functions [1]. The Copper edition, however, emphasizes material-dependent bass, vocal and treble character while retaining the standard model's published electrical specifications [2]. Such tonal claims do not establish greater fidelity. This material-only update adds weight and elaborate machining and coating without a demonstrated controlled-test improvement, and advances neither the platform's functions nor its published audio performance. The useful integrated platform prevents the product from being merely an audio accessory, but does not justify the Copper-specific sound-quality claims.

## Advice

Choose the standard SP4000 if you need this complete feature set and published electrical performance; the Copper edition has no established fidelity advantage. If you do not need its output voltage, optical connection or full format/network support, cheaper players or a phone/DAC combination may meet your actual needs, without being complete substitutes. Check output behavior with your own headphones, especially demanding low-impedance models. For source-faithful playback, start with a Sharp filter and DAR/EQ disabled; Advanced DAR's virtual-harmonic processing changes the signal rather than verifying recovery of the original recording [1][3].
