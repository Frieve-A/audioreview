## Overview

The earaku J235 open ear is a passive wired earphone with an open-ear hook form. Its official specifications list a 14.2mm driver, 3.5mm 4-pole connection, 1.2m cable, 14g mass, inline 3-button remote, omnidirectional microphone, and one-year warranty [1]. Its regular Japanese price is equivalent to 22.81 USD at the reference exchange rate used for this review [4][7].

## Scientific Validity

$$ \Large \text{0.5} $$

No independent frequency-response, distortion, isolation, or microphone measurements were identified. The official product page includes a response-like comparison image, but it does not provide sufficient axis units, identify the compared devices, or disclose the fixture, signal level, smoothing, and sample information needed for interpretation [1]. The available material therefore does not establish the J235's fidelity relative to an audible-performance target, so Scientific Validity cannot be evaluated beyond 0.5.

## Technology Level

$$ \Large \text{0.2} $$

The J235 combines a conventional dynamic earphone element, passive analog wiring, a microphone, and inline controls [1]. Earaku describes product R&D and design work with engineers worldwide, but the J235 documentation identifies no product-specific patent, technical paper, novel signal processing, or implementation that would create a durable replication barrier [2]. Its entirely analog implementation uses mature techniques and offers little technical differentiation from other wired open-ear headsets, placing Technology Level at 0.2.

## Cost-Performance

$$ \Large \text{0.8} $$

This site evaluates based solely on functionality and measured performance values, without considering driver types or configurations. The J235's current regular price is 22.81 USD [4][7]. The Avantree Resolve costs 18.49 USD and provides the relevant equal-or-better catalog functions: a 3.5mm wired open-ear hook design, microphone, and inline volume, playback, track, and call controls [5][6]. Neither product has interpretable measured fidelity data, so this is a provisional comparison using the available catalog functions. CP = 18.49 USD ÷ 22.81 USD = 0.8106, which gives a score of 0.8.

## Reliability & Support

$$ \Large \text{0.5} $$

The passive wired construction avoids batteries, charging circuits, and wireless electronics, leaving relatively few components subject to age-related failure [1]. This structural simplicity is balanced by a one-year warranty [1]. Manufacturer-direct web support and an FAQ are available through JUST MATCH Corporation, but the documented service is Japan-focused [3]. Product-specific failure-rate, RMA, MTBF, post-warranty parts-supply, and repair-price data are not published, so those factors remain unknown rather than being treated as evidence of either high or low reliability.

## Rationality of Design Philosophy

$$ \Large \text{0.4} $$

The open-ear hooks, passive connection, inline controls, and microphone directly serve ambient-aware listening and calls without charging or pairing [1]. The simple architecture and regular price of 22.81 USD avoid costly features unrelated to those purposes [4][7], and no physically implausible audio claim was identified. However, the product uses a conservative implementation, while its sound-quality presentation is not supported by a reproducible measurement set [1]. The design is functionally coherent and cost-conscious but not measurement-led or innovative, supporting a Rationality score of 0.4.

## Advice

The J235 is suitable for users who need a lightweight wired ear-hook headset with ambient awareness, microphone use, and inline playback controls without charging or pairing. Buyers seeking verified fidelity should note that neither independent measurements nor interpretable manufacturer performance data were identified. The Avantree Resolve provides the same relevant catalog functions at a lower current price, while the J235's manufacturer-direct support is documented primarily for Japan.
