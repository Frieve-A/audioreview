## Overview

FitEar, known primarily for custom in-ear monitors built for professional musicians and engineers, released the Origin-1 on February 14, 2026 [1] as its first mass-market, non-custom closed-back over-ear headphone. The product is positioned as extending the acoustic design of FitEar's professional-only Monitor-1 SR monitoring headphone to general consumer listening. Origin-1 uses a single 40mm dynamic driver custom-tuned by driver supplier QIGOM, with mechanical design and manufacturing outsourced to Sound Warrior (Shiroshita Kogyo) [2].

## Technology Level

$$ \Large \text{0.1} $$

Origin-1 combines standard, mature industry components: a 40mm dynamic driver sourced and custom-tuned by OEM driver supplier QIGOM [1], mechanical design and manufacturing outsourced to ODM partner Sound Warrior [2], a widely used mini XLR 4-pin detachable connector, and nylon 6 housing [1]. None of these represent cutting-edge or exclusive technology; all are readily available to competitors, and no patent, proprietary engineering, or independently verified performance benefit was found. Positioning as an heir to the professional Monitor-1 SR and the ear pad's ergonomic shape function as differentiators without independent measurement supporting any performance claim, serving primarily as marketing narrative. This reads as an OEM/ODM-driven consumer lineup-expansion product rather than an in-house technical achievement.

## Cost-Performance

$$ \Large \text{0.3} $$

This site evaluates based solely on functionality and measured performance values, without considering driver types or configurations. Origin-1 carries a current market price of 487 USD [3]. Origin-1 itself has no third-party measurement or manufacturer-published audio performance data on any policy-defined metric, so the comparison below is provisional, built from currently known facts. Two cheaper closed-back dynamic headphones with independently measured, problematic-level frequency response deviation were investigated and excluded as comparators. AKG K371 (158 USD [5]) is an equivalent closed-back, over-ear, dynamic-driver, detachable-cable headphone with no DSP or wireless functions, and third-party measurement confirms it has distortion in the 0.05–0.2% range and frequency response deviation of approximately ±2–3dB from the Harman target, with no problematic-level readings on either metric [6]. Since Origin-1 has no confirmed data on any metric, no measured basis exists to consider AKG K371 inferior, and its confirmed non-problematic measured performance combined with equivalent functionality qualifies it as an equivalent-or-better provisional comparator. CP = 158 ÷ 487 = 0.324 → 0.3.

## Reliability & Support

$$ \Large \text{0.4} $$

Origin-1 carries a below-average warranty: 1 year on the main unit and only 90 days on the included cable and accessories [1]. Support is provided directly by the manufacturer through an official FAQ and repair inquiry channel with a documented repair process [4], but the support footprint appears limited to the Japanese domestic market, with no evidence of international coverage or specialized support services. Build complexity is documented as moderate rather than clearly simple or robust, so no additional credit applies there. As a passive, non-firmware product released only around five months before this review, no statistical failure-rate data or established track record exists yet for this specific model.

## Rationality of Design Philosophy

$$ \Large \text{0.3} $$

Origin-1's design philosophy is framed as bringing FitEar's professional monitoring heritage (Monitor-1 SR) to consumer listening, but no measurement data of any kind has been published to substantiate the specific claims made, including an elevated signal-to-noise ratio despite the closed-back design and improved bass definition from the ear pad seal [1][2]. Cost allocation shows an explicit brand-premium component alongside an unclear link between price and demonstrated function or performance, and the product otherwise represents a conventional assembly of established industry-standard components — a single dynamic driver, standard connector, and standard housing — without any novel engineering approach [1][2]. The conservative technical approach, combined with the absence of demonstrated function-linked cost or verified proprietary benefit, keeps this evaluation below neutral.

## Advice

Given the absence of independent measurement data for Origin-1 itself, the below-average 90-day accessory warranty, and construction built entirely from OEM/ODM components, prospective buyers should treat Origin-1's price primarily as reflecting brand positioning rather than demonstrated technical differentiation. A provisional cost-performance comparison found a measured closed-back headphone (AKG K371) with confirmed non-problematic distortion and frequency response performance at roughly one-third of Origin-1's price. Buyers drawn to FitEar's design lineage may still find the product acceptable, but should wait for independent third-party measurements before assuming any specific performance advantage over other closed-back dynamic headphones. Confirm the shorter cable/accessory warranty terms before purchase.
