## Overview

WiiM Sub Pro is the company's first powered subwoofer. It pairs an 8-inch driver and 250 W RMS Class-D amplifier with a universal RCA line-level input, Wi-Fi 6/Ethernet connectivity, and RoomFit auto calibration, which uses a phone or external microphone sweep to adjust EQ, crossover, phase, level, and delay. [1] The official specifications list Bluetooth 5.3 LE, while the product-page FAQ describes Bluetooth as a future feature, so current Bluetooth availability is unclear. [1] The product can operate from a conventional line-level source or integrate wirelessly with compatible WiiM products. [1]

## Scientific Validity

$$ \Large \text{0.4} $$

Erin's Audio Corner measured maximum output using CEA-2010-A, a Klippel Distortion Analyzer 2/TBM, LFE-bypass mode, and 2m ground-plane peak SPL. [2] WiiM Sub Pro exceeded the distortion threshold at 20Hz; reached 82.9dB at 25Hz, 91.5dB at 31.5Hz, 100.2dB at 40Hz, 104.6dB at 50Hz, 106.7dB at 63Hz, 106.5dB at 80Hz, 105.3dB at 100Hz, 104.1dB at 125Hz, and 100.6dB at 160Hz; and averaged 104.5dB from 40Hz-80Hz and 100.0dB from 25Hz-100Hz. [2] Under the same conditions, the SVS 3000 Micro averaged 102.7dB and 98.0dB over those two bands, while the KEF KC62 averaged 96.9dB and 95.0dB. [2] This confirms useful mid-bass headroom, but the sharp output limit below 31.5Hz is an audible constraint for deep-bass effects. The manufacturer specifies 35Hz-200Hz ±3dB, 25Hz at -6dB, 104dB maximum SPL, and less than 1% THD+N, but does not state level or test conditions for the response and maximum-SPL figures or frequency and SPL for THD+N. [1] S/N ratio, independent fixed-level frequency response, normal-level distortion, compression, and group delay are not available. Stronger output above 40Hz does not offset the deep-bass limitation or the incomplete measurement set, supporting a Scientific Validity score of 0.4.

## Technology Level

$$ \Large \text{0.6} $$

WiiM Sub Pro integrates phone- or external-microphone RoomFit calibration with app-based DSP control and Wi-Fi 6/Ethernet connectivity. [1] Linking room measurement to adjustment of EQ, crossover, phase, level, and delay is a technically useful combination of acoustic measurement, embedded DSP, software, and network control. The cited technical material does not establish a novel calibration method or an implementation that would be difficult for competitors to reproduce, so the strength lies in integration rather than a demonstrated technical breakthrough.

## Cost-Performance

$$ \Large \text{1.0} $$

**Current market price: 449 USD.** [1]

The essential user-facing functions are a universal RCA line-level input, microphone-based RoomFit auto correction, 10-band parametric EQ, app adjustment of crossover, phase, level, and delay, and Wi-Fi 6/Ethernet connectivity. [1] The measured-performance floor is the review target's CEA-2010-A result: 82.9dB at 25Hz, 91.5dB at 31.5Hz, and a 104.5dB average from 40Hz-80Hz. [2] The functionally adjacent Sonos Sub Mini currently costs 499 USD, is limited to supported Sonos products, and lacks a universal line-level input and 10-band parametric EQ, so it is neither cheaper nor equivalent. [3] No product below 449 USD was confirmed to equal or exceed both the complete function set and these measured output figures. WiiM Sub Pro is therefore the cheapest confirmed equivalent option, so CP is 1.0.

## Reliability & Support

$$ \Large \text{0.6} $$

WiiM provides direct manufacturer support and OTA firmware maintenance. Its release notes list six Sub Pro updates between July 2025 and March 2026, including fixes for wake-up, volume, standby, playback crashes, RoomFit, and RCA synchronization. [4] The limited warranty for the original purchaser is one year; purchases from authorized retailers are handled through the retailer and require proof of purchase. [5] Statistical failure-rate, RMA, and MTBF data are not published in the cited sources, so long-term hardware reliability cannot yet be quantified.

## Rationality of Design Philosophy

$$ \Large \text{0.8} $$

WiiM Sub Pro centers its design on a measurement-based calibration process that uses a microphone sweep to adjust EQ, crossover, phase, level, and delay. [1] This directly addresses room interaction, while the universal RCA input preserves compatibility with conventional sources and the app and network interfaces consolidate control and wireless-system integration. [1] The documented design emphasis is therefore DSP correction and practical integration directed at measurable room response and user operation.

## Advice

WiiM Sub Pro is best suited to buyers who need universal RCA compatibility, app-controlled DSP, and automatic room correction in a wireless-capable subwoofer, especially within a WiiM system. Its measured 40Hz-80Hz average is higher than the SVS 3000 Micro and KEF KC62 results in the same table, but output is sharply restricted below 31.5Hz. Buyers requiring strong output in that deep-bass region should prioritize the CEA-2010-A figures over the unconditioned 25Hz extension claim; when required output is concentrated above 40Hz and remains within the measured headroom, the correction and integration functions retain practical value.
