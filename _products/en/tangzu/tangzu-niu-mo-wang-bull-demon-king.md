## Overview

The TANGZU Niu Mo Wang / Bull Demon King (牛魔王), sold internationally as the OX Demon King, is TANGZU's first full-size over-ear headphone and first open-back planar magnetic headphone, following a history focused primarily on in-ear monitors [1]. It is an open-back, wired, passive design built around a single 85mm planar magnetic driver per side, housed in CNC-machined aluminum-alloy earcups. The manufacturer states a design philosophy of "Original Sound as the Foundation." As of this review, TANGZU lists it at 349 USD [1].

## Scientific Validity

$$ \Large \text{0.4} $$

The manufacturer specifies a frequency response range of 10–40,000 Hz with no deviation tolerance and maximum total harmonic distortion of THD ≤3% at 1kHz [1]. Reference Audio Analyzer independently reports average sensitivity of 111.2 dB/V SPL and 95.3 dB/mW SPL, average impedance of 25.8 ohm, and an open over-ear acoustic design, with measurements performed on HDM-X, type 3.3 artificial ear, and Human: romanrex stands [2]. Its HDM-X frequency-response-to-target graph shows roughly -6.0 to +3.9 dB across 100 Hz to 10 kHz [3]. This verifies basic operation and adequate drivability, but the target-relative frequency-response spread is large enough to prevent a high fidelity score, and no independent THD/IMD measurements are published. Scientific validity is therefore slightly below neutral rather than unevaluable.

## Technology Level

$$ \Large \text{0.3} $$

The headphone is built on a single 85mm planar magnetic driver in an open-back enclosure with CNC-machined aluminum earcups — all standard, mature techniques widely used across the industry [1][2]. Planar magnetic transduction has been mainstream since the early 2010s, so it is neither cutting-edge nor differentiating. The manufacturer discloses no proprietary or patented technology, no novel diaphragm material, magnet topology, or stator geometry. In-house development is claimed but cannot be substantiated, and the driver description matches generic planar architecture. As a purely passive analog transducer with no digital, DSP, or software integration, and an implementation that is easily replicated in a crowded field, the technology level is below average.

## Cost-Performance

$$ \Large \text{0.1} $$

Cost-performance is judged by user-facing functions and documented performance rather than by driver type or configuration.

The review target's current market price is 349 USD [1]. The cheapest equivalent-function product identified is the Superlux HD681, a wired passive over-ear headphone. Superlux's official sheet confirms a circumaural semi-open design, 3.5mm plug with 6.3mm adapter, 32 ohm impedance, 98 dB SPL sensitivity, and 10–30,000 Hz frequency response [4]. Thomann lists it in stock at 21.90 USD [5]. It does not match the TANGZU's detachable cable or planar driver, but those are secondary to the core passive wired over-ear playback function, and this comparison is based on user-facing playback function and documented performance rather than driver type or enclosure material.

CP = 21.90 USD ÷ 349 USD = 0.063

Documented performance context:
- Review target: RAA frequency-response-to-target graph at roughly -6.0 to +3.9 dB across 100 Hz to 10 kHz, and manufacturer maximum THD ≤3% at 1kHz [1][3]
- Comparator: official 10–30,000 Hz response, 98 dB SPL sensitivity, 32 ohm impedance, and retailer-listed THD <1% at 100 mW/1kHz [4][5]

Because the review target's independent data remain incomplete and the comparator is not identical in secondary conveniences, this comparison is conservative. The price gap is still large enough that the calculated value gives a score of 0.1.

## Reliability & Support

$$ \Large \text{0.4} $$

The cited manufacturer page does not document a model-specific warranty term or a unified manufacturer-operated global service network [1]. Support documentation is therefore limited, so no positive support-infrastructure adjustment applies. As a passive analog wired headphone the device has no firmware or software. No documented recalls or systematic defects were found in the cited sources, though the metal earcup/yoke construction still has conventional mechanical wear points such as hinges and pads, and no statistical failure-rate data is available. The limited public support and warranty documentation reduces the base reliability assessment to 0.4.

## Rationality of Design Philosophy

$$ \Large \text{0.4} $$

TANGZU's stated philosophy of "Original Sound as the Foundation" is communicated mainly through qualitative descriptors; the official page gives a broad frequency range and maximum THD figure but no target curve or independent verification package [1]. RAA now provides external frequency-response context, but that third-party result does not change the manufacturer's limited measurement disclosure [2][3]. The choice of mature planar-magnetic and open-back technology with CNC mass-manufacturing is a rational, proven approach. The clear negative is cost allocation: for a passive headphone with no electronics or signal processing, a meaningful share of cost is directed to the machined-metal chassis and the heavily themed industrial design rather than to function or measurable performance. As the brand's first full-size model there is no predecessor against which to demonstrate measurable progression. Overall, the design philosophy is somewhat below average.

## Advice

This is a 349 USD open-back planar over-ear built on standard, mature technology. RAA measurements confirm basic operation but show notable target-relative frequency-response deviation, and independent distortion measurements are still unavailable. Functionally comparable wired passive over-ear headphones with documented performance sell for a small fraction of the price, which places the cost-performance very low. Buyers who specifically value the machined-metal construction and themed industrial design may find it appealing, but those prioritizing measurable performance per unit of cost have far cheaper options.
