## Overview

The Activo P1 is a portable Android-based digital audio player released in July 2024 by Activo, a sub-brand of Astell&Kern aimed at users seeking a simpler, lower-priced entry into hi-res portable audio. It pairs a dual ESS ES9219Q DAC configuration with Astell&Kern's TERATON ALPHA amplifier circuit, a 4.1-inch touchscreen, dual-band Wi-Fi streaming (Tidal, Qobuz, Apple Music), Bluetooth 5.3 with LDAC/aptX HD/LHDC, dual SE/balanced headphone outputs, native DSD256/PCM hi-res playback, full MQA decoding, and a 20-band parametric EQ, targeting active, on-the-go listening. Its 64GB storage is expandable by microSD; the Japanese distributor specifies support for cards up to 2TB. Battery life is specified as up to 20 hours with 16-bit/44.1kHz FLAC, volume 50, single-ended output and the screen off [1][2].

## Scientific Validity

$$ \Large \text{0.8} $$

The manufacturer specifies the complete P1 at THD+N of 0.0006% single-ended and 0.0007% balanced, S/N of 120/121dB, and crosstalk of -139/-140dB at 1kHz, with 20Hz–20kHz response within ±0.013dB. Published IMD is 0.0002/0.0003% using 800Hz and 10kHz tones at 4:1. These are product specifications, not the DAC chip's theoretical limits, and indicate very low noise, distortion and response error [1].

The specified 2Vrms single-ended and 4.2Vrms balanced outputs are unloaded values; they do not establish power into demanding headphones. Output impedance is 1/2Ω respectively. Independent bench measurements were not found in the consulted sources, and the published distortion and separation figures omit load and output-level conditions. Dynamic range is not specified separately. Performance under load and near clipping therefore remains unverified [1].

## Technology Level

$$ \Large \text{0.6} $$

The P1 combines two established ESS ES9219Q DACs with Astell&Kern's in-house TERATON ALPHA amplifier design. It uses a separate amplifier implementation rather than relying solely on the DACs' integrated amplifiers. Android, dual-band Wi-Fi, third-party streaming apps and software audio processing provide substantial digital integration [1]. The proprietary amplifier implementation and connected software platform are the notable engineering contributions; the DAC components and basic conversion approach are mature technology, without a demonstrated recent breakthrough in this implementation.

## Cost-Performance

$$ \Large \text{1.0} $$

The current reference price is 534.45 USD, converted from the official UK store's available listing using the ECB's September 21, 2026 rates. A current US purchase price was not established, and the checked Japanese retailer marks sales as ended. This is a UK purchase-price reference, excluding destination-specific shipping and import charges [3][4][13].

The comparison includes Android streaming apps, local PCM/DSD playback and MQA, Bluetooth transmission and reception with the supported codecs, USB DAC operation and digital output, physical playback controls, 3.5mm/4.4mm outputs, 64GB storage with microSD expansion, 20-band parametric EQ, crossfeed, fast charging and extended portable playback [1][2]. Because independent P1 measurements are unavailable, the audio comparison is provisional and uses explicitly published manufacturer specifications.

The 249 USD HiBy R4 is a lower-priced candidate, but its official audio table gives channel separation of 71/111dB, versus P1's 139/140dB. R4's 0.0007/0.0005% minimum THD also excludes the noise included in P1's THD+N. The FiiO JM21 similarly guarantees separation of only 70/110dB. The R4 and JM21 specifications are associated with loaded outputs, whereas P1 omits the separation load condition: these figures do not prove an audible difference or an identical-condition performance ranking, but they do not establish equal-or-better performance either [1][5][6][7]. Memory cards, playback software and a power bank can address storage, processing and endurance gaps; they cannot remedy the analog-output limitation.

External DAC combinations also have limitations. A smartphone or R4 feeding a FiiO BTR17 does not establish matching separation: BTR17 specifies 78/120dB even in desktop mode. The Astell&Kern AK HC5 specifies 139/141dB separation, but its IMD of 0.0006/0.0005% is higher than P1's 0.0002/0.0003%, using the same stated test-tone pair and ratio; neither supplies complete load and level conditions. At its official listed price of 450 USD, adding HC5 to R4 already totals 699 USD before storage, software or supplementary power [5][8][9]. No cheaper single device or completed combination meeting the full function and published-performance requirements was identified. P1 is therefore the cheapest equivalent option established here, and CP is 1.0.

## Reliability & Support

$$ \Large \text{0.4} $$

Activo specifies a one-year warranty for both the player and its embedded battery, with a German service partner handling European repairs and returns [10]. The warranty is relatively short, and this regional arrangement does not establish a worldwide manufacturer repair network. Official firmware records document maintenance after launch: version 1.11 corrected an initial menu that displayed seven DAC filters when only two were implemented, and version 1.12, documented in June 2025, fixed microSD recognition in some apps and improved stability [11][12]. Later updates were not found as of September 22, 2026, so a continuing regular update schedule is unconfirmed. This does not establish that support has ended. A guaranteed security-update period, repair-parts availability period and statistical failure-rate data were not found in the consulted documentation.

## Rationality of Design Philosophy

$$ \Large \text{0.6} $$

The combination of Android streaming, physical playback controls, headphone amplification and battery operation serves a clear portable purpose. A phone plus external DAC can perform much of the same work, but the P1's 155g package and separate playback battery provide practical convenience without keeping a second device attached [1][8]. Its adjustable EQ and crossfeed give users direct control over reproduction. DAR upsampling is advertised as improving sound quality even though resampling alone does not establish an audible improvement over correctly reproduced source audio [1]. The useful integration is more persuasive than that sound-quality claim.

## Advice

The P1 suits listeners who value a compact Android player, physical controls and a long stated runtime in one device. HiBy R4 offers a lower-cost route to Android playback, but the available specifications do not establish full performance equivalence. Check current regional availability, third-party app compatibility and warranty arrangements before purchase. The excellent published audio figures should not be mistaken for independent verification under headphone loads.
