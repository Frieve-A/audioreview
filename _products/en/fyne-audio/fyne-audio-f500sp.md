## Overview

The Fyne Audio F500SP is a UK-built passive bookshelf loudspeaker in Fyne Audio's Special Production series. Its two-way design combines a 150mm IsoFlare driver with a 25mm magnesium tweeter, a downward-firing BassTrax port, and a passive crossover near 1.7kHz. Its five-post terminal set supports bi-wiring plus a separate driver-chassis ground connection [1]. The current US price is 1,999 USD per pair across four in-stock finishes [3].

## Scientific Validity

$$ \Large \text{0.4} $$

Stereophile measured the F500SP without its grille, at 50 inches on the tweeter axis; the principal farfield graph is averaged across a 30-degree horizontal window [2]. Visual readings show approximately +2.5dB at 100Hz, a transition from 0 to -3dB across 200–600Hz, and roughly 0 to +2dB from 1–5kHz. The tweeter is balanced about 2dB above the woofer. Above 5kHz, multiple narrow peaks and dips appear, with the deepest notch at approximately 15.6kHz reaching 10–11dB below the midband before recovering near 20kHz [2]. The cumulative spectral-decay graph is generally clean but shows three narrow ridges around 6–8kHz, 11kHz, and 15.6kHz [2]. Measured voltage sensitivity is 87dB(B)/2.83V/m, 3dB below the 90dB specification. Impedance magnitude is mostly at least 6 ohms and falls to 5 ohms around 157–203Hz, but the EPDR minimum of 2.32 ohms near 121Hz requires meaningful amplifier current [2]. The port is tuned near 53Hz and has a low-level 600–700Hz peak. No THD or maximum-output measurement is published. The manufacturer specifies 42Hz–34kHz at -6dB, typical in room [1]. The tilted balance, large narrow high-treble notch, and unmeasured distortion limit Scientific Validity, while most of the lower band remains within approximately 3dB.

## Technology Level

$$ \Large \text{0.5} $$

The F500SP shows in-house loudspeaker engineering through its IsoFlare point-source driver, FyneFlute surround geometry, downward-firing BassTrax diffuser, cabinet work, and passive crossover implementation [1]. These are functional acoustic mechanisms rather than cosmetic-only features and demonstrate specialized integration knowledge. The underlying techniques are mature, however, and the product remains a passive analog loudspeaker without active correction, onboard DSP, software control, or integrated amplification [1]. In-house design expertise is balanced by mature technology and limited digital or system integration, supporting a middle-level result.

## Cost-Performance

$$ \Large \text{1.0} $$

The current US price is 1,999 USD per pair [3]. The F500SP provides passive speaker-level stereo operation, bi-wire-separated inputs, a separate driver-chassis ground terminal, and 42Hz low-frequency extension at -6dB typical in room [1]. Lower-priced passive bookshelf speakers with smoother measured response were found, but they lacked both the bi-wire separation and chassis-ground connection. These connectivity functions cannot be added externally without modifying the loudspeaker's internal crossover and driver grounding. No cheaper current finished product was found with the full user-facing connection set and equal-or-better measured performance. The F500SP is therefore the cheapest confirmed equivalent option, so CP is 1.0.

## Reliability & Support

$$ \Large \text{0.8} $$

Fyne Audio provides a 7-year warranty for passive loudspeakers bought through authorized dealers, limited to the registered original owner and non-transferable; qualifying manufacturing defects receive parts, labor, and return carriage for repair or replacement [4]. Claims proceed through the dealer, local distributor, and then Fyne customer service. The F500SP's passive construction has no active electronics, battery, wireless module, or network platform, reducing the number of failure-prone subsystems [1]. No published product-specific recall or recurring hardware failure pattern was identified, while post-warranty parts duration and repair costs are not publicly quantified. The unusually long warranty and simple passive construction support the high Reliability & Support score despite the ownership restriction.

## Rationality of Design Philosophy

$$ \Large \text{0.4} $$

The design direction is partly rational because development is directed toward acoustically relevant mechanisms: driver integration, passive crossover behavior, cabinet control, and port dispersion [1]. These mechanisms can directly affect frequency response and radiation. The limitation is the conservative commitment to a fully passive architecture, which omits active crossover control, onboard DSP correction, and software-assisted adaptation despite their ability to address response and room-integration errors. The separate chassis-ground terminal adds connectivity, but no controlled evidence establishes an audible benefit from grounding the driver chassis in normal use. The philosophy is functional and acoustically grounded but technologically conservative, supporting a below-average result.

## Advice

The F500SP provides simple speaker-level integration, bi-wire and chassis-ground connectivity, and documented 7-year warranty coverage. Independent measurements show a usable but not neutral response: a tweeter balance about 2dB high, a slightly shelved-down midrange, and narrow treble peaks and dips, while measured sensitivity is about 3dB below specification [2]. The 2.32-ohm EPDR minimum also favors an amplifier with adequate current capability. Its CP score reflects the absence of a cheaper product matching the complete connection set, not superior measured fidelity. Buyers who do not require bi-wiring and driver-chassis grounding can consider cheaper passive speakers with smoother measured response. Confirm authorized-dealer status and current finish availability before purchase [3][4].
