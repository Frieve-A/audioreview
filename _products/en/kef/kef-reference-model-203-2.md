## Overview

The KEF Reference Model 203/2 is a discontinued passive three-way floorstanding loudspeaker from the Reference /2 series documented in January 2007. It uses two 165mm woofers and a Uni-Q array containing a 165mm midrange driver and 25mm titanium tweeter. Rear-panel Uni Balance controls adjust bass and treble for placement and room acoustics. This generation integrates high-frequency reproduction into the Uni-Q tweeter and has no separate hypertweeter [1].

## Scientific Validity

$$ \Large \text{0.6} $$

KEF's manual specifies 50Hz–60kHz within ±3dB at 15° horizontally off-axis, bass extension to 40Hz at −6dB, and sensitivity of 89dB at 2.83V/1m [1]. These are manufacturer specifications, not independent results.

AUDIO magazine measured the exact 203/2 model. With bass and treble controls at 0dB, it found approximately ±1.5dB variation from 250Hz through most of the mid/treble region, with similar curves at ±7° vertically and 15° horizontally. The graph shows elevated output near 100Hz, a roughly 6dB decline above 14kHz, and additional response notches with the grille fitted. Reading the graph relative to the midrange gives roughly 40Hz at 3dB down; this is an estimate, not the laboratory's published cutoff. Sensitivity measured 87dB at 2.83V/1m, and the impedance falls to roughly 3–4Ω around 40Hz despite KEF's nominal 8Ω designation [2].

Midrange linearity and angular consistency are good, but bass emphasis and the upper-treble decline prevent uniformly neutral reproduction. The cited test does not establish THD, IMD or maximum clean output. Test-room details, acoustic gating and smoothing are not stated on the page, and these results do not establish the condition of an aged pair.

## Technology Level

$$ \Large \text{0.5} $$

KEF developed the Uni-Q drivers together, shaping the midrange cone as a waveguide and using finite-element analysis for the titanium tweeter. Independent woofer chambers reduce internal standing waves, while the passive crossover and Uni Balance network divide frequency bands and provide response adjustment [1]. These features demonstrate in-house driver development and accumulated acoustic expertise. By current standards, however, the implementation uses mature electromechanical technology and entirely passive filtering, without DSP or automatic room correction.

## Cost-Performance

$$ \Large \text{0.4} $$

A current regular new-pair price or firm, available used-pair price for the discontinued 203/2 could not be verified. The price basis is therefore SoundStage's documented **8,000 USD per pair on August 1, 2007** [3]. This historical new-product price is not a current offer or an estimate of used value. The following ratio compares that nominal historical price with present-day replacement costs, without inflation adjustment.

The least expensive complete alternative found with sufficiently comparable measured response is a **Neumann KH 150 pair** at 1,699 USD each, totaling 3,398 USD [4], an **Auray LMS-335 pair of floor stands** at 69.99 USD [5], and two **Hosa HRX-005 RCA-to-XLR cables** at 12.95 USD each [6]: **3,493.89 USD** in total. These are current new, in-stock US offers, with standard continental-US shipping included under the retailers' conditions; destination sales tax is additional. The 1.5m signal cables connect a nearby volume-controlled line source to the speakers. The KH 150 includes amplification and manual bass/treble controls, so no separate amplifier, equalizer or calibration microphone is required [7]. The source is common to both systems; the KEF pair additionally needs amplification and speaker cables, whose cost is not added to its historical price.

The KH 150 provides floor-supported stereo playback and bass/treble adjustment, with equivalent-or-better measured low-frequency extension and mid/treble linearity for listening near the reference axis. ASR's Klippel NFS measurements show 39Hz at −3dB and 35Hz at −6dB, a substantially flat mid/treble response, and a vertical listening region encompassing the KEF test's ±7° [8]. Its adjustment ranges also cover the KEF's 0/−2dB bass and +0.75 to −1.5dB treble settings [1][7]. This is a provisional comparison: different measurement methods limit precision, while the KEF test supplies neither comparable distortion nor maximum-clean-output data. It does not establish equality at every angle or at maximum playback levels.

Cheaper measured alternatives have material shortcomings. The Polk R500 reaches approximately 51Hz at −3dB and has a narrower vertical listening window; adding an equalizer does not remove its angular cancellation [9]. The Kali IN-8 V2 has a pronounced upper-treble dip and −4.67/+3.26dB variation over 80Hz–16kHz [10]. An inexpensive subwoofer can extend bass, but does not by itself resolve these mid/treble or directional differences.

## Reliability & Support

$$ \Large \text{0.6} $$

The relatively simple passive construction supports long service life. SoundStage's 2007 review records a five-year parts-and-labor warranty for this model [3]; that historical term does not establish coverage for a unit purchased today. The original manual directs service requests to the dealer and then KEF customer service [1]. Current replacement-driver availability, repair turnaround and model-specific failure statistics could not be verified, so obtain a condition assessment and repair quotation for the actual pair.

## Rationality of Design Philosophy

$$ \Large \text{0.6} $$

The design addresses identifiable acoustic problems: jointly developed drivers and a shaped waveguide control radiation through the crossover region, the divided woofer chambers limit standing waves without requiring a wide cabinet, and response controls accommodate room placement [1]. These mechanisms serve useful acoustic functions. The curved cabinet also has structural and acoustic purposes. There is no verified manufacturing-cost breakdown establishing how much of the price supports those functions. The manual combines engineering explanations with subjective tuning guidance, so it does not establish an exclusively measurement-led philosophy. Its broad ultrasonic specification alone does not establish an audible advantage; KEF describes it in connection with wide-band source formats rather than demonstrating ultrasonic audibility.

## Advice

Assess the condition of the actual pair, confirm that all drivers and response controls work, and obtain a parts-and-service quotation before buying an older unit. Use amplification capable of driving the measured low impedance, and evaluate placement with the response controls initially neutral. The published 203/2 test supports consistent midrange dispersion but also shows bass emphasis and reduced upper-treble output [2]. Price and repair availability must be checked for the specific offer.
