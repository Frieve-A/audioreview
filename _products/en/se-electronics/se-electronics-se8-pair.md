## Overview

The sE Electronics sE8 (matched pair, model sE8P) is a small-diaphragm true condenser microphone with a fixed cardioid pattern, supplied as two factory hand-selected, closely-matched units for stereo recording such as drum overheads, acoustic guitar, piano, and room miking [1]. Announced at Summer NAMM 2017, it sits above the sE7 in the company's pencil-condenser line. sE Electronics designs and builds its own capsules in-house, and the sE8 pairs that capsule with transformerless Class-A electronics in an all-metal chassis. The pair ships with a stereo bar, windscreens, clips, and a flight case, and currently sells for 549 USD per matched pair [3].

## Scientific Validity

$$ \Large \text{0.6} $$

As a small-diaphragm condenser microphone, the sE8 is assessed on signal-to-noise ratio, equivalent (self) noise, and maximum SPL. Manufacturer specifications list an S/N ratio of 81 dB, equivalent noise of 13 dB(A), and maximum SPL of 139 dB at 0 pad (rising to 159 dB with the −20 dB pad) at 0.5% THD [1]. Frequency response is a near-flat 20 Hz–20 kHz, and Sound On Sound describes the shape as essentially flat to roughly 30 Hz with only a slight presence rise of about a couple of dB near 8 kHz [2]. These figures indicate low self-noise and high headroom on the published specifications. However, no independent third-party laboratory measurements are available for verification; the values above are manufacturer specifications only, so a conservative view is taken. Overall measured performance is moderate-to-high on the available data.

## Technology Level

$$ \Large \text{0.6} $$

The sE8 is built around an in-house-designed and in-house-manufactured 1/2-inch true condenser capsule with a 3-micron gold-sputtered diaphragm, individually tuned per unit, paired with transformerless discrete Class-A output electronics and an all-metal chassis [1][2]. The in-house capsule design and manufacture, together with the accumulated capsule-building and pair-matching know-how, is reflected in the low 13 dB(A) self-noise. However, every core technology is mature and now common: externally-polarized condenser transduction, gold-sputtered diaphragms, and transformerless Class-A microphone electronics have been standard since the 1970s–1980s, so no cutting-edge or currently-desirable-to-license technology is present. The "newly-developed backplate" is a manufacturer claim with no located patent or independent verification. The device is entirely analog with no digital, DSP, or software integration. The net result sits modestly above average.

## Cost-Performance

$$ \Large \text{0.4} $$

The sE8 matched pair sells for 549 USD [3]. The cheapest product offering equivalent-or-better essential user functions and measured performance is the sE Electronics sE7 matched pair at 228.77 USD [4][5], giving:

**CP = 228.77 USD ÷ 549 USD = 0.417**

The sE7 pair provides the same essential user functions — balanced XLR output, 48V phantom power, a switchable attenuation pad, a switchable low-cut filter, and factory stereo matching — and demonstrates comparable manufacturer-specified performance for the cited metrics:

- S/N Ratio: 78 dB vs the sE8's 81 dB (equivalent)
- Maximum SPL (0 pad, 0.5% THD @ 1 kHz): 136 dB SPL vs 139 dB SPL (equivalent)
- Frequency range: 20 Hz–20 kHz vs 20 Hz–20 kHz (identical)

Equivalent self-noise follows from the S/N figure (16 dB(A) vs 13 dB(A)), and the difference does not alter the cost-performance comparison on the available specifications. As neither product has independent third-party measurements, this comparison is provisional and based on manufacturer specifications [1][4].

## Reliability & Support

$$ \Large \text{0.7} $$

Warranty is a standard 2-year term, extendable to 3 years total via free product registration, which is average-to-good for the category. The microphone is mechanically simple and robust: a passive condenser capsule with no moving parts beyond the diaphragm, housed in a rugged all-metal brass chassis with a gold-plated XLR connector, and the cited review reports no workmanship defects in the tested unit [1][2]. Support is provided by the manufacturer through a global network of authorized distributors and service centers, giving worldwide coverage. Under warranty, parts and labor are furnished free or the unit is replaced at the manufacturer's discretion; out-of-warranty repairs route to authorized service centers at customer cost, with no evidence of unreasonable repair pricing. No statistical failure-rate data is published. The only reported negatives are minor handling notes — recessed switches that need a thin tool, and a painted finish that shows fingerprints — which are cosmetic rather than core reliability concerns [2].

## Rationality of Design Philosophy

$$ \Large \text{0.5} $$

Released in 2017, when transformerless solid-state small-diaphragm condensers were fully mature, the sE8 adopts the contemporary mainstream approach using rational solid-state electronics. The design pursues genuinely measurable goals — low 13 dB(A) self-noise, high maximum SPL via switchable pads, and consistent off-axis response. However, the philosophy is explicitly balanced rather than purely measurement-driven: the company frames capsule building as "a subtle, artistic process" and leans on subjective descriptors in its marketing [1]. Costs are reasonably distributed across functional elements: a low-noise front end, switchable filters, factory pair-matching, and a protective build. The hand-tuning is labor-intensive but yields a functional payoff in unit-to-unit matching. There is no demonstrated proprietary audible benefit and no quantified generational improvement over earlier models. The net is a rational but unremarkable philosophy.

## Advice

The sE8 matched pair has robust construction and strong manufacturer specifications, particularly the 13 dB(A) self-noise and high maximum SPL, though none of this is independently verified by third-party measurement. The decisive consideration is value: the sE Electronics sE7 matched pair delivers equivalent essential functions and comparable manufacturer-specified performance (S/N 78 dB, Max SPL 136 dB, identical 20 Hz–20 kHz range) at 228.77 USD, well under half the sE8's 549 USD [4][5]. Buyers specifically needing the documented lower self-noise figure and the dual pad/low-cut options have a reason to consider the sE8, but price-focused buyers should compare the sE7 first. Note that the recessed pad and filter switches require a thin tool to operate.
