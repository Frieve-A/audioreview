## Overview

The EPZ P40 is a passive four-driver in-ear monitor combining two dynamic drivers, one micro-planar driver, and one piezoelectric ceramic driver through a four-way electronic/acoustic crossover [1]. Its in-house DLP-printed shell uses 0.78 mm two-pin cable connectors, and the supplied cable has interchangeable 3.5 mm and 4.4 mm plugs [1]. The current US listing is 159.99 USD, although the site simultaneously shows a sold-out/unavailable variant message and an enabled add-to-cart control; availability should therefore be confirmed before purchase [1].

## Scientific Validity

$$ \Large \text{0.6} $$

Boizoff measured an EPZ-supplied sample with an IEC 60318-4 ear-simulator clone and GRAS KB501X-style pinnae, calibrating each run at 1 kHz to 94 or 114 dB with an IEC 60942-compliant calibrator [3]. With stock silicone tips, the frequency-response graph broadly follows the Harman IE 2019 v2 target through 10 kHz: bass is slightly lower, the region near 1.5 kHz slightly higher, and 3–8 kHz close to or slightly below the target. The 10–15.5 kHz shelf lies above the target, but the source explicitly warns that absolute results above 10 kHz are limited by the ear simulator and fit [3]. EPZ's own B&K 4128C HATS graph shows a broadly similar bass and pinna-gain contour but omits level, channel, smoothing, and tolerance details [1].

At 94 dB SPL, independent THD is below 0.1% at 1 kHz and below 1% across the plotted audible range [3]. At 104 dB SPL, low- and mid-frequency THD generally rises to approximately 0.3–0.7%, reaches up to 0.3% at 1 kHz, and peaks at 3.3% at 10 kHz. The 10 kHz result alone does not establish audible distortion because its harmonics fall mainly at or above 20 kHz and the fixture is least reliable there; the broader rise at 104 dB nevertheless shows reduced linearity at very high level. Impedance also varies from about 42 ohms at 20 Hz to 12 ohms at 1 kHz and 7 ohms at 20 kHz, so response can be more source-dependent than a flat-impedance IEM. Good target tracking and low normal-level distortion support an above-neutral score, while high-level distortion and upper-treble uncertainty prevent a stronger conclusion.

## Technology Level

$$ \Large \text{0.4} $$

The P40 combines mature dynamic, micro-planar, and piezoelectric transducers through a passive four-way crossover in an in-house DLP-printed shell [1][2]. Acoustic integration of four drivers, crossover design, and shell manufacture demonstrate product-specific engineering and accumulated production skill. The cited sources identify no patent, technical paper, proprietary transducer mechanism, or durable barrier to replication, however, and the fully passive analog architecture lacks digital processing or software integration. Technology Level is therefore modest: more involved than a generic single-driver assembly, but based on established techniques that capable competitors can reproduce.

## Cost-Performance

$$ \Large \text{0.4} $$

This site evaluates based solely on functionality and measured performance values, without considering driver types or configurations. The P40's current US price is 159.99 USD [1]. The least-expensive confirmed function-matched solution is a TRUTHEAR GATE at 22.99 USD [6], a 0.78 mm two-pin Tripowin Amber cable with interchangeable 3.5 mm/2.5 mm/4.4 mm plugs at 26.99 USD [7], and a Linsoul carrying case at 8.99 USD [8], totaling 58.97 USD. The cable and case replace the P40's interchangeable 3.5 mm/4.4 mm termination and included case, while the GATE provides the same passive wired-IEM role and detachable 0.78 mm two-pin interface [9].

Audio Science Review's independently measured GATE response is approximately 2 dB below the Harman target at 20–80 Hz, within roughly ±1.5 dB from 100 Hz to 5 kHz, and 4–5 dB below around 6–7 kHz [5], making its target tracking at least comparable to the P40 data [3]. GATE THD stays near or below 0.1% across almost the entire plotted range at both 94 and 104 dB SPL, outperforming the P40's 104 dB result. Its group delay falls from about 6 ms at 20 Hz to near zero by 150 Hz, and its impedance is nearly flat at approximately 29 ohms, both more consistent than the P40 measurements [3][5]. The rigs differ and neither product has controlled passive-isolation data, so the comparison remains provisional, but every available common measurement axis is equal or better for the GATE bundle.

CP = 58.97 USD ÷ 159.99 USD = 0.3686, which rounds to 0.4.

## Reliability & Support

$$ \Large \text{0.4} $$

EPZ's FAQ states a one-year warranty, with a paid Care+ option extending coverage to 18 months [2]. The detachable cable is user-replaceable, but the cited information does not state a parts-supply period, repair pricing, regional service network, RMA rate, MTBF, or statistically useful model-specific failure data. The short documented coverage supports a slightly below-neutral Reliability & Support score; missing failure data and handling comments about the cable are not treated as evidence of product failure.

## Rationality of Design Philosophy

$$ \Large \text{0.4} $$

The P40 is a functional passive IEM, and its detachable cable with interchangeable 3.5 mm and 4.4 mm plugs provides a concrete compatibility benefit [1]. EPZ describes measurements as part of development but leaves final tuning to subjective listening [1][2], so the design narrative is not fully measurement-led. The four-driver crossover and silver-plated cable add material and implementation complexity, while the cited evidence does not establish that either choice improves finished-device frequency response, distortion, isolation, or audibility over simpler approaches. The result remains practical audio equipment, but its allocation toward unverified complexity is less rational than a function- and measurement-first design.

## Advice

The P40 tracks the Harman target reasonably well and controls distortion at ordinary measurement level, but linearity worsens at 104 dB SPL and its upper-treble result is fixture- and fit-sensitive. A current GATE, modular cable, and case bundle restores the P40's user-facing termination and storage functions while providing equal-or-better available measurements for 58.97 USD. The P40 may still appeal as a single coordinated package, but its four-driver architecture and silver-plated cable do not establish a measured fidelity advantage. The official store's conflicting availability indicators and the one-year warranty should be confirmed before purchase.
