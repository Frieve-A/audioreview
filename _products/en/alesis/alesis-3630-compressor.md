## Overview

The Alesis 3630 is a discontinued 1U dual-channel analog compressor/limiter with two noise gates. Each channel provides switchable Peak/RMS detection, Hard/Soft knee operation, manual attack and release, sidechain access, and +4dBu/-10dBV level selection; the two channels can operate independently or as a stereo pair [1]. Its audio connections are unbalanced 1/4-inch jacks. Reverb's current Price Guide estimates 66-101 USD for Good-to-Mint examples, based on 1,640 orders, so 85 USD near the midpoint is used as the representative secondhand price [2].

## Scientific Validity

$$ \Large \text{0.6} $$

The official manual publishes the following performance specifications [1].

| Measurement | Published result | Stated condition |
|---|---:|---|
| Frequency response | 10Hz-30kHz, 0/-0.5dB | Not further stated |
| Dynamic range | Greater than 118dB | A-weighted |
| Signal-to-noise ratio | Greater than 100dB | A-weighted |
| Distortion | Less than 0.05% | +4dBu, 20Hz-20kHz, A-weighted, 6dB compression, nominal attack/release |
| Crosstalk | Less than -85dB | 10kHz |

These figures indicate a wide, flat passband and low stated noise and distortion under the listed conditions. They are manufacturer specifications rather than independently reproduced measurements, however, and the distortion figure is not a THD+N result. Unit-to-unit variation and performance across other gain-reduction, attack, and release settings therefore remain unverified.

## Technology Level

$$ \Large \text{0.2} $$

The manual documents a conventional all-analog gain-control path with Peak/RMS detection, selectable knee behavior, sidechain routing, and discrete front-panel controls [1]. This is a mature and readily reproducible compressor architecture built from standard analog functions, without a documented proprietary semiconductor, new control method, patent, paper, digital control, recall, or software integration. Combining two channels, gates, sidechains, and metering in 1U is useful engineering, but it does not make the underlying technology advanced by current standards.

## Cost-Performance

$$ \Large \text{1.0} $$

Because the 3630 is discontinued, the representative used value of 85 USD is used [2]. No lower-priced product was found that matches its two independent compressor/limiter/gate channels, stereo linking, sidechain access, and published performance.

The closest low-price current product found was the Behringer MDX2600 V2 at 109 USD [3]. It matches the core two-channel compressor/gate/limiter and sidechain functions, adds balanced XLR/TRS connections, de-essing, and automatic timing, and therefore exceeds the target functionally [4]. Its official specifications list a 10Hz-70kHz frequency range at +0/-3dB, 115dB unweighted S/N, and 0.02% typical THD at +4dBu, 1kHz, unity gain [4]. The Behringer has the wider stated bandwidth and lower numerical distortion, but the figures are not like-for-like: its THD is specified at one frequency with no gain reduction, whereas the Alesis distortion limit covers 20Hz-20kHz during 6dB compression; the S/N weighting and bandwidth tolerances also differ. The Behringer costs more than the target, and the search found no cheaper candidate that clears both the functional and performance requirements. CP is therefore 1.0.

## Reliability & Support

$$ \Large \text{0.3} $$

The original warranty covered parts and labor for one year and applied only to the original retail purchaser [1]. That warranty no longer protects ordinary secondhand units. The official manual remains available, and the simple analog architecture avoids firmware dependence, but Alesis's current support search returns no result for “3630 Compressor” and does not document model-specific repair service or replacement-parts availability [5]. No model-wide failure-rate study or other statistical longevity evidence was found. Ordinary analog components alone are not evidence of either unusual longevity or an abnormal model-specific failure risk. The expired warranty and uncertain current support yield 0.3.

## Rationality of Design Philosophy

$$ \Large \text{0.8} $$

The design is tightly aligned with its practical goal: two complete dynamics channels, gates, sidechains, stereo linking, level switching, bypass, and gain-reduction/input/output metering are concentrated in one rack space [1]. Direct controls keep every processing parameter immediately accessible, while the analog signal path avoids conversion latency and software dependence. The use of established circuitry and unbalanced jacks reflects cost control, with resources directed toward a broad working feature set instead of cosmetic or prestige features. Judged independently of technical novelty and measurement completeness, this functional coherence and cost allocation yield 0.8.

## Advice

At roughly 85 USD used, the 3630 remains attractive when two channels, gates, sidechains, and hands-on analog control are the priorities. Check both channels, switches, pots, meters, sidechain jacks, and the external power supply before purchase because the original warranty has expired and current model-specific service is not documented. Buyers who need balanced connections, a new-product warranty, or additional processing should compare the Behringer MDX2600 V2, while buyers who require independently verified noise and distortion performance should wait for suitable third-party bench data or choose a product with such measurements.
