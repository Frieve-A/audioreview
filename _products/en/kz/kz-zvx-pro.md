## Overview

The KZ ZVX Pro is a wired in-ear monitor from KZ (Knowledge Zenith). It uses an inner-magnetic dynamic driver in a full-metal alloy housing with a detachable 0.75mm 2-pin OFC cable. The documented connection is 3.5mm, with no-microphone and inline-microphone cable options; the no-microphone version was offered on a 15-day pre-order at 17.99 USD when checked [1][4].

## Scientific Validity

$$ \Large \text{0.5} $$

The manufacturer publishes a frequency response range of 20–40,000 Hz (no deviation tolerance stated), with no THD, S/N ratio, or distortion figures [1].

Reference Audio Analyzer provides frequency-response measurements on SIEC, IEC711 Coupler, and experimental type 4.3 artificial-ear fixtures. Its IEC711 graph, shown with 1/3-octave smoothing and the Harman In-Ear 2019 target, indicates moderate departures from the target rather than uniformly neutral reproduction [2]. Public left/right data from Ineras/Squiglink likewise show bass roughly 7–9 dB above 1 kHz and a broad rise centered near 3 kHz, with close channel agreement [3]. Both sources show structure around 8 kHz, but that region is strongly affected by coupler and insertion-depth resonances, so it is not treated as a confirmed audible peak. No third-party THD or passive-isolation result is available. The verified frequency-response deviations are audible, but incomplete distortion and isolation data prevent a more complete fidelity assessment.

## Technology Level

$$ \Large \text{0.3} $$

KZ's cited product materials describe internal driver refinement, but the implementation is otherwise industry-mature: the inner-magnetic dynamic driver with a claimed 0.1mm magnetic gap uses a conventional electrodynamic design common across wired IEMs; the 0.75mm 2-pin detachable connector and OFC flat cable are ubiquitous; the all-metal housing is not technically unusual. The cited product materials do not document proprietary patents [1]. The device is purely passive and analog with no digital or software integration. The 40 kHz upper frequency specification covers a range beyond human auditory perception. Internal driver refinement is a modest technical positive, but the mature architecture, ease of replication, and absence of meaningfully differentiated technology bring the overall assessment considerably below average.

## Cost-Performance

$$ \Large \text{1.0} $$

This site evaluates based solely on functionality and measured performance values, without considering driver types or configurations.

The no-microphone KZ ZVX Pro was offered for pre-order at 17.99 USD when checked [4]. The current Tanchjim Zero Ultima 3.5mm version adds an inline microphone and is in stock at 19.99 USD [6][7]. Paul Wasabii's public frequency-response graph shows a broad target-style contour with less bass elevation than the ZVX Pro data, while its official specification reports THD of 0.05% at 1 kHz and 94 dB [5][6]. Because the products were measured on different fixtures, the magnitude comparison is provisional; neither has a published passive-isolation result. Even so, the Zero Ultima provides equal-or-better user-facing functions and at least comparable published frequency-response performance. No cheaper current product meeting those requirements was established, so the ZVX Pro is the least expensive verified option and CP is 1.0.

## Reliability & Support

$$ \Large \text{0.5} $$

Linsoul provides a 1-year warranty for the earphone and 3 months for cables and adapters, which is short coverage [8]. The product's construction is inherently robust: a single driver in an all-metal shell with a detachable cable has few failure modes, and the standard 0.75mm 2-pin connector makes replacement cables widely available. Support is primarily retailer- or distributor-based. No statistical failure-rate or longevity data specific to the ZVX Pro is available. The robust, serviceable construction balances the limited warranty coverage, supporting an average overall assessment.

## Rationality of Design Philosophy

$$ \Large \text{0.3} $$

KZ applies iterative driver refinement, mass production, and simple wired construction, which are cost-rational choices. However, the stated design philosophy also emphasizes subjective genre-specific experiences, premium metal aesthetics, and a 40 kHz response limit that lies beyond human hearing [1]. The claimed 0.1mm magnetic gap is a rational attempt to reduce distortion, but no product-level distortion result is published to quantify the outcome. Independent graphs confirm audible tuning departures through the bass and upper midrange [2][3]. The combination of efficient construction with inaudible and aesthetic marketing priorities supports the below-average rationality score.

## Advice

The KZ ZVX Pro offers durable all-metal construction and a user-serviceable detachable cable at a 17.99 USD pre-order price. Third-party graphs show audible bass and upper-midrange departures from target reproduction, while the apparent 8 kHz structure is too fixture-dependent to treat as conclusive [2][3]. The Tanchjim Zero Ultima adds an inline microphone and offers at least comparable published frequency-response performance at 19.99 USD [5][6][7], making it the better-documented measured-performance choice for 2.00 USD more. The 1-year earphone warranty and 3-month cable warranty are short, although the simple hardware and replaceable cable partially offset that limitation [8].
