## Overview

The Furman M-8X2 is a Merit-series AC power distributor for instrument and studio racks. Its nine switched outlets (eight rear, one front), resettable breaker and protection-status indicator combine distribution, basic surge suppression and RFI/EMI filtering in a 1U chassis approximately 95 mm deep [1][2][3]. It uses MOV protection and standard filtering, not SMP/LiFT. It is not a voltage regulator, and its documentation does not specify sustained over/undervoltage shutdown [3][4]. The Japanese distributor lists the model as discontinued; remaining dealer stock must be distinguished from current production [4].

The same model name covers different regional specifications:

| Manufacturer specification | US version | Japanese version |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Supply | 120 VAC, 60 Hz | 100 VAC, 50/60 Hz |
| Total current | 15 A | 15 A |
| Surge protection | Line–neutral, 150 J, 12,000 A peak | Line–neutral, 170 J, 12,000 A peak |
| Noise attenuation, 200 kHz–10 MHz | >23 dB in linked datasheet/manual; >26 dB on product page | 23 dB |
| Captive grounded cord | 6 ft, 14 AWG | 1.8 m, 14 AWG |

These are published electrical specifications, not independent audio measurements. The US attenuation discrepancy remains unresolved; the Japanese page also specifies a 400 V maximum clamp voltage, without the pulse-test conditions needed for a detailed cross-product comparison [1][2][3][4].

## Scientific Validity

$$ \Large \text{0.5} $$

This is basic AC distribution and protection equipment, with no demonstrated fidelity improvement for an already normally operating audio system. Furman does advertise quieter sound, specifically discussing incoming interference, pops and radio pickup [1]. Reducing such interference is a different claim from universally improving an amplifier or DAC's sound. Published RF attenuation cannot be translated directly into an audio S/N or distortion improvement. No controlled downstream audio measurements or audibility trials were verified, so the magnitude and audibility of any improvement cannot be evaluated from the available data. Its practical distribution and protection functions remain separate from that uncertainty.

## Technology Level

$$ \Large \text{0.2} $$

The implementation consists of mature MOV suppression, passive interference filtering and electromechanical distribution controls [3][4]. The available documentation does not establish design ownership or OEM/ODM arrangements. No model-specific patented implementation or exceptional technical know-how was established. The publicly described functions are readily replicated in ordinary power-distribution products; there is no distinctive contemporary digital or software integration. Compact packaging is useful, but does not itself demonstrate advanced power-processing technology.

## Cost-Performance

$$ \Large \text{1.0} $$

The US reference price is 139.95 USD, the manufacturer's listed price on 2026-09-12, but its store is out of stock [1]. The Reverb new-unit listing is also sold [5]. No currently purchasable US new-unit offer was verified, so this is a listed-price comparison rather than a current purchase quote. The comparison covers nine grounded, switched, filtered and surge-protected AC outlets, a front-accessible master switch and status indication, a 15 A total load, a supplied mains cord and 1U mounting in a standard rack. Electrical ratings must match the country of use.

The lowest-priced catalog-based comparison option identified for those electrical and rack functions is the Furman M-8Lx, advertised at 169.95 USD per unit and marked in stock by BAI Distributors [6][7]. Delivery charges were not established; its advertised unit price alone already exceeds the M-8X2 reference price. It supplies nine outlets, the same 120 V/15 A, line–neutral 150 J/12,000 A protection ratings and >26 dB filtering over 200 kHz–10 MHz, with a captive cord and rack ears; the shared manual also applies the same outlet-current limits [2][3][6]. Its approximately 190 mm depth needs more rack clearance, so it is not a substitute in every shallow case. Its added lights do not make it cheaper than the M-8X2.

Less expensive candidates do not establish the full specification set. Rockville RDF9U supplies nine outlets and a 15 A rating, but publishes 1,200 A peak impulse current without matching pulse conditions or a quantitative filter-attenuation specification [8]. This does not prove tenfold worse real-world surge protection. CyberPower CPS1215RMS offers twelve outlets and 1,800 J protection, but its 150 kHz–150 MHz filter-band specification gives no attenuation in dB; wider coverage alone does not establish equal filtering [9]. A general-purpose Belkin SRA009p12tt8 plus a rack shelf was also considered: “up to 75 dB” is not a guaranteed minimum throughout the required band [10]. Adding a shelf does not resolve that evidence gap or establish a complete 1U installation. No validated, safely specified add-on filter bundle was identified. These overseas versions also cannot automatically substitute for the Japanese 100 V model.

At the cited reference price and on the available catalog evidence, the M-8X2 is the lowest-cost option demonstrated to meet this function set; no cheaper equivalent finished product or complete bundle was confirmed. CP is provisionally 1.0, subject to the unresolved US new-stock availability. This is not a claim of superior measured audio performance, and the US 23/26 dB discrepancy limits precision.

## Reliability & Support

$$ \Large \text{0.6} $$

The US manufacturer specifies a three-year limited warranty for the original purchaser from an authorized dealer, with proof of purchase. Covered products are repaired or replaced; the purchaser pays shipping to the service center [1][3]. This does not automatically establish Japanese or parallel-import coverage. Steel construction and simple circuitry are practical, but the surge-protection components are not indefinitely immune to damage: the manual requires service if the Protection OK indicator goes out [3]. Model-specific failure rates, guaranteed parts-support duration and typical repair costs were not verified. Discontinuation alone does not demonstrate that repair support has ended.

## Rationality of Design Philosophy

$$ \Large \text{0.6} $$

Resources are directed toward useful distribution, protection, filtering and rack installation rather than exotic materials or unsupported sound-enhancement mechanisms. The Japanese distributor documents a reduction from the preceding M-8x's 190 mm depth and 2.2 kg mass to 95 mm and 1.8 kg, providing a concrete portability and shallow-rack benefit [4]. Omitting metering and lights is reasonable for users who do not need them; those features are useful elsewhere, not inherently nonfunctional. The interference-control rationale is physically plausible, but it does not justify promising an audio upgrade on clean mains. This is an incremental, purpose-focused design rather than an advanced integrated power-management system.

## Advice

Choose it for compact rack distribution and basic protection, not an assumed improvement in an already quiet system. Verify the actual regional label, grounded outlet, load and remaining-stock warranty. The US manual specifies a 15 A total and 12 A per outlet, but also contains both 20 A and 15 A supply-receptacle wording; confirm the applicable installation requirement with the manufacturer or a qualified installer rather than assuming the descriptions are interchangeable [3]. Do not transfer those US instructions automatically to the Japanese 100 V version. The captive cord needs no separate IEC lead. Filtering is not voltage regulation, and neither a fitting plug nor a surge indicator establishes protection against every power fault.
