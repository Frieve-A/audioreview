## Overview

Lark Studio is a Chengdu, China-based maker of custom-fit and universal-fit in-ear monitors, founded in 2018 by three audiologists whose combined experience was reported as more than 18 years [3][4]. The manufacturer's own website currently fails certificate validation, so active availability was checked through e☆earphone's operating custom-order form instead. That form currently lists LSIV at 68,000円, LSVI at 110,000円, LSX Standard Edition universal at 258,500円, LSXC Standard Edition custom at 330,000円, and higher-priced Splendor editions, with ear-impression, shell, and model selections still active [1]. This review uses LSIV, LSVI, and LSX Standard universal as entry, middle, and flagship representatives.

## Scientific Validity

$$ \Large \text{0.4} $$

No independent frequency-response, distortion, isolation, or noise measurement was found for the current LSIV or LSVI, so their fidelity cannot be determined from public evidence. The current retailer listing does not identify an acoustic revision to LSX, so two available independent LSX records are used as the best measurement evidence. Reference Audio Analyzer's SIEC summary plots the measured left/right average against Harman In-Ear 2019: the LSX is about 5 dB above the target near 200 Hz, roughly 8–13 dB below it around 3–4 kHz, and about 8 dB low near 7 kHz; its recommended-EQ plot correspondingly reduces the low-midrange by several decibels and raises 3–4 kHz by as much as about 10 dB [5]. Crinacle's graph shows broadly matching left and right channels but the same strong bass balance, deep 3–5 kHz depression, a 7–8 kHz peak, and irregular response above 10 kHz [6]. Those large mid/treble errors establish poor target fidelity even though LSX THD and S/N remain unknown. Weighting LSIV at 0.25, LSVI at 0.35, and the flagship LSX at 0.40 gives a company-level Scientific Validity result of 0.4.

## Technology Level

$$ \Large \text{0.3} $$

The documented models use custom acrylic shells or universal earpieces, detachable two-pin cables, passive multi-driver arrays, acoustic bores, and passive crossovers [1][3][4]. Third-party reports describe an in-house tuning and production operation, so the products are not treated as unmodified catalog shells. Custom fabrication and repeatable crossover integration require practical know-how, but the disclosed architecture is mature, purely passive, and readily reproducible by other IEM makers. No patent-backed mechanism, recent signal-processing technology, or durable engineering distinction is disclosed in the accessible material. The result is competent specialist manufacturing without evidence of advanced or difficult-to-replicate technology, supporting a Technology Level score of 0.3.

## Cost-Performance

$$ \Large \text{0.5} $$

This site evaluates solely by user-facing functions and measured performance, without considering driver types or configurations. Three current representatives are weighted by their roles: LSIV 0.25, LSVI 0.35, and LSX Standard universal 0.40. The flagship receives the largest share as the company's highest-specification and highest-priced offering, the middle model follows, and the entry model receives the remainder. Japanese retail prices are converted for this English review at the ECB rates for 2026-07-16 (1 EUR = 1.1467 USD, 0.84873 GBP, and 185.99円; 1 USD = 162.196円) [1][10]. Comparisons for the unmeasured custom models are necessarily provisional and use the best catalog evidence available.

**LSIV custom (419.25 USD; 68,000円)** [1][2]: The lowest-priced current custom-fit candidate confirmed with an active order path was ACS Classic Engage at 399 GBP including VAT, or 539.08 USD [7]. It requires ear impressions, is custom-made, and uses a detachable two-pin cable; its maker describes flat response and no harmonic distortion, but provides no numeric test conditions. Because LSIV is less expensive and no cheaper custom-fit model with definitively equal-or-better published performance was confirmed, LSIV is the cheapest established equivalent and its CP is 1.0.

**LSVI custom (678.19 USD; 110,000円)** [1] vs **ACS Classic Engage (539.08 USD; 399 GBP)** [7]: Both provide passive custom-fit monitoring and a detachable two-pin cable. Neither has a comparable independent measurement set; ACS's flat-response and no-harmonic-distortion statements are manufacturer claims, so equality is provisional rather than measured like-for-like. CP = 539.08 ÷ 678.19 = 0.7949.

**LSX Standard universal (1,593.75 USD; 258,500円)** [1] vs **Truthear x Crinacle ZERO:RED (64.99 USD)** [8]: Both are passive universal wired IEMs with detachable 0.78 mm two-pin cables and no DSP, wireless, or ANC functions. ZERO:RED's official catalog lists 20 Hz–20 kHz effective response and THD below 1% at 1 kHz [8]. In nine independently published measurement graphs, its left/right response broadly follows the measurement target, with approximately -4 to +1 dB error through most of 20 Hz–10 kHz apart from the upper-frequency edge; THD stays generally below 0.5% at 94, 104, and 114 dB SPL [9]. This is clearly better than the LSX's measured 3–4 kHz deficit and other large response errors [5][6]. CP = 64.99 ÷ 1,593.75 = 0.04078.

Weighted CP = (1.0 x 0.25) + (0.7949 x 0.35) + (0.04078 x 0.40) = 0.5445, which rounds to 0.5.

## Reliability & Support

$$ \Large \text{0.5} $$

The currently orderable LSIV listing specifies a one-year warranty, and the Japanese retailer maintains an active ear-impression and custom-order process [1][2]. The passive products contain no batteries, firmware, or exposed moving mechanisms, which limits common wear-out modes, while their detachable cables can be replaced. Earlier reviews nevertheless note externally exposed two-pin connectors as a potential mechanical vulnerability rather than a documented production failure [3][4]. No company-wide warranty term for every model, repair-price schedule, parts-supply period, statistical failure rate, RMA rate, or global manufacturer service network could be verified from accessible current sources. The short documented warranty is balanced by simple passive construction, placing Reliability & Support at 0.5.

## Rationality of Design Philosophy

$$ \Large \text{0.3} $$

Custom shells and replaceable cables serve real fit, isolation, and maintenance needs, and the continued made-to-order process is rational for users who require custom earpieces [1]. The product hierarchy, however, is built primarily around larger passive driver arrays and increasingly elaborate shell editions. Accessible evidence does not show that higher-priced models progress toward better measured fidelity; the measured flagship instead has large response errors [5][6], while current lower models have no independent results. The Standard and Splendor variants also allocate substantial price differences to presentation and cosmetic execution without documented fidelity improvement [1]. This is a functional but conservative direction, with meaningful customization undermined by quantity- and appearance-led differentiation that is not tied to demonstrated acoustic progress. Rationality of Design Philosophy is therefore 0.3.

## Advice

Lark Studio remains orderable through a Japanese custom-IEM retailer, but buyers should distinguish custom fit and appearance from verified fidelity. The LSX's available graphs show major response departures, while LSIV and LSVI lack independent measurements. LSIV is competitively priced among confirmed custom-fit options, but LSVI and especially the universal LSX face cheaper alternatives with equal user functions and stronger published evidence. The inaccessible manufacturer website and the fact that only a one-year retailer warranty could be confirmed should also be considered before an international order.
