## Overview

GRAS Sound & Vibration A/S was founded in 1994 in Holte, Denmark, by Gunnar Rasmussen, who had previously spent decades as a measurement-microphone engineer at Brüel & Kjær, together with family members [1]. GRAS manufactures precision measurement microphones, preamplifiers, ear and head-torso simulators (KEMAR), and calibration systems for acoustic, NVH, aerospace, automotive, and audiology test applications, rather than consumer audio products [1]. The company was acquired by Audio Precision in 2020 and now operates within the Battery Ventures-backed Axiometrix Solutions group, with a catalog spanning over 100 SKUs distributed in more than 40 countries [1].

## Scientific Validity

$$ \Large \text{0.6} $$

Third-party accredited-laboratory measurements (SP Technical Research Institute of Sweden/RISE, Report 3P03218, four units tested) [2] on GRAS's 46AE 1/2-inch free-field standard microphone set show an average equivalent noise level of 15.0 dB(A) (14.3-15.6 dB(A)), maximum SPL at 3% microphone-induced distortion and 350 Hz averaging 138.8 dB SPL (138-139 dB SPL), and a -3 dB low-frequency point of 0.7-0.8 Hz. Across the four samples, the measured frequency-response curves span -0.5 to +0.2 dB through 10 kHz and -1.2 to +0.4 dB at 20 kHz. These results support strong reference-microphone performance, with especially wide dynamic and low-frequency capability [2].

The broader current range is supported mainly by manufacturer specifications and is therefore provisional evidence. The 40HL specifies 6.5 dB(A) self-noise and a 110 dB upper limit for low-level measurements; the 40AZ specifies 14 dB(A), 134 dB maximum SPL, and extended low-frequency operation; and the current 40PH-10 specifies a 33 dB(A) lower dynamic-range limit, a 135 dB upper limit, and 10 Hz-20 kHz bandwidth for array use [1]. These are rational intended-use trade-offs rather than general-purpose omissions, but no independent measurements of the three specialist products were found.

The independently measured 46AE provides strong scientific support for the core microphone business, while the specialist specifications do not extend that independent evidence to the entire catalog.

## Technology Level

$$ \Large \text{0.8} $$

GRAS is a vertically integrated, in-house-engineered manufacturer of laboratory-standard measurement microphones, ear/head-torso simulators, and calibration systems, rather than an OEM/ODM reseller [1]. Its EQset DSP-equalized microphone technology flattens frequency response and fixes gain and sensitivity across units, removing the need to apply individual correction data in its intended measurement chains [3]. This is a meaningful in-house development supported by more than three decades of measurement-microphone engineering, an extensive IEC 61094-conforming catalog, and ruggedized automotive-NVH microphones co-developed with Volvo Cars [1][3].

Public sources do not establish a patent-backed moat or provide independent competitor benchmarking of EQset's uncertainty improvement. Industry awards document recognition, not measured technical superiority [3]. The score therefore reflects substantial in-house breadth and differentiated engineering while leaving the durability of its competitive advantage neutral.

## Cost-Performance

$$ \Large \text{1.0} $$

Two current representative products are weighted by their importance to the company's audio-measurement business: 70% for the 46AE reference microphone set and 30% for the 42AG multifunction sound calibrator. Current Pageotech single-unit listings price the 46AE at 1,896.84 USD [6] and the 42AG at 1,929.03 USD [7].

For the 46AE, the independent SP/RISE comparison supplies like-for-like measured evidence [2]. PCB Piezotronics 378B02 has lower average noise (14.35 versus 15.0 dB(A)) but a materially higher -3 dB low-frequency point (1.9-2.4 versus 0.7-0.8 Hz). Brüel & Kjær 4189-A-021 has a still higher low-frequency point (9.6-11.6 Hz), while BSWA MPA201 has higher average noise (15.95 dB(A)). None is equal or better across the directly measured core functions, so each is disqualified before price comparison [2][5]. The 46AE is therefore treated as the lowest-priced available product at its own measured performance level.

For the 42AG, a function-only search for a portable IEC 60942:2017 Class 1 calibrator with 250/1,000 Hz and 94/114 dB output identified the CRYSOUND CRY3018. It matches those core functions and published accuracy and adds USB-C charging, but its official page is quote-only and provides no verifiable current price [7][8]. With no qualified lower-priced candidate carrying a verifiable price, the 42AG is likewise treated as the lowest-priced available product at its performance level.

## Reliability & Support

$$ \Large \text{0.9} $$

GRAS documents a 5-year warranty on its core precision measurement microphones, preamplifiers, and special microphone sets; accessories such as the 42AG carry 2 years [4][7]. The company also documents HALT during development and provides diaphragm replacement and recalibration services rather than treating a damaged microphone as irreparable [1].

In the independent SP/RISE environmental tests, the four 46AE samples recorded temperature coefficients from -0.014 to -0.008 dB/°C and humidity coefficients from -0.002 to 0 dB/%RH. All remained operable and met the manufacturer's temperature and humidity limits. In the separate A-weighted noise-floor comparison, three of the four cartridge results met the manufacturer limit and the fourth was indeterminable within the report's 1 dB expanded uncertainty; one BSWA sample failed after its diaphragm tension ring came loose [2]. This is useful sample-level stress evidence, but the report is not a field failure-rate study, so population reliability remains unquantified.

Manufacturer support is global, with calibration and service centers across Europe, Asia, and North America, and GRAS states that technical support continues through a product's lifetime and for up to seven years after discontinuation [4]. Exact repair pricing, parts availability, and independent response-time data are not published and therefore remain neutral.

## Rationality of Design Philosophy

$$ \Large \text{0.9} $$

GRAS's product philosophy is built around standards-conformant, traceable, repeatable measurement rather than subjective or brand-driven audio design: its core lines are built and specified directly to IEC 61094 and IEC 60318-4/60318-7 / ANSI S3.36/S3.25 standards, every unit is factory-calibrated with traceable equipment, and further accredited (ISO/IEC 17025) calibration services are offered after sale, with no subjective or audiophile marketing language, consistent with GRAS selling no consumer listening product [1].

New-model development — EQset DSP calibration launched in 2023, and ruggedized automotive-NVH microphones co-developed with Volvo Cars — directs engineering cost toward measurable functional improvement, namely reduced calibration uncertainty and environmental durability, rather than aesthetics, materials, or brand value, and represents a genuinely innovative rather than conservative direction for the category, reflected in industry innovation awards [3]. As precision laboratory-grade instrumentation built to explicit international standards, GRAS's products have clear, uncontested justification for existing as dedicated equipment; there is no general-purpose substitute for an accredited reference microphone [1].

EQset's precise measurement-uncertainty benefit is presented only as a manufacturer claim, without independent third-party quantification [3]. That limits evidentiary confidence but does not undermine the standards-driven rationale for the product line.

## Advice

The 46AE is the strongest independently supported choice in the range for buyers needing a free-field standard microphone with very wide low-frequency and dynamic capability. The 40HL, 40AZ, and 40PH-10 address low-level, low-frequency, and array work respectively; their different operating ranges are application choices, but buyers should seek project-specific validation because the public evidence reviewed here is manufacturer-supplied. The 42AG is a flexible Class 1 field calibrator, while its 2-year accessory warranty is shorter than the 5-year coverage on core microphones. Long-term institutional buyers also benefit from documented repair, calibration, and post-discontinuation support.
