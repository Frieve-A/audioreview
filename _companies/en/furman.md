## Overview

Furman is a Nice North America power-management brand serving stage, studio, installed-AV and home-theater users [1]. This review represents three distinct applications with the SS-6B portable power block, PL-8C illuminated rack conditioner and IT-Reference 20i high-current home-system conditioner. These are current products offered for purchase; they are not interchangeable merely because all distribute AC power [2][3][4]. The catalog also includes sequencing and remote-control products such as the CN-1800S, and battery backup with voltage regulation in the F1500-UPS [1].

## Scientific Validity

$$ \Large \text{0.2} $$

Surge suppression, overload protection and removal of conducted electrical interference are real engineering functions. Their specifications describe the power supply, however, not the noise or distortion at a connected amplifier's audio output. An audible improvement depends on the incoming disturbance and the connected equipment's susceptibility; it cannot be inferred from an AC attenuation figure alone.

The SS-6B provides basic distribution and protection at a modest price. The PL-8C and IT-Reference 20i add electrical functions but also make much stronger sound-quality claims: exceptional audio clarity for the PL-8C, and improved detail, imaging and perceived amplifier power for the IT-Reference 20i [3][4]. Controlled listening tests or matched downstream audio measurements substantiating those claims were not identified. This limits their demonstrated audio value without negating their electrical protection.

Furman's published LiFT graphs show a smoother decline in filtered AC-noise levels with frequency than the conventional-filter example [12]. They do not identify the tested product, source/load conditions or connected audio equipment. These electrical filter traces therefore cannot establish the advertised changes in reproduced sound.

## Technology Level

$$ \Large \text{0.5} $$

Furman combines surge suppression, filtering, voltage detection and load switching, with more elaborate filtering and bank separation in its upper range [2][3][4]. These are mature electrical-engineering techniques, not evidence of a new audio-reproduction technology. Nevertheless, implementing different current ratings, protection functions and installation controls represents accumulated power-management expertise. The company is not exclusively passive or analog: the CN-1800S provides sequenced outlets, RS-232 control and monitoring, and optional BlueBOLT connectivity, although it was listed out of stock when checked [1]. BlueBOLT adds cloud monitoring, remote power cycling, scheduled shutdown and network checks with corrective actions [1]. This is useful integration of power hardware and software, although these established functions do not demonstrate a long technical lead. Branded circuit names alone do not establish adoption of in-house patents or exclusive design ownership.

## Cost-Performance

$$ \Large \text{1.0} $$

The three applications receive equal weights of one third: portable distribution, protected rack operation with lighting, and a high-current multi-component home system. This balances distinct uses rather than assuming unpublished sales shares. Prices are current new-unit US listings for the 120V models, before location-dependent sales tax; used stock, coupons and optional accessories are excluded. B&H lists the SS-6B and SS-6 in stock, Sweetwater lists the PL-8C in stock, and Audio Advisor lists the new IT-Reference 20i for dispatch in 1–3 business days [5][6][7]. Electrical comparisons below are provisional manufacturer-specification comparisons, not independently verified audio-performance comparisons.

**SS-6B — 36.40 USD.** The cheapest confirmed equivalent is Furman's SS-6 at 33.35 USD [5]. Both provide six NEMA 5-15R outlets, 120V/15A operation, a 15-foot cord, illuminated switching and overload protection. Their manufacturer specifications match at 400V voltage-protection rating, 540J surge-energy rating and at least 20dB filtering from 100kHz to 1MHz [2]. Neither provides sustained over/under-voltage shutdown. Their different outlet layouts do not remove these functions. The cheaper Watson MSP6-15 at 29.99 USD explicitly lacks EMI/RFI filtering [5], so it is not equivalent. CP = 33.35 USD / 36.40 USD = 0.91620879.

**PL-8C — 275.00 USD.** Equivalence requires 120V/15A operation, nine NEMA 5-15R outlets in two filtered banks, a 10-foot cord, dimmable front rack lights and a rear BNC lamp-power connection. Its manual specifies blocking startup below 85–90V and disconnecting above 140V during operation, followed by manual reset; the website's abbreviated 90V under-voltage entry should not be read as an unconditional continuous low-voltage cutoff. Initial clamping is specified at 188VAC peak under a 3,000A pulse, with attenuation of 10dB at 10kHz, 40dB at 100kHz and 50dB at 500kHz [3]. The low-cost Pyle PCO850 has nine outlets but no specified sustained voltage cutoff and no rack lighting [8]. Adding a lamp does not supply the missing protection. Nor does adding a basic filtered strip establish the PL-8C's frequency-specific attenuation and cutoff behavior. No lower-cost single product or completed set satisfying these requirements was established; the PL-8C is therefore the lowest-priced confirmed equivalent option, with CP = 1.0.

**IT-Reference 20i — 4,645.95 USD.** The current product page specifies 120V and a 20A maximum rating, eight GFCI-protected NEMA 5-15R plus four NEMA 5-20R outlets, five separated power banks, telephone and three coaxial protection paths, and 140V ±3V shutdown. It lists no under-voltage shutdown or voltage regulation. It also specifies 188VAC peak initial clamping at 3,000A; differential attenuation above 40dB at 10–100kHz and 80dB at 100kHz–1GHz over 0.05–100Ω line impedance; and common-mode attenuation on the video outlets above 75dB at 10Hz–50kHz and 40dB at 50kHz–1MHz [4]. These are electrical requirements, not a requirement to use Furman's particular circuit construction.

There is a material documentation conflict: the manual linked from that current page, bearing a 2006 copyright, states 12–20A continuous output depending on load reactance, no sustained over/under-voltage shutdown, and common-mode attenuation above 95dB rather than 75dB [4] (pp. 3, 8, 10). The comparison provisionally uses the current web specifications, but does not assume an unconditional 20A continuous output. The applicable hardware revision, cutoff function and attenuation need manufacturer confirmation.

General-purpose Eaton LCR2400 provides 14 NEMA 5-15R outlets and voltage regulation, with 16A continuous output on a 20A supply. That current figure alone does not establish inferiority to the Furman; however, Eaton's published 20/40/75dB filtering entries do not establish the required mode-and-frequency attenuation [9]. Juice Goose RX 100-20 supports 20A, but provides eight outlets and shuts down above 150V rather than the currently advertised 140V; its published high-frequency normal-mode minimum is 60dB, not the IT-Reference's specified 80dB band [10]. Additional distribution outlets and signal-line protectors can address connector counts, but not the missing cutoff and filtering requirements. Series-connected filters cannot simply have their advertised dB values added: equivalent end-to-end attenuation under the stated impedances must be established. No complete, adequately rated lower-cost set was established. The IT-Reference 20i is provisionally treated as the lowest-priced confirmed equivalent option, with CP = 1.0, conditional on the advertised specification set; this is not evidence of superior sound.

Using the unrounded product results, company CP = (0.91620879 + 1.0 + 1.0) / 3 = 0.97206960, rounded to 1.0. The two full scores are conditional on the documented functional requirements and available specifications, not proof that every buyer needs those capabilities.

## Reliability & Support

$$ \Large \text{0.6} $$

The representative products have markedly different warranties: one year for SS-6B, five years for PL-8C and five years for IT-Reference 20i [11]. Many main product families thus offer long warranties, although entry-level coverage is shorter. Construction varies across the range: steel enclosures offer physical protection, while voltage-sensing circuits, switching components, surge suppressors and UPS batteries have different failure and replacement risks. A manufacturer warranty should not be mistaken for a measured service life.

The wider range also needs model-specific checking: Merit and Elite receive three years; most Contractor products receive 15 years, but PS-8RE III receives three; PST-2+6/PST-6 receive one year, whereas PST-8/PST-8D receive three. F1500-UPS coverage is three years for the unit and two for its batteries [11]. Authorized-dealer purchase and proof of purchase are important warranty conditions. Comparable RMA/MTBF statistics, guaranteed long-term parts availability and internationally uniform repair terms were not established, so exceptional longevity or global service coverage is not assumed.

## Rationality of Design Philosophy

$$ \Large \text{0.6} $$

Combining protection, outlet distribution and rack lighting serves a clear installation need; sequencing and remote operation address real equipment-management tasks [1][3]. BlueBOLT uses internet and software control to restore unresponsive equipment remotely and schedule shutdown during idle hours, reducing manual intervention and unnecessary power use [1]. Spending on these functions and on specified electrical filtering has a practical rationale. The approach remains conservative, extending established power-management methods rather than introducing a fundamentally new solution.

The weak point is the leap from better AC conditions to broadly promised improvements in musical detail or perceived amplifier power [3][4]. The design rationale for avoiding a damaging surge is different from the rationale for improving the sound of equipment already operating correctly. Useful integration and protection therefore coexist with overextended audio marketing.

## Advice

Choose by required current, outlet type, voltage protection and operating environment. SS-6B suits basic portable distribution; PL-8C adds rack lighting and voltage protection. Neither those functions nor the IT-Reference 20i's more extensive filtering guarantees an audible upgrade. Before buying the IT-Reference 20i for voltage cutoff or a high continuous load, obtain confirmation resolving its conflicting specifications. These comparisons cover US 120V models, not automatic compatibility with Japanese 100V supplies. None of the three representative products regulates output voltage; buyers needing that function must select a model that explicitly provides it.
