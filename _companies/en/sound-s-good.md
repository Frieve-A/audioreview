## Overview

Sound's Good is a Canadian consumer-audio brand founded in 2019 as a market stall at Quebec City's Jean-Talon Market. It has since developed an online store and a network of more than 50 retail locations across Canada [1]. Its current audio range includes true-wireless earbuds (SG Oasis, Ultimate V6, and SG Mini), ANC over-ear headphones (SG Session), and a portable Bluetooth speaker (SG Balade) [2][3][4][5][6].

## Scientific Validity

$$ \Large \text{0.5} $$

No independent measurement set was found for the five representative current products. Sound's Good claims maximum ANC attenuation of 44 dB for SG Oasis and 43 dB for SG Session, but gives no frequency-dependent attenuation curve, fixture, test signal, or other conditions needed to interpret those maxima [2][3]. Ultimate V6 and SG Mini list only a nominal 20 Hz–20 kHz range without a deviation tolerance [4][5], while SG Balade publishes no frequency-response result [6]. The company also provides no THD, THD+N, S/N, dynamic-range, IMD, or crosstalk results for these products. The available catalog information therefore does not establish whether any representative product attains high-fidelity reproduction, so Scientific Validity cannot be determined beyond an evidence-neutral score of 0.5.

## Technology Level

$$ \Large \text{0.4} $$

The current range combines contemporary but widely available technologies: Bluetooth 5.2–5.4, ANC, multipoint connection, app-based EQ, and ordinary ingress-protection measures [2][3][4][5][6]. These are useful integrations, but the company discloses no proprietary implementation, patent-backed feature, technical paper, or in-house design detail that would distinguish its engineering from readily reproducible market-standard products. Design ownership is not established by the public material and is therefore not assumed in either direction. The range is technically current enough for its intended uses, but its disclosed differentiation appears easy for competitors to reproduce, supporting a Technology Level score of 0.4.

## Cost-Performance

$$ \Large \text{0.9} $$

Cost-Performance was evaluated across five representative products spanning the current range. Sound's Good sells in Canadian dollars; the prices below convert the regular Canadian prices to USD at the ECB rates for 2026-07-16 (1 EUR = 1.1467 USD and 1.6082 CAD, hence 1 CAD = 0.713033 USD) [2][3][4][5][6][15]. Because the target products have no independent measurements, comparisons are provisional and use their published functions and catalog performance claims.

**SG Oasis (96.25 USD)** [2] vs **EarFun Air Pro 4 (89.99 USD)** [8]: Among current true-wireless earbuds with adaptive ANC, transparency, multipoint, app-based 10-band EQ, wear detection, sub-50 ms low-latency mode, customizable controls, firmware support, six microphones, and at least seven hours per charge, Air Pro 4 is the cheapest confirmed equal-or-better option. Its catalog claims up to 50 dB ANC and 7.5 hours with ANC, both at least matching Oasis's 44 dB and seven hours; SoundGuys independently measured 8 hours 44 minutes of operation and average perceived-noise reduction of 75% [7][8]. Its plotted default response is near the site's preference curve but shows roughly 2.5 dB of bass excess, a 2 dB dip near 450 Hz, a 3 dB rise around 4–5 kHz, and a deep 8–10 kHz dip; the ANC plot indicates roughly 6–22 dB reduction at low frequencies, about 32 dB near 1.3 kHz, and approximately 18–35 dB above that [7]. CP = 89.99 ÷ 96.25 = 0.935.

**Ultimate V6 (71.30 USD)** [4]: Its required combination includes a secure sport fit, independent single-ear use, IPX5 protection, seven-hour earbud operation, and wireless case charging. CMF Buds Pro 2 does not provide wireless case charging [16], and no cheaper current product was found that definitively meets the complete set. Ultimate V6 is therefore the cheapest established equivalent and its CP is 1.0.

**SG Session (71.30 USD)** [3]: In addition to adaptive ANC, transparency, multipoint, 60 hours without ANC or 35 hours with ANC, and low-latency mode, it supports USB-C digital listening while retaining microphone and ANC functions, plus passive AUX listening [3]. The functionally closest current candidate, Edifier W800BT Pro, provides Bluetooth 5.4, ANC, multipoint, low latency, USB-C audio, a microphone, and app EQ, but its 45/26-hour battery claims are shorter, it lacks 3.5 mm AUX, and it does not state that microphone and ANC remain available over USB-C [9]. It is therefore not equivalent, and no cheaper current product with the complete set was found; SG Session's CP is 1.0.

**SG Mini (56.32 USD)** [5] vs **JLab GO Air Sport (30.00 USD)** [10]: GO Air Sport provides a smaller secure sport earhook, IP55 protection, independent left/right use, a call microphone, and at least 32 hours including its case, matching or exceeding Mini's small-ear hook, IPX4, independent use, microphone, and 20-hour total claim [5][10]. A third-party review measured more than nine hours per charge and graphed all three onboard EQ modes, but did not disclose sufficient fixture and test details for a controlled comparison [17]. In the plotted 20 Hz–10 kHz result, Balanced mode is near 92 dB in the bass and 86–88 dB from about 200 Hz to 1 kHz, dips to roughly 78 dB near 2.5 kHz, and rises near 92 dB at 9 kHz; Signature and Bass Boost raise low bass toward roughly 100 and 104 dB while mostly converging above 200 Hz [17]. Since Mini has no independent performance data, equivalence remains provisional rather than measured like-for-like. CP = 30.00 ÷ 56.32 = 0.533.

**SG Balade (64.17 USD)** [6]: Its published set combines 16 W output, 16-hour operation, IPX7 floatability, stereo pairing, bass boost, microphone, AUX input, USB-C audio, and microSD playback [6]. The in-stock Tribit StormBox Micro 2 at 59.99 USD provides stereo pairing and IP67, but its 10 W output and 12-hour claim are lower, while its USB-C port is specified for charging and phone power-bank use rather than audio; AUX, microSD playback, a microphone, and floatability are not listed [11]. It is not equivalent, and no cheaper current speaker with the complete set was found; SG Balade's CP is 1.0.

Weighted CP = (0.935 x 0.25) + (1.0 x 0.25) + (1.0 x 0.25) + (0.533 x 0.10) + (1.0 x 0.15) = 0.937, which rounds to 0.9. SG Oasis, SG Session, and Ultimate V6 each receive 0.25 because they are the ANC earbud flagship, the sole over-ear model, and the advertised best-seller respectively; SG Balade receives 0.15 as the sole speaker, and SG Mini receives 0.10 as the secondary sport-earbud model.

## Reliability & Support

$$ \Large \text{0.4} $$

Sound's Good provides a one-year warranty on new wireless earbuds, headphones, and speakers, and six months on refurbished units [12]. The warranty page states that covered functional defects are handled without repair charges and that the company supplies a prepaid return label [12]. Manufacturer-direct troubleshooting and manuals are available through the Help Center [13]. The published delivery scope lists Canadian regions, and the company's warehouses and retail network are in Canada [1][14]. No statistical failure-rate, RMA, or MTBF data is published. The short warranty is the documented limitation; absent failure-rate data is treated as unknown rather than as evidence of poor durability. Reliability & Support is therefore 0.4.

## Rationality of Design Philosophy

$$ \Large \text{0.6} $$

The range concentrates spending on functions with direct user value—ANC, multipoint connection, app-adjustable EQ, battery life, and water resistance—and avoids expensive audiophile constructions or claims of inaudible mechanisms [2][3][4][5][6]. Using mature, mass-market technology is a rational way to contain cost, while combining those functions in portable products is appropriate to their use. Development remains incremental: the published material shows no measurement-led tuning program or new engineering approach, and it does not establish whether newer products improve measured fidelity over earlier ones. That unknown progression is not itself penalized, but the conservative implementation limits the assessment. On balance, the function-focused, cost-conscious direction supports a Rationality of Design Philosophy score of 0.6.

## Advice

Buyers should treat the published ANC maxima and nominal frequency ranges as catalog claims rather than verified performance. The products combine practical mainstream functions without costly audiophile excess, but the lack of independent frequency-response, distortion, and noise measurements prevents a fidelity-based recommendation. The one-year warranty and Canada-focused sales and delivery scope should also be considered before purchase.
