## Overview

Khadas is a Shenzhen-based electronics manufacturer founded in 2016. It entered audio with the Tone Board in 2018, but that historical model is no longer treated as part of the current company evaluation. As of this review, the company's own homepage presents two current Music Series products with equal prominence: the Tone2 Pro desktop USB DAC/headphone amplifier and the Tea Pro portable Bluetooth/USB DAC/headphone amplifier [1].

## Scientific Validity

$$ \Large \text{0.9} $$

Audio Science Review measured the Tone2 Pro at 3.992 Vrms and 0.000133% THD+N on its balanced line output, equivalent to about 117.5 dB SINAD; the unbalanced output measured 1.995 Vrms and 0.000211%, or about 113.5 dB SINAD [5]. The same test found excellent intermodulation performance. Its 48 kHz wide-band THD+N-versus-frequency graph also exposes a reconstruction-filter weakness at high frequencies, although the reviewer notes that the measurement bandwidth extends well beyond the audible band and the issue largely disappears at 192 kHz. Headphone testing reached 52 mW into 300 ohms and 200 mW into 33 ohms from the balanced output, while the unbalanced output was limited to 10 mW into 33 ohms [5].

For Tea Pro, Khadas specifies 0.000126% THD+N, 122 dB SNR and 121 dB dynamic range from the 4.4 mm output, and 0.000158%, 120 dB and 119 dB respectively from the 3.5 mm output, all at 1 kHz with no load [3][4]. It also specifies 165 mW into 33 ohms balanced and 120 mW into 33 ohms unbalanced, with output-power distortion below -90 dB [4]. These figures are manufacturer data and remain less conclusive than independent testing. Taken together, the independently verified Tone2 Pro and conservatively treated Tea Pro specifications support an excellent current-line assessment without relying on discontinued models.

## Technology Level

$$ \Large \text{0.6} $$

Khadas describes its Music Series as hardware it creates and supports, and the current products show clear design ownership in their compact packaging and system integration [6]. Tone2 Pro uses the company's patented CN211265767U Balanced RCA connector, a compact three-contact connection that remains backward-compatible with ordinary RCA plugs [7]. Tea Pro integrates USB and Bluetooth reception, dual headphone outputs, a display, battery power and magnetic phone attachment in a 96 g enclosure [3].

The underlying signal path nevertheless relies on widely available ESS, XMOS, Qualcomm and Texas Instruments parts [2][3][4]. Balanced RCA is deliberately available to third parties under Khadas's registration and compliance terms, and magnetic attachment is readily reproducible, so neither creates a durable technical barrier. The patent and genuine in-house integration raise the assessment above a purely standard implementation, while the mature components and ease of competitive catch-up keep it from a higher score.

## Cost-Performance

$$ \Large \text{1.0} $$

The company evaluation uses the two current audio products shown with equal prominence on Khadas's homepage [1], so each receives a weight of 0.5. Temporary sale prices are excluded.

**Tone2 Pro — 159 USD | Weight: 0.5.** FiiO K11 is a cheaper 142.99 USD market candidate [9][10] and offers much greater headphone power, 4.4 mm and 6.35 mm headphone outputs, and RCA line output. Its 4.4 mm connector is headphone-only, however, whereas Tone2 Pro provides balanced line output through Balanced RCA as well as balanced headphone output [2][9]. K11 therefore does not match the full user-facing output set and is not a valid equivalent comparator. No cheaper candidate with equal-or-better functions and output performance was confirmed, so Tone2 Pro's CP is 1.0.

**Tea Pro — 199 USD | Weight: 0.5.** HiBy W4 is a cheaper 99 USD candidate with Bluetooth support for SBC, AAC, aptX, aptX Adaptive, aptX HD and LDAC, PCM 768 kHz/DSD512 support, 3.5 mm and 4.4 mm outputs, a display, and up to 475 mW output [11]. It specifies only 6.3 hours from 3.5 mm and 5 hours from 4.4 mm, compared with Tea Pro's specified 11 hours, and its MagSafe-compatible magnetic backplate is an add-on rather than an integrated feature [3][11]. Because those are material portable-use disadvantages, W4 is not functionally equivalent. No cheaper candidate matched Tea Pro's complete function set, so Tea Pro's CP is 1.0.

The equally weighted company CP is therefore (1.0 × 0.5) + (1.0 × 0.5) = 1.0.

## Reliability & Support

$$ \Large \text{0.4} $$

Khadas's published warranty table gives the Tone and Tea categories 12 months of coverage [8]. The same policy documents initial remote troubleshooting, return shipment for testing, paid service outside warranty, and coverage of inspection, parts, labor and courier costs when Khadas confirms a product-quality defect. Full return, exchange and refund handling applies to purchases from the official store; purchases from authorized distributors depend on the distributor for those remedies [8].

The one-year term is shorter than the common two-year baseline. Khadas provides direct online support, documentation and firmware resources, but publishes neither a worldwide service-center network nor a parts-supply period or typical out-of-warranty repair prices. No statistical RMA, MTBF or failure-rate data was found. These limitations justify the below-average score; unverified forum anecdotes are not used as reliability evidence.

## Rationality of Design Philosophy

$$ \Large \text{0.8} $$

The current products direct most of their engineering toward measurable performance and practical integration: compact solid-state conversion, useful balanced and unbalanced outputs, battery operation, Bluetooth/USB input, a display and phone attachment [2][3][4]. The company publishes detailed THD+N, SNR, dynamic-range and load-specific output figures, and uses mass-produced modern components instead of tubes, R2R conversion or excessive mechanical construction. Balanced RCA and the magnetic portable format also address identifiable connection and carrying problems.

The marketing is not consistently scientific, however. Tone2 Pro's official page says MQA restores an original master and delivers sound as the artist intended, while Tea Pro is promoted as rivaling a professional studio [2][3]. No controlled audibility evidence is provided for either claim, and the MQA assertion goes beyond the measured performance data. This prevents the otherwise measurement-led and function-focused design direction from receiving a higher score.

## Advice

Tone2 Pro is the better-established choice for buyers who need both compact balanced line output and a headphone amplifier: its line performance is independently verified, but demanding headphones should use the balanced output because the unbalanced amplifier is weak. Tea Pro is the more versatile mobile option and has strong manufacturer specifications, but independent measurements are still needed. In both cases, weigh the distinctive connection and mounting functions against the one-year warranty and the absence of published global repair infrastructure. Buyers who do not need balanced line output or 11-hour battery life can spend less on FiiO K11 or HiBy W4 respectively.
