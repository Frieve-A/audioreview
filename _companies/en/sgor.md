## Overview

SGOR is a small Chinese in-ear monitor brand distributed globally through Linsoul Audio. Three current IEM models address the main driver configurations in the 19.99-38 USD no-microphone wired-IEM band: Venus (single dynamic driver, 19.99 USD), Adonis (1DD+1BA hybrid, 20 USD), and Luna (13.5mm planar magnetic, 38 USD). No official company website is operational as of the review date.

## Scientific Validity

$$ \Large \text{0.5} $$

**SGOR Venus (19.99 USD):** Manufacturer specifies a 7Hz–45kHz frequency range (±dB not stated) and 110 dB sensitivity [1]. A low-confidence personal blog REW graph [4] shows a V-shaped response with a concave midrange; because it is not treated as a credible primary measurement source and does not provide extractable numeric deviation values, it is used only as qualitative context. No THD, S/N ratio, SINAD, or IMD figures are available from any accessible source.

**SGOR Adonis (20 USD):** Manufacturer specifies an 8Hz–45kHz frequency range and 118 dB/Vrms sensitivity [3]. The accessible product page provides no numeric frequency-response graph or distortion data. No THD, S/N ratio, or distortion figures are available.

**SGOR Luna (38 USD):** Manufacturer specifies a 7Hz–40kHz frequency range and 88 dB/Vrms sensitivity [2]. The official product page provides no numeric FR deviation, THD, S/N ratio, or distortion figures.

Across all three products, THD, S/N ratio, SINAD, IMD, and crosstalk figures are absent from both manufacturer publications and accessible credible third-party measurement sources. Because available evidence is limited to manufacturer specifications plus low-confidence qualitative frequency-response context for Venus, all three models are treated as unable to be objectively scored beyond the neutral 0.5. Weighted across the three products, the company-level score remains 0.5.

## Technology Level

$$ \Large \text{0.2} $$

All three products use driver configurations that are standard and widely deployed across the Chinese IEM market: dual magnetic-circuit dynamic drivers, 1DD+1BA hybrid topology, and a 13.5mm planar magnetic driver with 7+7 N52 magnet array [1][2][3]. No patents have been identified in available databases for any SGOR technology. "Spatial Harmonic Resonance" is a marketing label for a tuned acoustic vent cavity, a routine IEM design practice without proprietary dimension. "Sub-nanometer diaphragm" describes a photolithographic thin-film planar membrane in non-technical language [2]. The brand's characteristics are consistent with OEM/ODM sourcing; no in-house engineering team or R&D publications have been identified. The Adonis reads as a lineup-extension product rather than a technology-driven development. The sole digital integration point is an optional USB-C DSP cable for the Luna, an approach widely offered by competing brands [2].

## Reliability & Support

$$ \Large \text{0.4} $$

All SGOR IEMs carry a 1-year warranty on earphone units and a 3-month warranty on cables [1][2][3], which is limited coverage for a product line without a direct manufacturer service channel. IEM construction is passive and structurally simple — no active electronics in standard wired models, few mechanical failure points, and user-replaceable detachable cables — supporting reliability through design simplicity. Post-sale support routes entirely through Linsoul Audio as sole authorized distributor; no SGOR manufacturer support infrastructure, direct repair channel, or spare parts program has been identified. Replacement or refund is handled through Linsoul's standard return process. No statistical failure rate data is publicly available, and the brand's limited market presence provides no established long-term reliability record.

## Rationality of Design Philosophy

$$ \Large \text{0.4} $$

SGOR's approach covers three dominant IEM driver categories at 19.99-38 USD no-microphone wired prices without publishing measurement data or adopting a measurement-first orientation [1][2][3]. Performance claims are stated qualitatively rather than through objective metrics. Marketing terminology — "Spatial Harmonic Resonance," "sub-nanometer diaphragm," "400+ days of development" — describes standard industry practices in unverifiable or non-technical language without scientific substantiation [2]. Model progression from Venus to Adonis to Luna reflects driver-type diversification rather than demonstrable improvement in measured performance over predecessor designs; no comparative measurement data across model generations has been published. The USB-C DSP variant for the Luna reflects pragmatic awareness of digital integration possibilities but involves no original development [2]. The approach is conservative — following established Chi-Fi product patterns without contributing novel technical direction. No occult audio claims are present, and all products provide functional audio value.

## Advice

The three representative SGOR products span 19.99-38 USD no-microphone wired IEMs, but the absence of credible third-party measurement data for any product makes objective performance verification impossible at the time of this review. For Venus and Adonis, compare against alternatives with published third-party measurements before buying. Luna uses a 13.5mm planar driver, but its 88 dB/Vrms sensitivity requires substantially more source voltage for the same loudness than 105 dB-class dynamic-driver IEMs [2]. Buyers using low-output portable sources should check volume headroom before purchase. Buyers who prioritize verified measured performance should seek alternatives with published third-party measurement coverage before purchasing any SGOR product. The CCA CRA at 14 USD provides equivalent core functionality at a lower price across all three SGOR product prices [5].
